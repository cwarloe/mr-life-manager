# Monthly Scorecard

One page for deciding what to do next. This is not an analytics warehouse.

## Definitions

| Measure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Visitors | Human visits reported by the selected privacy-first analytics service |
| Task completions | Visits to the four explicit post-completion pages; a directional measure, not proof every step was performed |
| Subscribers | New MailerLite subscribers, excluding tests |
| Replies | Human replies to the three-email sequences |
| Founding reservations | People who explicitly said they would seriously consider *First Place* at $39; interest, not revenue |
| Unsubscribes/complaints | Deliverability and trust warning signs |
| Sales | Completed paid transactions, once a paid offer exists |
| Support hours | Inbox, refunds, delivery help and other customer administration |

Do not use open rate for product decisions. Record the four MailerLite route
groups separately only when diagnosing a door; the six measures above are the
operating dashboard.

## Decision rules

- **No visitors:** improve distribution; do not build another product.
- **Visitors, no task completions:** repair the task or traffic match before asking for more email addresses.
- **Completions, no subscribers:** repair the post-win promise before adding another free guide.
- **Visitors, no subscribers or replies:** test the page promise and traffic
  match before changing the product.
- **Subscribers, no replies:** keep the sequence short; use interviews and
  direct conversations for learning.
- **Delivery, complaint or unsubscribe problem:** stop distribution and repair
  operations before adding traffic.
- **Repeated reader problem:** promote it into the research inventory after the
  second independent occurrence.
- **Administration above 20% of available business time for two months:** remove
  complexity before adding revenue channels.
- **Paid demand:** build the smallest paid product that tests the named problem;
  do not jump to a book, course or membership.
- **Reservations without purchases:** the reservation was curiosity, not demand;
  do not count it as validation.
