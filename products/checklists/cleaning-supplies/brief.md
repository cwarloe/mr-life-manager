# Product Brief — Cleaning Supply Starter List

**Status:** Draft 1, 2026-09-08. Not reviewed, not designed, not price-checked.
**Tier:** Rung 0 — free ([product-ladder.md](../../../docs/business/product-ladder.md))
**Format:** printable, black-and-white, US Letter
**Files:** [`cleaning-supply-starter-list.md`](cleaning-supply-starter-list.md) (source text) ·
[`cleaning-supply-starter-list.html`](cleaning-supply-starter-list.html) (designed, printable)

**Design:** buy / tools / laundry as priced tables with right-aligned tabular
figures, then **Skip** gets an amber tag and its own visual weight — it's the
credibility move, so it shouldn't read as an afterthought. The storage section
is three room cards, because "keep a set in each room" is spatial advice and
reads better as space than as a sentence.

---

### 1. Who is this for?

Anyone setting up a home for the first time, and anyone who has been buying
cleaning products by guesswork for a decade. Pairs directly with the
[First Apartment Checklist](../first-apartment/), which references supplies but
doesn't cost or explain them.

### 2. What problem does it solve?

From [customer-problems.md](../../../docs/research/customer-problems.md):
"Doesn't know what supplies to buy; over-buys specialty products, under-buys
basics" and "doesn't know what actually cleans what (and which combinations are
dangerous)."

### 3. What outcome should it produce?

Rung 1 — less decision fatigue. Target quote:

> "I stopped buying things I didn't need. Turns out it's about nine products."

### 4. Domains covered

Home (cleaning) — narrow by design. It's a single-purpose reference.

### 5. Principles it serves

§5 make the right action easy (the storage section is the whole point) ·
§9 simple enough to keep · §10 teach the why

### 6. Ladder position

Rung 0, beside the other two free assets. Leads to room-by-room guides ($9) and
*First Place* ($49).

### 7. Content drawn on

Overlaps deliberately with the First Apartment Checklist's cleaning section —
that one **lists**, this one **explains and costs.** When `content/` is
populated, both should draw from one source rather than duplicating.

### 8. Price, and why

**$0.** Cheap to make, highly shareable, and it earns trust fast by telling
people what *not* to buy — which is the fastest credibility move available in a
category full of people selling things.

---

## Design decisions

**Leads with what to skip.** Telling someone not to spend money is the quickest
way to be believed. Most competing content in this category is affiliate-driven
and therefore structurally unable to say "you don't need this."

**Prices are ranges and undated.** They'll drift; ranges age gracefully.

**The storage section is the actual content.** Everyone else's supply list stops
at the list. "Keep a set in each room, and buy the second bottle" is the part
that changes behavior — §5, made concrete.

**The $15 version is included on purpose.** A free asset that assumes $150 of
disposable income excludes part of the audience. It also happens to be true.

**Chemical safety is stated plainly and briefly.** Bleach + ammonia and bleach +
vinegar are genuinely dangerous and widely unknown. Two sentences, no lecture.
