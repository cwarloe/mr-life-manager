Cleaning Supply Starter List

What to actually buy, what to skip, and roughly what it costs.

The cleaning aisle is designed to make you think you need forty products. You need about nine. Most specialty cleaners are the same few ingredients with a different label and a different smell.

Here's the honest list.

Skip

Don't buy these — at least not yet

This is the part the cleaning aisle would rather you didn't read.

  • A separate cleaner for every roomKitchen, living room, and bedroom surfaces are the same surfaces. One all-purpose does it.
  • Wood-, granite-, and stainless-specific spraysBuy these only when you actually have that surface and the general one isn't working.
  • Disinfecting wipes as your main cleanerConvenient, expensive, wasteful, and worse at actual cleaning. Fine to keep a tub for quick jobs.
  • Scented everythingYou're paying for fragrance — and it masks the smell of a surface that isn't clean.
  • Premium "natural" brandsOften the same actives at triple the price. Vinegar and baking soda are the actual budget natural option.
  • Steam mops, robot vacuums, specialty gadgetsMaybe later. Not in month one.
  • Bulk anything, at firstBuy small until you know what you'll use. Bulk cleaner you hate is worse than no cleaner.
⚠ One safety rule

Don't mix cleaning products. Some combinations — bleach with anything, especially — make fumes that will genuinely hurt you.

One product at a time. Rinse in between. If a surface needs two treatments, rinse with water first. Open a window when you're using anything strong.

That's the whole rule. You don't need to know which combinations are the bad ones if you never mix any of them.

Storage

Where to keep it

Not all in one closet. This is the part that changes whether cleaning actually happens.

Bathroom

Bathroom cleaner, toilet cleaner, brush, a couple of cloths

Kitchen

All-purpose, dish soap, cloths, sponges

Central closet

Vacuum, mop, broom, backups, laundry supplies

Why the duplicate is worth it Buying a second cheap bottle of bathroom cleaner feels wasteful and isn't. The bathroom gets wiped when the supplies are already in the bathroom. Every trip down the hall is a reason not to bother — and reasons not to bother are what you're actually fighting.
Later

The upgrade order

Once the basics are working, in this order:

  1. More microfiber cloths. Genuinely, more. You'll never regret it.
  2. A squeegee for the shower — thirty seconds after showering prevents most soap scum, which is the worst job in the bathroom.
  3. A handheld vacuum for quick messes.
  4. An extendable duster for ceiling fans and high corners.
  5. A better vacuum, once you know what you actually need.

If money is tight

You can clean an entire apartment with four things, for about fifteen dollars:

Dish soapBaking sodaWhite vinegarMicrofiber cloths

That covers dishes, counters, most surfaces, glass, scrubbing, and descaling. Add a toilet brush and a plunger and you're genuinely fine. Everything else on this page is convenience.

Nobody teaches this, so most people learn it by buying $200 of cleaning products and using four of them.
