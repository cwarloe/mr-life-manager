# The First Apartment Checklist

**Everything to do, buy, and find out — in the order you'll need it.**

Most first-apartment lists are shopping lists. This one is a sequence, because
the order matters more than the list. Do these roughly in order and you won't
spend your first month discovering things you should have handled in week one.

Nobody is born knowing this. It just doesn't get taught.

---

## ☐ PART 1 — Before you sign

Fifteen minutes here saves months later.

**Read the lease. All of it.** Yes, really. Find and write down:

- ☐ Rent due date, and the late fee after it
- ☐ Length of the lease, and what happens at the end (does it auto-renew?)
- ☐ **Notice required to move out** — usually 30 or 60 days. Miss it and you owe
  another month.
- ☐ What you pay for vs. what's included (water, trash, heat, electric, internet)
- ☐ Rules on guests, pets, subletting, painting, and mounting things on walls
- ☐ How to submit a repair request, and who to call after hours
- ☐ Deposit amount, and the conditions for getting it back

**Ask before you sign:**

- ☐ Which utilities do I set up, and with which companies?
- ☐ Is renter's insurance required? (Get it either way — see Part 3.)
- ☐ What's the parking situation, and is it extra?
- ☐ Which appliances are included, and who repairs them?
- ☐ Has anything in this unit been recently repaired or had a problem?

> **Why it matters:** almost every expensive surprise in a first apartment is
> written somewhere in the lease. It's not that people agree to bad terms — it's
> that they never read them, so they don't know what they agreed to.

## ☐ PART 2 — Before you move a single box

### Set up utilities — do this at least a week ahead

- ☐ Electric ☐ Gas ☐ Water/sewer (often landlord) ☐ Trash (often landlord)
- ☐ Internet — **book the install appointment now.** It's frequently 1–2 weeks out.
- ☐ Put everything on **autopay** the day you set it up. This is the single
  cheapest thing you will ever do to protect your credit.

### Change your address

- ☐ USPS mail forwarding ☐ Bank and credit cards ☐ Employer/payroll
- ☐ Driver's license and vehicle registration ☐ Voter registration
- ☐ Insurance (auto, health) ☐ Phone ☐ Subscriptions that ship physical things
- ☐ Doctor, dentist, pharmacy ☐ School or student records

## ☐ PART 3 — Move-in day, before you unpack

**This is the most valuable half hour in this entire document.** Do it before a
single box comes in, while the place is empty.

### Document the condition of everything

- ☐ **Photograph and video every room**, empty, with the date visible if you can
- ☐ Get close-ups of every existing flaw: scuffs, stains, chips, holes,
  scratched floors, dinged counters, torn screens, cracked tiles
- ☐ Check and photograph: inside the fridge and oven, under every sink, the
  inside of closets, window frames, and the corners of bathrooms
- ☐ Fill out the move-in condition form if you were given one. **If you weren't
  given one, email your landlord a list of what you found anyway**, and keep
  that email.
- ☐ Test every outlet, every light switch, every faucet (hot *and* cold), every
  window and lock, and the toilet
- ☐ Run the shower for five minutes — check drainage and how long hot water lasts

> **Why it matters:** at move-out, the default assumption is that damage is
> yours. Photos with a date are how you prove it wasn't. This routinely decides
> whether people get several hundred dollars back. It takes thirty minutes and
> it's the highest-paid half hour of your year.

### Find these five things and write down where they are

- ☐ **Water shutoff valve** — for the whole unit, and under each sink and toilet
- ☐ **Breaker panel** — and which switch controls what
- ☐ **Water heater**, and whether it's yours or shared
- ☐ **Smoke and CO detectors** — where they are, and test each one now
- ☐ **Fire extinguisher** — where the nearest one is

> **Why it matters:** you will not learn where the water shutoff is during a
> leak. You'll learn it after.

### Get renter's insurance

- ☐ It's cheap — a few dollars a month. Get it before you move in.
- ☐ **Your landlord's insurance covers the building, not your things.** If
  there's a fire, a burst pipe, or a break-in, this is what replaces your stuff.
- ☐ Photograph your valuables and keep the receipts somewhere that isn't the
  apartment.

## ☐ PART 4 — The first shopping trip

Grouped by where things go. **You do not need all of this on day one** — the
starred items ★ are what you'll miss in the first 24 hours.

### Cleaning (buy these first — you'll want them before you unpack)

- ☐ ★ Paper towels ☐ ★ Trash bags (check the can size) ☐ ★ Dish soap
- ☐ All-purpose cleaner ☐ Bathroom cleaner ☐ Glass cleaner
- ☐ Sponges and a few microfiber cloths ☐ Toilet brush ☐ **★ Plunger**
- ☐ Broom and dustpan ☐ Vacuum or a good sweeper ☐ Mop and bucket
- ☐ Rubber gloves ☐ Laundry detergent ☐ Dryer sheets or wool balls

> Keep a small set of cleaning supplies **in each room where you use them.**
> A second $4 bottle of bathroom cleaner buys a behavior change — the bathroom
> gets wiped when the supplies are already in the bathroom.

### Kitchen

- ☐ ★ Dish soap, sponge, dish towels ☐ ★ Can opener ☐ ★ One good knife
- ☐ Cutting board ☐ Skillet ☐ Saucepan with a lid ☐ Baking sheet
- ☐ Mixing bowl ☐ Spatula, wooden spoon, tongs ☐ Measuring cups and spoons
- ☐ Colander ☐ Plates, bowls, cups, silverware ☐ Food storage containers
- ☐ Aluminum foil, plastic wrap, storage bags ☐ Oven mitt ☐ Dish rack

### Bathroom

- ☐ ★ Toilet paper ☐ ★ Hand soap ☐ ★ Shower curtain, liner, and rings
- ☐ Bath towels, hand towels, washcloths ☐ Bath mat ☐ Trash can
- ☐ Toilet brush and holder ☐ Basic first aid kit ☐ Any medication you take

### Bedroom

- ☐ ★ Sheets (two sets, so laundry day isn't a crisis) ☐ ★ Pillows
- ☐ Comforter or duvet ☐ Mattress protector ☐ Hangers ☐ Laundry hamper
- ☐ Curtains or blinds if the windows are bare ☐ Alarm clock

### The stuff nobody puts on the list

- ☐ ★ **Light bulbs** (check what fits before you buy) ☐ ★ Batteries
- ☐ **Toolkit:** hammer, screwdriver with multiple bits, tape measure, pliers,
  level, utility knife, adjustable wrench
- ☐ Command strips and picture-hanging hardware ☐ Extension cords, power strips
- ☐ Flashlight ☐ Duct tape ☐ Scissors ☐ Step stool
- ☐ Fire extinguisher (kitchen-rated) ☐ Smoke detector batteries
- ☐ Air filters for the HVAC — **check the size on the existing one**
- ☐ Drain hair catcher (cheaper than a plumber, every single time)

## ☐ PART 5 — The first week

- ☐ **Make your bed properly on night one.** Everything else can wait; sleeping
  well makes the rest of the week possible.
- ☐ Set up the kitchen before anything else. A working kitchen makes the whole
  apartment feel livable, and it's what stops the delivery spending.
- ☐ **Give things a home as you unpack.** Unpacking is the only time you'll ever
  decide where things live from scratch — don't waste it by piling boxes.
- ☐ Put daily-use items at waist-to-eye height. Rarely-used things go high or low.
- ☐ Note the trash and recycling schedule, and where the bins go
- ☐ Find the nearest grocery store, pharmacy, urgent care, and laundromat
- ☐ Learn where mail is delivered and how packages are handled
- ☐ Meet at least one neighbor — worth more than it sounds when you're locked out
- ☐ Buy one week's groceries for **three or four meals you already know how to
  make.** Ambitious grocery shopping in week one becomes food waste in week two.
- ☐ Test the smoke detectors ☐ Note the HVAC filter size and set a reminder

## ☐ PART 6 — The first month

### Set up the systems

- ☐ **Autopay everything** — rent if possible, utilities, phone, insurance
- ☐ Put these on a calendar now, with reminders, for the whole year:
  - Rent (monthly) · HVAC filter (every 1–3 months) · Smoke detector batteries
    (yearly, pick a memorable date) · Renter's insurance renewal · Lease
    end date **and the notice deadline before it**
- ☐ Pick a **home for incoming mail** and process it there, standing up, near a
  recycling bin. A stack of mail is a stack of unmade decisions with deadlines.
- ☐ Pick a **home for important documents** — lease, insurance, pay stubs,
  anything with an account number
- ☐ Pick a **home for keys** by the door, and use it every time

### Set the rhythm

- ☐ **A ten-minute reset before bed.** Dishes in, counters wiped, things put
  back. This single habit is the difference between a place that stays livable
  and one that spirals.
- ☐ **One weekly reset**, 45–90 minutes: laundry, bathroom, floors, fridge clear-out.
  Same day each week so you stop deciding.
- ☐ Laundry on a schedule, not when you run out of clothes

### If you have roommates

- ☐ **Agree out loud who does what**, and write it down. Not "we'll both do the
  kitchen" — "you own dishes, I own trash and floors."
- ☐ Decide how shared bills are split and *who actually pays the company*
- ☐ Agree on guests, quiet hours, and shared groceries **before** it's a problem
- ☐ Name the invisible work too: noticing supplies are low, taking out recycling,
  scheduling repairs. It's real work and it's what people end up resenting.

## The five things that cost people the most money

1. **Not documenting move-in condition.** Hundreds of dollars, every time.
2. **Missing the move-out notice deadline.** An extra month's rent.
3. **Not having renter's insurance.** One burst pipe replaces everything you own.
4. **Aspirational grocery shopping.** Produce rots; delivery fills the gap.
5. **Ignoring small repairs.** Report them in writing, early. A slow drip becomes
   a ceiling.

---

## If you only do five things

1. Photograph everything before you unpack.
2. Put every bill on autopay.
3. Find the water shutoff and the breaker panel.
4. Give things a home while you're unpacking.
5. Reset the kitchen for ten minutes before bed.

Everything else on this list is easier once those five are true.
