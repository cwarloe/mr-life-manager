The First Apartment Checklist

Everything to do, buy, and find out — in the order you'll need it.

Most first-apartment lists are shopping lists. This one is a sequence, because the order matters more than the list. Work through it roughly in order and you won't spend your first month discovering things you should have handled in week one.

Nobody is born knowing this. It just doesn't get taught.

Part 1

Before you sign

15 minutes

Fifteen minutes here saves months later.

Read the lease — all of it

Yes, really. Find these and write them down:

  • Rent due date, and the late fee after it
  • Length of the lease, and what happens at the end — does it auto-renew?
  • Notice required to move out — usually 30 or 60 days. Miss it and you owe another month.
  • What you pay for vs. what's included: water, trash, heat, electric, internet
  • Rules on guests, pets, subletting, painting, and mounting things on walls
  • How to submit a repair request, and who to call after hours
  • Deposit amount, and the conditions for getting it back

Ask before you sign

  • Which utilities do I set up, and with which companies?
  • Is renter's insurance required? (Get it either way — see Part 3.)
  • What's the parking situation, and is it extra?
  • Which appliances are included, and who repairs them?
  • Has anything in this unit recently been repaired or had a problem?
Why it matters Almost every expensive surprise in a first apartment is written somewhere in the lease. It's not that people agree to bad terms — it's that they never read them, so they don't know what they agreed to.
Part 2

Before you move a single box

A week ahead

Set up utilities

  • Electric
  • Gas
  • Water & sewer (often landlord)
  • Trash (often landlord)
  • Internet — book the install now; it's often 1–2 weeks out
  • Put it all on autopay the day you set it up
Why it matters Autopay is the cheapest thing you will ever do to protect your credit. One missed bill you never even saw can follow you for years.

Change your address

  • USPS mail forwarding
  • Bank and credit cards
  • Employer / payroll
  • Driver's license
  • Vehicle registration
  • Voter registration
  • Auto and health insurance
  • Phone
  • Subscriptions that ship things
  • Doctor, dentist, pharmacy
  • School or student records
Part 3

Move-in day, before you unpack

30 minutes

The most valuable half hour in this document. Do it before a single box comes in, while the place is still empty.

Document the condition of everything

  • Photograph and video every room, empty, with the date visible if you can
  • Close-ups of every existing flaw: scuffs, stains, chips, holes, scratched floors, dinged counters, torn screens, cracked tiles
  • Check and photograph inside the fridge and oven, under every sink, inside closets, window frames, and bathroom corners
  • Fill out the move-in condition form if you got one. If you didn't, email your landlord a list of what you found anyway — and keep that email.
  • Test every outlet, light switch, faucet (hot and cold), window, lock, and the toilet
  • Run the shower five minutes — check drainage, and how long the hot water lasts
Why it matters At move-out, the default assumption is that the damage is yours. Dated photos are how you prove it wasn't. This routinely decides whether people get several hundred dollars back — which makes it the highest-paid half hour of your year.

Find these five things and write down where they are

  • Water shutoff valve — for the whole unit, and under each sink and toilet
  • Breaker panel — and which switch controls what
  • Water heater — and whether it's yours or shared
  • Smoke and CO detectors — where they are, and test each one now
  • Fire extinguisher — where the nearest one is
Why it matters You will not learn where the water shutoff is during a leak. You'll learn it after.

Get renter's insurance

  • It's cheap — a few dollars a month. Get it before you move in.
  • Your landlord's insurance covers the building, not your things. If there's a fire, a burst pipe, or a break-in, this is what replaces your stuff.
  • Photograph your valuables and keep the receipts somewhere that isn't the apartment
Part 4

The first shopping trip

Day one

Grouped by where things go. You don't need all of it on day one — the ★ items are what you'll miss first.

Cleaning — buy these before you unpack

  • Paper towels
  • Trash bags (check the can size)
  • Dish soap
  • Plunger
  • All-purpose cleaner
  • Bathroom cleaner
  • Sponges and microfiber cloths
  • Toilet brush
  • Broom and dustpan
  • Vacuum or a good sweeper
  • Mop and bucket
  • Rubber gloves
  • Laundry detergent
  • Dryer sheets or wool balls
Where to keep it Keep a small set of supplies in each room where you use them. A second cheap bottle of bathroom cleaner buys a behavior change — the bathroom gets wiped when the supplies are already in the bathroom.

Part 5

The first week

Days 1–7
  • Make your bed properly on night one. Everything else can wait; sleeping well is what makes the rest of the week possible.
  • Set up the kitchen before anything else — a working kitchen makes the place feel livable, and it's what stops the delivery spending
  • Give things a home as you unpack. Unpacking is the only time you'll decide where things live from scratch. Don't waste it by stacking boxes.
  • Put daily-use items at waist-to-eye height; rarely-used things go high or low
  • Note the trash and recycling schedule, and where the bins go
  • Find the nearest grocery store, pharmacy, urgent care, and laundromat
  • Learn where mail is delivered and how packages are handled
  • Meet at least one neighbor — worth more than it sounds when you're locked out
  • Buy one week of groceries for three or four meals you already know how to make
  • Test the smoke detectors; note the air filter size and set a reminder
Why it matters Ambitious grocery shopping in week one becomes food waste in week two. Cook what you already know until the kitchen works.
Part 6

The first month

Weeks 2–4

Set up the systems

  • Autopay everything — rent if you can, utilities, phone, insurance
  • Put these on a calendar for the whole year, with reminders: rent (monthly) · air filter (every 1–3 months) · smoke detector batteries (yearly) · insurance renewal · lease end date, and the notice deadline before it
  • Pick a home for incoming mail — process it standing up, near a recycling bin
  • Pick a home for important documents: lease, insurance, pay stubs, anything with an account number
  • Pick a home for keys by the door, and use it every time
Why it matters A stack of mail is a stack of unmade decisions with deadlines attached. The stack isn't the problem — the deferred decisions are.

Set the rhythm

  • A ten-minute reset before bed — dishes in, counters wiped, things put back
  • One weekly reset, 45–90 minutes: laundry, bathroom, floors, fridge clear-out. Same day each week, so you stop deciding.
  • Laundry on a schedule, not when you run out of clothes
Why it matters The nightly reset is the single habit that separates a place that stays livable from one that spirals. Skip it four days and you've got a project instead of ten minutes.

If you have roommates

  • Agree out loud who does what, and write it down. Not "we'll both do the kitchen" — "you own dishes, I own trash and floors."
  • Decide how shared bills are split, and who actually pays the company
  • Agree on guests, quiet hours, and shared groceries before it's a problem
  • Name the invisible work too: noticing supplies are low, taking out recycling, scheduling repairs. It's real work, and it's what people end up resenting.
Costly

The five things that cost people the most

  1. Not documenting move-in condition. Hundreds of dollars, every time.
  2. Missing the move-out notice deadline. An extra month's rent.
  3. No renter's insurance. One burst pipe replaces everything you own.
  4. Aspirational grocery shopping. Produce rots; delivery fills the gap.
  5. Ignoring small repairs. Report them in writing, early. A slow drip becomes a ceiling.

If you only do five things

Everything else on this list gets easier once these are true.

  1. Photograph everything before you unpack.
  2. Put every bill on autopay.
  3. Find the water shutoff and the breaker panel.
  4. Give things a home while you're unpacking.
  5. Reset the kitchen for ten minutes before bed.

That's it. Five things, and the first month stops happening to you.
