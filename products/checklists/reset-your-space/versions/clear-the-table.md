# I have to clear the table before we can eat.

Get your table back for meals, and give the things that keep covering it somewhere to go.

**Today's job:** Reset the dining table and every seat needed for an ordinary meal, including homes for recurring blockers. Name the seats before starting. This is a planned storage reset before a meal, rather than a quick clear-off while food waits. Leave the rest of the room outside the job.

**Have ready:** A trash bag, a surface-appropriate cloth and cleaning method, a carry box, and paper for labels and pending actions.

**Before starting:** Check that returning items and recurring blockers have accessible storage outside the eating places. Agree on shared changes and other people's belongings. Name a protected waiting place for review items and pending handoffs. Missing permission or storage is a real blocker; arrange it before spreading out the contents. No completion time is promised.

- [ ] **1. Make a small place to start.** Remove obvious trash and take dishes to the kitchen. Clear enough of the tabletop to sort a small batch. Keep the route around the table open; putting the pile on the floor creates another obstacle.

- [ ] **2. Sort by next destination in small batches.** Separate obvious trash, giveaways, frequent-use keepers, and occasional-use keepers. Keep borrowed items, unfamiliar records, and belongings needing an owner's decision in the protected review place. Label what needs asking, who will ask, and when; do not discard it to clear the table.

- [ ] **3. Put things with homes back in them.** Return the tools, clothes, bags, and other belongings that already have a place. Use the box to carry a batch, then empty it into those homes. Do not leave the box as the table's new storage system.

- [ ] **4. Give recurring blockers usable homes.** Put everyday items near their use, such as keys by the exit or work supplies in the work area. Keep action papers in one labeled tray and schedule their review. Reading bills or completing every paper task is a separate session. Use the agreed storage, rather than emptying other rooms to find space.

- [ ] **5. Store occasional-use groups.** Put them in the checked storage away from the table and eating seats. Make contents visible or label their containers. If capacity is still missing, record the group, its named waiting place, the needed space, and its next action. A waiting place preserves access but is not a finished permanent home.

- [ ] **6. Wipe the table and free the seats.** Lift the items that genuinely belong on the table, wipe underneath, and put them back only if the meal still fits. Clear the seats and enough room to pull them out. The table's useful space includes the people around it.

- [ ] **7. Complete returns and record pending exits.** Empty the carry box into actual homes and take out the trash. Label pending giveaways and review items in their agreed waiting places; write the next action, owner, and exact time. Keep eating places and walkways clear. Record handoffs that have not happened as pending.

- [ ] **8. Test the meal setup and the new homes.** Set every place you named and sit down without moving a pile. Retrieve and return the recurring items to check that their homes work. Leave the carry box and staging space clear. If seats remain blocked or recurring items lack usable homes, record the blocker; the full storage reset is not finished.

**Your finished win:** Every selected eating place is usable and recurring blockers can be retrieved from and returned to their homes. Pending decisions, missing storage, and handoffs remain separate unfinished actions.

**Keep the result:** After using the table for another activity, return those materials to their homes. If the same object keeps landing here, make its home easier to use.
