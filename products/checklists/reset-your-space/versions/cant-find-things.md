# I put things away. Then I can't find them.

Make the household cupboard or hall closet a place you can find things, rather than the place things disappear.

**Today's job:** Write down the complete cupboard or closet section you will reset, including its shelves or boxes. Keep the session inside that boundary. The finish is identifiable contents and easy retrieval and return; finding every missing object is a separate result.

**Have ready:** Staging space for one shelf or box, a suitable cleaning cloth, a trash bag, a return box, paper for labels and pending actions, and existing containers.

**Before starting:** Check storage capacity and permission for shared changes. Leave private belongings for their owners. Name a protected review place for undecided items, with an owner and time to ask. Arrange missing access before emptying the section. There is no promised completion time.

- [ ] **1. List the things you keep looking for.** Use up to five real examples, such as tape, scissors, a charger, spare batteries, or light bulbs. Mark each found or still missing as you go. The list guides the test; five is not a rule about how much you should remember.

- [ ] **2. Open one selected shelf or box at a time.** Work on the clear staging surface. Separate obvious trash, giveaways, frequent-use items, and occasional-use items. Keep borrowed belongings, unfamiliar records, and decisions needing another person in the protected review place. Restore each shelf or box before opening the next one on your list.

- [ ] **3. Group items within the chosen section.** Keep cables together, bulbs together, or tape with wrapping supplies as they turn up. Put known misplaced items in the return box. Do not expand into nearby cupboards or rooms to complete a category. Record an outside search as a separate action for later.

- [ ] **4. Choose a home for each group.** Put a group near where it is used when that is practical. Keep daily-use groups easy to reach; occasional supplies can go farther back or higher up. Put items that belong elsewhere in their actual homes.

- [ ] **5. Make the contents recognizable.** Wipe exposed shelves, then place groups so you can identify them. Use clear containers, open trays, or simple labels on opaque boxes. Put the label on the side you see when opening the door. A neat stack of unidentified boxes has not solved the search.

- [ ] **6. Make putting things away easy too.** Leave room to remove and return a group without dismantling the shelf. Split unrelated contents into visible groups using existing containers. Show shared-storage users the arrangement. If items still do not fit accessibly, record the capacity gap instead of filling another unknown box.

- [ ] **7. Finish returns and record pending work.** Put return-box items in their actual homes and take out the trash. Label where giveaways or review items wait and write their next action, owner, and time. For every still-missing target, name the next search and when it will happen. Do not count a scheduled search or replacement purchase as finding the item.

- [ ] **8. Test retrieval and return.** Close the door, then retrieve and return each found target without opening unrelated boxes. Adjust confusing labels or awkward homes. Check every selected shelf or box is restored and the staging surface is clear. Record the two results separately: storage usable, and targets found versus still missing. If the storage test fails, record what blocks its finish.

**Your finished win:** The chosen storage section has identifiable contents and usable homes for its available items. Record each target as found or still missing; a usable cupboard does not mean every missing object has been found.

**Keep the result:** Put things back in the same named homes. If you share the storage, show the other people the arrangement so they can use it too.
