# I put things away. Then I can't find them.

Make the household cupboard or hall closet a place you can find things, rather than the place things disappear.

**Today's job:** Work on the cupboard or closet where household odds and ends collect. Start with the things you repeatedly lose there. This is not a house-wide search or an inventory of everything you own.

**Have ready:** A clear working surface, a cloth, a trash bag, paper for labels, and containers you already have. Leave other people's private belongings for them to decide about.

- [ ] **1. Write down five things you keep looking for.** Choose real examples, such as tape, scissors, a charger, spare batteries, or light bulbs. This list gives the reset a purpose: finding the things that interrupt your day.

- [ ] **2. Open one shelf or box at a time.** Use a clear surface to examine a manageable section. Put obvious trash in the bag. Separate things to give away, things used often, and things used occasionally. Restore that section before opening another.

- [ ] **3. Bring matching items together.** Gather each category from the cupboard or closet: cables together, spare bulbs together, tape and wrapping supplies together. Check the usual nearby hiding places for the five missing items, but do not empty other rooms. If something is still missing, note it as a separate next action.

- [ ] **4. Choose a home for each group.** Put a group near where it is used when that is practical. Keep daily-use groups easy to reach; occasional supplies can go farther back or higher up. Put items that belong elsewhere in their actual homes.

- [ ] **5. Make the contents recognizable.** Wipe exposed shelves, then place groups so you can identify them. Use clear containers, open trays, or simple labels on opaque boxes. Put the label on the side you see when opening the door. A neat stack of unidentified boxes has not solved the search.

- [ ] **6. Make putting things away easy too.** Leave enough room to remove and return a group without dismantling the shelf. If a box holds unrelated things, separate them into smaller groups. Use existing containers first; list any real storage gaps with quantities or measurements.

- [ ] **7. Finish the removals and missing-item decisions.** Take out the trash. Choose where giveaways are going and write when the next handoff action will happen. For an item you could not find, write where you will check next and when. It remains unfinished rather than silently becoming a replacement purchase.

- [ ] **8. Close the door and try the five-item test.** Without rummaging through boxes, retrieve each item you found and return it. Adjust confusing labels or awkward placements. For any item still missing, check that its next action is written down. The cupboard is done when the things you have are findable and the gaps are explicit.

**Your finished win:** You can identify what is in the cupboard and retrieve the things you use, without opening every box. Missing items have a specific next action.

**Keep the result:** Put things back in the same named homes. If you share the storage, show the other people the arrangement so they can use it too.
