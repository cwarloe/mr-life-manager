# Product brief — three organizing checklist versions

**Status:** Three complete comparison drafts, 2026-10-27; not reader-tested.
**Public comparison page:** `/organizing-ideas.html`.
**Source page:** [organizing-ideas.html](../../../landing/organizing-ideas.html).

## The three versions

| Draft | Felt problem | Space and scope | Finished win |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Every drawer has become a junk drawer](junk-drawers.md) | Finding a basic tool means searching several drawers. | The kitchen drawers used for an ordinary meal, completed one drawer at a time. | Everyday cooking and eating tools are visible and easy to retrieve. |
| [I put things away, then can't find them](cant-find-things.md) | Putting something away makes it disappear into storage. | One household cupboard or hall closet, anchored to five frequently sought items. | Contents are recognizable; available essentials are retrievable; missing items have specific next actions. |
| [I have to clear the table before we can eat](clear-the-table.md) | The table and chairs serve as storage instead of a place to eat. | One dining table and the seats normally needed for meals. | Set the places and sit down without moving a pile. |

Eight actions each. This is not an experiment in whether six beats twelve
steps. The question is whether the felt problem and finished result make the
task worth doing. The broad [Reset Your Space](../checklist.md) remains
available as the underlying method.

## Audience and problem

For Dana, the overwhelmed householder, and Maya setting up independent life
([audiences](../../../../docs/vision/target-audiences.md)). These serve
[home problems](../../../../docs/research/customer-problems.md#home-general):
undefined homes, accidental storage, unusable storage, and surfaces that become
drop zones. No claim is made about which headline resonates with those audiences.

## Outcome, domains, and principles

The relevant [customer outcome](../../../../docs/vision/customer-outcomes.md#cleaner-more-functional-homes)
is that daily things are reachable and the home supports actual activities.
Each draft ends with its own observable test, rather than a generic promise
of being organized.

Primary domain: Home; secondary: Hospitality
([domain map](../../../../frameworks/life-domains.md)).
Uses [house principles](../../../../docs/vision/principles.md) 1 (defined homes),
4 (systems over memory), 5 (easy access), 8 (dignity), 9 (simple enough to keep),
and 10 (teach the reason).

## Ladder, format, and price

Free task checklists under ADR-020 and ADR-022 in the
[decision log](../../../../planning/decisions.md). They may become distinct entry
points after review. This iteration publishes all three on one printable public
comparison page. It does not choose a winner, add three homepage choices, or
create signup promises and automations. There is no new paid offer.

The public page is direct-link for comparison, with semantic anchors for each
version and browser checkboxes. It is a review surface, not a registered
doing-mode entry page. Existing entry pages and email sequences continue to work.

## Sources and adaptations

All three use the [2014 recording](../../../../source-material/2014-01-15-dave-webster-vu-intervention.md),
its [analysis](../../../../source-material/2014-01-15-analysis.md), and the
[principles engine](../../../../frameworks/dave-principles.md).

| Method | Transcript evidence | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Begin with use and constraints | 03:58–07:40 | Meal-time drawers; frequently lost household items; a table used for meals. |
| Open working space and start easy | 08:09–10:14; 16:10–17:26 | Work one drawer, box, shelf, or tabletop batch at a time. |
| Sort into clear destinations | 11:49–12:53 | Trash, giveaways, frequent use, occasional use; return misplaced items to their homes. |
| Group like items | 10:14–11:16; 15:08–15:41; 17:51–18:55 | Utensils; household supplies; the materials repeatedly left on the table. |
| Clean exposed space | 19:55–21:07 | Wipe the emptied drawer, shelf, or tabletop. |
| Make contents visible and place by use | 07:13–08:39; 17:26–18:20; 24:31–26:19; 28:44–30:01 | Easy everyday reach, identifiable groups, consistent homes. |
| Count supplies and specify next actions | 10:42–11:49; 12:53–13:58 | Measured storage gaps and dated handoff actions. |

The exact spaces, five-item list, labels as an alternative to clear containers,
small-batch boundaries, paper tray, shared-storage explanation, and finish tests
are our design. Final trash removal is our close-out step; the recording shows
trash staged for removal. The table version also draws on the written
[weekly schedule](../../../../source-material/2009-11-04-dave-webster-weekly-schedule.md)
and its instruction that tables are not storage. None of the reader-facing
wording is presented as a Dave quotation.

## What remains to learn

First find out which problem people recognize in their own home. Then observe
whether they start and reach the specific finish: retrieve meal tools, retrieve
household supplies, or sit down at the table. At follow-up, ask whether the homes
are still usable. Headline preference, task completion, and durability are
different observations; there is no winner or measured completion-time claim yet.
