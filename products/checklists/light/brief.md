### Where it came from

Extracted from Part 3 of
[What Is This Room For?](../room-for/brief.md), which was doing four jobs at once
and overwhelming readers. That part was the only section of the guide that
**taught** anything — the rest asked the reader to produce. Burying a teaching
asset inside a worksheet wasted it.

The underlying material is Dave's: lighting runs through the whole
[2010 house goals document](../../../source-material/2010-01-02-dave-webster-house-goals.md)
— track, recessed, indirect, sconces, *"make it light and open when wanted, can
tone it down for evening use."* That was written for a condo owner with a
decorator budget. This is the renter-scale translation, which is the job
[the source analysis](../../../source-material/2009-supply-and-goals-analysis.md)
said every guide has to do.

### Why it's a guide and not a checklist

Deliberate exception to the [checklist standard](../README.md). There is nothing
to tick — it's six short explanations and **one action at the end**. The reader
doesn't need to decide anything; they need to be told something nobody told them.

That makes it the only free asset that delivers value on the first read rather
than on completion, and it's the cheapest to act on: a lamp and a matching bulb.

### What's in it

| | Why it's in |
|---|---|
| Two low lights beat one overhead | The single highest-impact change available in an afternoon |
| Kelvin, with a table | Mixing 2700K and 5000K in one room reads as "wrong" and nobody can name why |
| Lumens, not watts | LEDs broke the watts-to-brightness link everyone learned |
| Something you can turn down | A lamp used instead of the big light costs the price of a lamp |
| Light where the task is | Your own shadow lands on the chopping board |
| Replace dead bulbs the day they go | A dark corner becomes the place things get put down |

### Decisions worth recording

**Renter-first, stated in the second block.** Almost all of it unplugs and comes
with you, and none of it needs permission. This is the most portable advice on
the site and the guide says so early, before anyone decides it isn't for them.

**Hedged where it should be.** "Two lamps beat one ceiling light" is labelled a
rule of thumb rather than physics. The dimmer advice names the real gotcha —
dimmable bulb *and* an LED-rated switch, or it buzzes and it isn't the bulb's
fault.

**No product links, no brands, no prices beyond "about forty pounds and an
evening."** Consistent with
[commerce-rules.md](../../../docs/business/commerce-rules.md): basics carry no
commission, so there's nothing to distort and no reason to link.

**It hands off rather than over-reaching.** The last note says that if the room
still feels wrong after fixing the light, the question is what the room is *for*
— which is a different guide, named but not sold.

### Untested assumptions

1. **That "check the light first" lands as a revelation.** It's the whole
   premise. If readers find it obvious, the guide has no reason to exist.
2. **That the kelvin table is the right depth.** It's the most reference-like
   thing here; it may be the part people actually keep, or the part that makes it
   feel like homework.
3. **That opening with "there's a room you don't much like being in" is
   recognised.** If it isn't, nothing after it lands.
