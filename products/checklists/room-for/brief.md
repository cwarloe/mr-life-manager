# Product Brief — What Is This Room For?

**Status:** Drafted and designed 2026-09-20. Not yet reviewed or tested with a
real reader.
**Tier:** Rung 0 — free ([product-ladder.md](../../../docs/business/product-ladder.md))
**Format:** printable checklist, black-and-white, US Letter. Four parts, one
sitting, ~45 minutes.
**Files:** [`what-is-this-room-for.html`](what-is-this-room-for.html) (designed, printable)

---

### 1. Where this came from

Dave's [2010 "House goals and fix-up"](../../../source-material/2010-01-02-dave-webster-house-goals.md),
written for one specific condo. The document itself is not reusable — skylights,
decorators, refacing cabinets, selling a pub table. **The method is.**

What was lifted:

| Dave's | Here |
|---|---|
| Four first-person goal statements | Part 1, as a menu you tick |
| "What is the focal point of each room?" | Part 2, as "its job" per room |
| "The dining room table, coffee tables are not for storage" | Living room and kitchen rows |
| "Reduce size of table when no company is around" | "Furniture is too big for the room" |
| "Decide where you are going to put current clutter and move it" | The do-list |
| Lighting: track, recessed, indirect, sconces, skylights | Part 3, renter-scale |
| "Set date... Have someone check you to see if you started and finished on time" | Part 4, third column |

The car section comes from the [2009 weekly schedule](../../../source-material/2009-11-04-dave-webster-weekly-schedule.md),
where "check car for anything left in it" appears on all seven days. It was a
documented gap in our coverage; this guide closes it.

### 2. Who is this for?

Wider than the beachhead. It needs no money, no tools and no ownership, so it
works for a first renter — but it's equally aimed at someone who has lived
somewhere for three years and never decided what any of it is for.

### 3. What problem does it solve?

Every other guide we publish assumes you know what you're maintaining and why.
This is the step before that. Dave's own goals were about **people and feeling**,
not tidiness — three of his four. Cleaning was downstream of a decision he had
already made. Our guides skip the decision.

It also answers a complaint the cleaning advice can't: *"I clean and it still
doesn't feel right."* Usually the problem is a room with no job, furniture doing
the wrong one, or one overhead light.

### 4. Why a checklist and not a worksheet

Decided deliberately. A blank worksheet is far easier to abandon than a tick
list, which puts it in direct conflict with
[ADR-014](../../../planning/decisions.md) and
[Principle 11](../../../docs/vision/principles.md). **Every choice in this guide
is named.** The reader is selecting from options, never facing a blank line —
except in Part 4, where writing it down *is* the deliverable.

The two hard caps are the completion mechanism: **three to five goals**, and
**five do-list items**. Both are stated as refusals rather than suggestions.

### 5. What's deliberately not in it

- **No spending.** Lamps and bulbs are the only purchases mentioned, and they're
  generic — no products, no links. Per
  [commerce-rules.md](../../../docs/business/commerce-rules.md), basics carry no
  commission anyway, so there's nothing to distort.
- **No décor advice.** Colour theory, styling and arrangement are a different
  business and we'd be faking the expertise. Dave hired decorators; he didn't
  pretend to be one.
- **No ownership assumptions.** Nothing requires permission from a landlord.

### 6. Open question for review

**Should the body credit Dave by name?** It currently doesn't, matching the other
five guides. The provenance is real and would carry weight, but
[ADR-011](../../../planning/decisions.md) hasn't been worked through for
in-product attribution. Flagged, not decided.

### 7. Known risks

- **It's a different shape from the other five.** They tell you what to do; this
  asks you to decide. That's the point, but it will convert differently and
  should be measured separately.
- **Part 2 is long.** Seven rooms. If testing shows drop-off, cut to four (way
  in, living, kitchen, bedroom) and move the rest to a paid version.
- **Untested claim:** that "two lamps beat one ceiling light" lands as
  revelatory rather than obvious. It's the strongest line in Part 3 if it works.
