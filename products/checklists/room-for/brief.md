real reader.

**Format:** printable checklist, black-and-white, US Letter. Four parts, one

> **Rewritten 2026-09-20.** The first version shipped with **76 tick boxes**
> across seven rooms and four parts — it moved the analysis paralysis inside the
> product instead of removing it, which is the exact failure
> [ADR-014](../../../planning/decisions.md#adr-014--completion-is-what-converts)
> exists to prevent. It is now **one room, four steps, 13 ticks, about twenty
> minutes.** Part 3 (light) was pulled out as
> [its own guide](../light/brief.md); the whole-home goals material moved to a
> question in the email sequence, where it belongs.

### 1. Where this came from

Dave's [2010 "House goals and fix-up"](../../../source-material/2010-01-02-dave-webster-house-goals.md),
written for one specific condo. The document itself isn't reusable — skylights,
decorators, refacing cabinets. **The method is.**

| Dave's | Here |
|---|---|
| "What is the focal point of each room?" | Step 2 — pick the room's one job |
| "The dining room table, coffee tables are not for storage" | Step 3 — furniture doing a job it wasn't meant for |
| "Decide where you are going to put current clutter and move it" | Step 4 — the three things |
| "Set date… Have someone check you to see if you started and finished on time" | Step 4, third column |

### 2. What changed in the rewrite

**Seven rooms became one**, chosen by the reader — and not the worst room, *the
one they're in most*. Effort on a daily room pays back daily.

**Twenty-four goal options became one pick-one list of seven jobs.** The jobs are
universal rather than room-specific, which is what makes a single generic guide
work for any room. "Storing things" is included as a real answer, because a room
that is honestly storage beats one pretending to be a study.

**Forty-one room-specific problems became six universal failure modes.** They're
failure modes, not chores — something with no home, furniture doing the wrong
job, clearing before using, an unowned pile, one overhead light, and two jobs
losing to each other. Between them they cover nearly everything the seven-room
version listed.

**Five do-list items became three.**

Total decisions: one room, one job, up to six ticks, three actions. Against the
[standard](../README.md) of roughly a dozen.

### 3. Who it's for

Wider than the beachhead. No money, no tools, no ownership required.

### 4. What problem it solves

Every other guide assumes you know what you're maintaining and why. This is the
step before. Dave's own goals were about **people and feeling** — three of his
four — with cleaning downstream of a decision already made. It also answers the
complaint the cleaning advice can't: *"I clean and it still doesn't feel right."*

### 5. Open

- **Currency and idiom read British** in places across the free tier. The
  beachhead is US. Needs a site-wide decision, not quiet per-file fixes.
- **Untested:** that "pick the room you're in most, not the worst one" is
  followed. The instinct will be to pick the worst one.
