How Often Should I…?

The cadences nobody tells you, in one place.

This is the most common question in running a home, and it's almost never answered plainly. So here's the honest version.

Two numbers per row Good enough is the standard that actually keeps a home working. Better is the ideal, for when you have the time or the situation calls for it. Good enough is genuinely fine. A schedule you keep beats a stricter one you abandon.

Organized by how often, not by room — so it turns into a routine instead of a reference you never open.

The honest footnotes

These are starting points, not laws. Pets, allergies, kids, a dusty climate, working from home, or a health condition all shift them. Adjust, and don't feel bad about it.

If you're behind on all of it, don't start here. Pick one room — usually the kitchen — get it to baseline, and add the ten-minute nightly reset. Then add one line at a time. Trying to adopt this whole page at once is the most reliable way to abandon it by Thursday.

Renting? A good deal of the annual list is your landlord's job. Your job is to notice and report it in writing, promptly. That's the whole task — and it's what protects your deposit.

Health rows are scheduling reminders only. Your doctor and dentist set your actual intervals.

The ⚠ rows are different in kind. Dryer lint, dryer vents, smoke alarms, and backups aren't about a home looking nice. Everything else here can slip a week without consequence. Those can't.
