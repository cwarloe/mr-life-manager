# Product Brief — How Often Should I…?

**Status:** Draft 1, 2026-09-08. Not reviewed, not designed, not fact-checked by
a second pair of eyes.
**Tier:** Rung 0 — free ([product-ladder.md](../../../docs/business/product-ladder.md))
**Format:** 2-page printable reference, black-and-white, US Letter. Designed to
be **taped inside a cabinet door.**

---

### 1. Who is this for?

Everyone in [target-audiences.md](../../../docs/vision/target-audiences.md).
Uniquely, this asset has **no persona ceiling** — Dana at 34 needs it as much as
Maya at 20, which makes it the widest-reaching thing in the free tier.

### 2. What problem does it solve?

**The single most-asked question in the entire category.**
[customer-problems.md](../../../docs/research/customer-problems.md) identifies it
as the first of five cross-cutting patterns: *"Nobody knows the cadence.
'How often should I…' shows up in every single domain."*

### 3. What outcome should it produce?

Rung 2 (routines) and rung 3 (fewer crises) of
[customer-outcomes.md](../../../docs/vision/customer-outcomes.md). Target quote:

> "I don't have to decide what to clean. It's already decided."

### 4. Domains covered

Home · Food · Maintenance · Money · Digital Life · Transportation · Health
(scheduling only) — **7 of 13.** The broadest free asset in the ladder.

### 5. Principles it serves

§3 preventative over crisis (the whole document *is* this principle in table
form) · §4 systems beat memory · §6 reset before overwhelm · §9 simple enough to
keep

Sits beside the [First Apartment Checklist](../first-apartment/) at rung 0. That
one is **sequenced for a moment**; this one is a **standing reference**. Leads

**$0.** Chosen as a free asset specifically because it is the most **shareable**

photographs it, it travels. Free assets that don't spread on their own make paid
tiers that don't sell.

## Design decisions, and the reasoning

**Organized by frequency, not by room.** A room-organized list is a reference. A
frequency-organized list **converts directly into a routine** — which is the
product. This is the structural difference between this and every "cleaning
schedule" printable on Etsy.

**Every row gives good-enough and ideal, and says which is fine.** This is the
brand's signature move
([brand-positioning.md](../../../docs/business/brand-positioning.md)) and it
matters most here: a cadence list that only states the ideal produces guilt, and
guilt produces abandonment.

**Ranges, not false precision.** Where real-world variation is large (oil
changes, water heaters), the honest answer is "check your manual" — and saying so
builds more trust than inventing a number.

**Health is scheduling only.** No medical advice.
[life-domains.md](../../../frameworks/life-domains.md) sets that boundary and it
is not negotiable here.

**Fire-risk items are marked.** Dryer lint and vents are the two places where the
consequence of skipping is categorically different from "it gets dirty."
