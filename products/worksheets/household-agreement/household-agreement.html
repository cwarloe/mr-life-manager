The Household Agreement

Decide it once, together, before it's a problem.

Almost every roommate fight and most household resentment comes from the same place: nobody ever said out loud who does what. So it gets absorbed by whoever notices first — and that person slowly becomes the household's memory, and burns out.

This takes about forty minutes. Do it at the start, when nobody's annoyed yet. Fill it in together, print two copies, stick one on the fridge.

Why writing it down works An unwritten expectation gets renegotiated every single time. A written one was decided once. That's the entire trick — you're not being bureaucratic, you're closing a decision so it stops costing you.

Why this section exists Noticing is work. Tracking is work. Being the person who has to bring it up is especially work. If one person is doing all of it, they're carrying a second job nobody has acknowledged — and that's what turns into a fight about dishes that isn't really about dishes.

When it slips — the script

It will slip. Agreeing now how you'll raise it means nobody has to invent a way to do it while annoyed.

Within a few days

Say it early, while it's still small and boring. A week of silence turns a dish into a character judgment.

Name the thing, not the person

"The kitchen's been piling up this week" — not "you never do the dishes."

Ask before you assume

"Is something going on, or did it just get away from you?" Usually it's the second one.

Fix the system, not just the instance

If the same thing keeps slipping, the agreement is wrong. Change the agreement.

Assume good faith

Almost nobody is trying to get away with anything. They forgot, or they're underwater, or their normal is different from yours.

08

Check back in

An agreement nobody revisits becomes an agreement nobody honors. Put a real date on it.

Ten minutes, weekly Anything coming up? Anything not working? That's the whole meeting. It sounds unnecessary right up until the month you skip it.

We agree to this

Not a contract — a record of what we decided, so nobody has to remember it.

The awkward forty minutes now is cheaper than the year of quiet resentment later.
