We keep arguing about the dishes.

Write one mutually accepted agreement: the job, finished standard, owner, timing and a review date. Agreement is the finish; a discussion alone is not.

Both people need to be willing and available. Bring a pen and paper or a shared note. Choose the dishes as the one job to discuss. Agree on a conversation time together before using the calendar option.

This tool does not assign chores without consent or guarantee agreement in fifteen minutes. If either person wants to stop or you cannot agree, record the open question and arrange another discussion; do not treat the job as assigned.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Ready to do this? Start with the first step.

The reminder reserves 15 minutes to start, not a tested finish time. Allow time for the whole task. If a prerequisite is missing, arrange it before choosing your start.

  1. Name one recurring situation together.

    Describe what happens with the dishes and ask the other person how they see it. Keep this conversation on the job rather than judgments about either person.

    A shared description is a starting point; it does not require agreeing on blame.

  2. Write a finished standard you both accept.

    Describe the visible result, such as dishes washed or loaded and the sink usable. Ask each person whether that standard is acceptable. If not, write the difference rather than assuming agreement.

    The job needs a shared standard before anyone can fairly be responsible for it.

  3. Agree an owner or explicit rotation.

    Ask who can take each turn. Include noticing the job and checking supplies. Write only an ownership arrangement both people accept.

    A proposed assignment is not consent.

  4. Agree the timing.

    Choose when the first turn will be finished and when later turns normally happen. Check actual availability and constraints together. Record a workable time rather than a unilateral deadline.

    A time helps only if the person taking the turn agrees it is workable.

  5. Read the whole agreement back.

    Record the standard, owner or rotation and timing in one place both use. Read it aloud together and check for different interpretations. Keep unresolved points labeled as open.

    The result should not depend on one person remembering the conversation.

  6. Accept it together and set a review.

    Choose a review time and ask both people to confirm the complete arrangement. If either does not accept it, do not mark Finished. Write what remains open and agree when, or whether, to return to the conversation.

    Acceptance now is the finish for this task. Whether the arrangement works in practice needs checking at the review.

Agreement or still unresolved?

Only finish if both people accepted the standard, ownership, timing and review. If not, the dishes are not assigned by this page; keep the open question and any mutually chosen discussion time.

We both accepted the full agreement.

Check the agreement, not just the person.

If the job keeps slipping, revisit the timing, the standard, or the ownership. The point is a workable arrangement, not a new reason to keep score.
