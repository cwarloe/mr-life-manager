We keep arguing about the dishes.

Make one agreement about one recurring job: who owns it, what done means, and when it happens. Leave the rest of the household agreement for another conversation.

Today's job. Do this with the person who shares the job. If they are not available, agree on a time together before using the calendar option. This is a conversation and a written agreement, not a way to assign someone a chore without asking.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Ready to do this? Start with the first step.

If you are not in the right place or ready to act, pick the exact time you will start. Set aside fifteen minutes to begin.

  1. Name the recurring problem together.

    Start with the situation: the dishes keep piling up, or nobody knows whose turn it is. Keep the conversation on this job.

    Describing the task makes it easier to discuss than a judgment about the other person.

  2. Agree what finished looks like.

    Write a visible minimum you both accept: for example, dishes washed or loaded, the eating utensils ready, and the sink usable.

    Different definitions of done can turn a completed effort into another argument.

  3. Choose one owner for each turn.

    Agree who owns the job, or write an explicit rotation. Owning it includes noticing when it needs doing and checking whether supplies are running out.

    A job described as everyone's responsibility can still fall to whoever notices first.

  4. Choose the first deadline.

    Agree when the first turn will be finished and when later turns normally happen. Check that the timing works for the person taking it.

    A named owner without an agreed time can leave both people expecting something different.

  5. Write the agreement where both can see it.

    Record the owner or rotation, the minimum standard, and the timing in one place you both use. Read it back together.

    The result should not depend on one person remembering the entire conversation.

  6. Agree when you will check it.

    Choose a specific time to ask whether the arrangement worked. Confirm that both people accept this first version before ending the conversation.

    The win is one shared decision with a way to revisit it, not a promise that nobody will ever forget.

One job has an agreement.

Done means both people accepted the arrangement. If you did not agree, do not treat the job as assigned; write down what remains to discuss and when you will return to it.

This job is finished.

Check the agreement, not just the person.

If the job keeps slipping, revisit the timing, the standard, or the ownership. The point is a workable arrangement, not a new reason to keep score.
