Mr. Life Manager

All my clean clothes are still in a pile.

Put one basket away and get tomorrow's clothes ready. The washing is already done; this is the part the machine cannot finish.

Today's job. One basket or one chair's worth of clean clothes. Leave the rest of the laundry alone. You'll need a clear patch of bed and access to your closet or drawers.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Ready to do this? Start with the first step.

If you are not in the right place or ready to act, pick the exact time you will start. Set aside fifteen minutes to begin.

  1. Make a place to work.

    Clear a patch of bed or another clean surface. Bring over just the basket or pile you chose.

    A bounded pile gives this job a finish. Emptying every drawer would make it bigger.

  2. Separate anything that needs washing.

    Put actually dirty items in the hamper. Leave the clean clothes in front of you; don't start a new load now.

    This task finishes clean laundry. Starting the wash would leave the original job waiting again.

  3. Hang the things you normally hang.

    Put shirts, dresses, or other hanging clothes on the hangers you already use. Hang them in their usual closet section.

    Hanging can finish an item without a separate folding stage.

  4. Group and fold the rest.

    Put socks with socks, underwear with underwear, and folded clothes with their own kind. Aim for a stack that fits its home, not matching display folds.

    Grouping removes the search when you need an item tomorrow.

  5. Put each group in its home.

    Move the stacks into their usual drawers or shelves. If there is no defined home, choose one reachable spot and make room for this group there.

    A basket moved to the bedroom is still a basket to search through.

  6. Set out tomorrow's outfit and finish the pile.

    Take out what you need for tomorrow, put it in one place, and return the empty basket. Leave the sorting surface clear.

    Ready-to-wear clothes and an empty basket are the result. Another load can wait.

You know where tomorrow's clothes are.

The basket you chose is empty and the seat or bed is usable again. Another load is another job.

This job is finished.

Make the last step part of the load.

Next time, leave room to put the clothes away when they are dry. The useful finish is clothes ready to wear, not another full basket.
