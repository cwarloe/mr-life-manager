Mr. Life Manager

All my clean clothes are still in a pile.

Finish one basket of clean clothes and set out tomorrow's outfit. The result is clothes in usable homes and an empty basket.

Choose one complete basket belonging to one person. Keep other loads separate. You need a clean sorting surface, enough hangers for the clothes you hang, and usable space in drawers or shelves. Check that space before tipping the basket out.

If storage is full, arrange space first without displacing another pile. Clothes left waiting for a home are still unfinished; buying or redesigning storage is a separate job.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Ready to do this? Start with the first step.

The reminder reserves 15 minutes to start, not a tested finish time. Allow time for the whole task. If a prerequisite is missing, arrange it before choosing your start.

  1. Mark the basket and clear a sorting surface.

    Choose the whole basket you will finish. Clear enough clean bed or table space to lay out one group at a time. Check the drawer and hanging space you will use.

    A named basket bounds the work without turning a whole laundry backlog into one session.

  2. Separate anything that needs washing.

    Put genuinely dirty items into the hamper. Keep clean items in front of you. Do not start another load during this task.

    Putting dirty items in the hamper is containment; the washing remains another task.

  3. Hang the clothes you normally hang.

    Put each hanging item on an available hanger and into its usual closet section. If a home is missing, keep the item with its named group while you arrange space; do not call it put away.

    Use existing storage. An unavailable hanger or full closet is a real prerequisite to resolve.

  4. Group and fold the remaining clean clothes.

    Group socks, underwear and other folded clothes by type. Fold them to fit the space you checked. Keep another person's clothes together for a handoff rather than deciding their storage.

    The goal is clothes ready to find and wear, not display folds.

  5. Put every group in its usable home.

    Move each group into its drawer, shelf or hanging space. If a group does not fit, record the storage problem and arrange a next action before rescheduling the unfinished task. Do not fill the bed with a displaced pile.

    The basket is finished only when its contents have suitable homes or an agreed handoff.

  6. Set out tomorrow's outfit and check the finish.

    Choose tomorrow's clothes and put them in one reachable place. Check that the basket and sorting surface are empty and the clothes are accessible. If a group is still waiting for storage, use Not right now to schedule finishing it.

    The full win is stored clothes, a usable surface and tomorrow's outfit; an unresolved storage list alone is not that win.

The selected basket is put away.

Tomorrow's clothes are ready, the basket is empty and the sorting surface is usable. If clothes still lack a home, the task remains open.

The clothes are stored. I finished.

Make the last step part of the load.

Next time, leave room to put the clothes away when they are dry. The useful finish is clothes ready to wear, not another full basket.
