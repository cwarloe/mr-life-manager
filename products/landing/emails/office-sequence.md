## The shape

| | When | Ask | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Immediate | The Week, plus the end-of-day close-out | one close-out |
| 2 | Day 3 | The same thing, smaller | one drawer |
| 3 | Day 7 | Nothing. One question. | a few words |
| — | After | **Stop.** | — |

**This reader just cleared a desk at work.** The risk is the same as the
one-room door: they think the shelves have to be another whole afternoon, and
never start them. The other risk is specific to an office: new paper arrives
every day, so the desk refills unless the tray actually gets emptied.

---

## Email 1 — immediate

**Subject:** Here's the thing worth printing

> You cleared your desk. Here's what keeps it that way.
>
> https://mrlifemanager.com/print/the-week.pdf
>
> It's one page, adapted from Dave's weekly schedule for a home. Its useful idea for a desk is an end-of-day close-out: return current tools and papers to their authorized homes.
>
> Before you leave, file completed material under your workplace rules and put current tools away. Keep pending work in its authorized place with the owner and next action recorded. Allow the time the job needs; a paper backlog is separate reset work.
>
> Print it and pin it somewhere you'll see it at the end of the day.
>
> — Charles

*Link (not attach): <https://mrlifemanager.com/print/the-week.pdf>*

## Email 2 — day 3

**Subject:** The next one is smaller than you think

> If you're picturing another full afternoon before you touch the shelves, you'll be picturing it for a while. That's on me for how I framed it.
>
> So here's the smaller version.
>
> Do one drawer. Give it twenty minutes. Same three piles as before: throw away, return, or it stays.
>
> You've already made most of the hard decisions about what you keep and where it goes. The second pass is mostly doing, and it goes about twice as fast as the first.
>
> And if it doesn't happen this week, nothing is lost. The desk is still clear.
>
> — Charles

---

## Email 3 — day 7

**Subject:** One question

> There's nothing attached to this one.
>
> I'm trying to figure out what to write next, and the most useful thing anyone has told me so far came out of a question like this one.
>
> What's the one thing that keeps landing on your desk?
>
> A few words is a complete answer. Just hit reply. It's me reading these, not a system.
>
> — Charles

Then it ends. **Silence means stop.**

---

## Notes

**Email 3 asks what keeps landing on the desk** because that is the thing that
refills an office: a report that prints every week, samples that come back from
the shop, mail. The answers tell us whether a second office page is worth
writing, and what it should be about.
