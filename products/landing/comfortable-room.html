Choose one spot and one activity: reading, talking or sitting with a drink. You need a usable seat, an existing movable surface and working lighting or daylight. Try the spot at the time of day you mean to use it.

Work with what you already have and can move safely. Fixture installation, heavy furniture moves and shopping are separate jobs. If the available equipment cannot support the activity, record the missing need; identifying it does not mean the spot is comfortable yet.

Ready to do this? Start with the first step.

The reminder reserves 15 minutes to start, not a tested finish time. Allow time for the whole task. If a prerequisite is missing, arrange it before choosing your start.

  1. Try the chosen activity in the spot.

    Sit down and read a page, talk, or hold your drink. Notice the specific obstacle: blocked seat, poor light or nowhere to set things. Choose the same activity for the final test.

    A real use gives you something observable to improve.

  2. Clear the seat and access route.

    Return things with known homes. Put unresolved loose items in an available container outside the route. Keep the seat and path usable; get help rather than moving heavy furniture alone.

    A temporary clutter container is a separate sorting task, not a finished storage system.

  3. Compare the available light.

    At the time you intend to use the spot, try the existing lamps or daylight. For an evening reading spot, test after dark.

    A daytime test cannot tell you whether the same arrangement works at night.

  4. Adjust existing lighting safely.

    If a movable working lamp helps, switch it off before moving it and follow its instructions. Keep the cord clear of the walkway. Do not install a fixture or improvise an unsafe cord route.

    Use available lighting; a missing lamp or suitable outlet is a need to resolve separately.

  5. Make one reachable place for your things.

    Clear an existing nearby surface for your book, glasses or drink. Put displaced items in their homes or the clutter container. Keep the path clear and avoid balancing an unstable makeshift surface.

    The place should support the activity without repeated trips or juggling.

  6. Repeat the activity and decide whether it works.

    Try the same activity in the intended lighting conditions. Adjust once if needed. If it still does not work, write the specific need and a next action, then reschedule instead of marking Finished.

    A usable sitting spot is the win. A documented equipment gap is useful but is a different, incomplete result.

Does the spot support the activity?

Finish when you can do the chosen activity with a usable seat, suitable light and a reachable surface. If not, keep the missing need and next action visible.
