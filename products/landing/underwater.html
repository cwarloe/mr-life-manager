Mr. Life Manager

Your place is a mess. Again.

You've tried the lists and the apps, the videos and the workbooks. This isn't another thing to read. It's about forty minutes, it starts tonight, and it counts if you only do the kitchen and one basket of clutter.

This is not a deep clean. You are not catching up on the month. You are doing the few things that keep tomorrow from being harder than today. Do them in order. Stop when you are tired.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Are you there right now?

This only works at home, where the mess is. If you're somewhere else, pick the exact time you'll do it.

  1. Reset the kitchen first.

    15 min

    Dishes washed or in the dishwasher. Counters wiped. Trash out, new bag in. Stovetop if it's still sticky. Then stop touching the kitchen.

    Why this and not the bedroom. A kitchen you reset every day never needs a big clean. Skip it for four days and it becomes a project. The kitchen is the room that makes a hard week feel out of control, and it's the one you use first tomorrow morning.

  2. Put the clutter from your main room into one basket.

    10 min

    One laundry basket. Walk the room you actually sit in. Put everything that doesn't live there into the basket. Basket goes in the bedroom. Door shuts.

    You need one clear place to sit, not a new way of living. Tomorrow you can sort the basket. Tonight you need one room that looks cared for.

  3. Make the bed.

    4 min

    Pull the sheet up. Pull the duvet up. Put the pillows back. You don't need to do more than that. Do not wash the sheets unless they are actually wet.

    One thing in the house is finished. Making the bed is the fastest way to make a room look better.

  4. Wash one load, or just get the clothes off the floor.

    5 min

    If you can finish it today — wash it. If you will be out, just get the dirty clothes off the floor and into the hamper. Do not start a load you cannot finish.

    Clothes in the hamper are enough for tonight. A wet load left overnight starts to smell and has to be washed again.

  5. Open the mail for two minutes, then sit down.

    5 min

    Open whatever has been sitting there. Bills in one pile. Junk in the bin. Anything with a due date, put the date on a calendar or a note on the fridge. Do not pay anything unless it is due in three days.

    Unopened mail is how late fees happen. You do not need a budget tonight. You need to know whether anything is due soon.

  6. Write down the first thing you'll do tomorrow.

    2 min

    One line on a scrap of paper or your phone: the next small thing. Not "clean the house." Something like empty the dishwasher or put the basket away.

    Tonight's list is done. Tomorrow starts with a plan. One written next step means you don't have to work out where to start in the morning.

One thing not to do

Do not start a whole-house clean tonight. That is how this turns into three hours and the feeling that you failed again.

The list is the job. When the list is done, you are done.

That's enough for tonight. The rest can wait.

Whatever you got through is what there was time for. Kitchen first, because that is the room that gets worse fastest.

If you only did the first two, you already changed tomorrow morning.

This is not a character problem.

The house got behind because nothing in it had a next step smaller than "deal with all of it." That is a problem with the system, not with you. It feels personal only because nobody ever explained it as a system.

A kitchen reset plus one clear room is a system. Doing it on a random Tuesday, with nobody coming over, is what keeps it working.

"Do it now" does not mean do everything now. It means do the small thing while it is still small.

Dave taught me that after I had spent a long time treating every untidy room as a personal failure. It isn't. It's a pile that needed a first step.

What just happened

The house is livable again.

Nothing on that list made the house impressive. Each item kept one part of the house from getting worse.

That's the method when there is no guest and no move-in day. Do the small things before they become big ones.
