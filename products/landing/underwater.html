Mr. Life Manager

Nothing particular happened. You're just underwater.

Six things that put a floor under it. About forty minutes — and it still counts if you only do the first two.

This is not a deep clean. You are not catching up on the month. You are stopping the slide so tomorrow is not worse than today. Do them in order. Stop when you run out of steam.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Ready to start?

The steps are below either way. These just clear everything else out of the way.

  1. Kitchen reset. That's the whole first move.

    15 min

    Dishes washed or in the dishwasher. Counters wiped. Trash out, new bag in. Stovetop if it's still sticky. Then stop touching the kitchen.

    Why this and not the bedroom. A kitchen that has been reset never needs a rescue. Skip it four days and you have a project. This is the room that turns a bad week into a crisis, and it's the one you use first tomorrow morning.

  2. One basket. Clear the room you live in.

    10 min

    One laundry basket. Walk the room you actually sit in. Put everything that doesn't live there into the basket. Basket goes in the bedroom. Door shuts.

    You are buying a surface, not organizing your life. Tomorrow you will sort the basket. Tonight you need one room that does not look like the week won.

  3. Make the bed.

    4 min

    Pull the sheet up. Pull the duvet up. Put the pillows back. That is the whole job. Do not wash the sheets unless they are actually wet.

    One finished object in the house. A made bed is the cheapest way for a room to look handled.

  4. Start one load of laundry. Or don't.

    5 min

    If you can finish it today — wash it. If you will be out, just get the dirty clothes off the floor and into the hamper. Do not start a load you cannot finish.

    The floor is not a hamper. Containing the clothes is the win. A wet load left overnight is a new problem.

  5. Open the mail. Two minutes. Then sit down.

    5 min

    Open whatever has been sitting there. Bills in one pile. Junk in the bin. Anything with a due date, put the date on a calendar or a note on the fridge. Do not pay anything unless it is due in three days.

    Unopened mail is how a manageable week becomes a fee. You do not need a budget tonight. You need to know if something is on fire.

  6. Write tomorrow's first ten minutes.

    2 min

    One line on a scrap of paper or your phone: the next small thing. Not "clean the apartment." Something like empty the dishwasher or put the basket away.

    Tonight is a floor. Tomorrow is a direction. One written next step means you don't invent the day from scratch.

One thing not to do

Do not start a whole-house clean tonight. That is how this turns into three hours and the feeling that you failed again.

The list is the job. When the list is done, you are done.

That's the floor. The rest can wait.

Whatever you got through is what there was time for. Kitchen first, because that is the room that rots a week the fastest.

If you only did the first two, you already changed tomorrow morning.

This is not a character problem.

The place got away from you because nothing in it has a next action smaller than "deal with all of it." That is a systems problem. It feels like a you problem because nobody named it the other way.

A kitchen reset plus one clear room is a system. Doing it on a random Tuesday, with nobody coming over, is the whole method.

"Do it now" does not mean do everything now. It means do the small thing while it is still small.

Dave taught me that after I had spent a long time treating every mess as a moral event. It isn't. It's a pile that needed a first move.
