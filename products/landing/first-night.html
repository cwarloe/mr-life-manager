It's your first night in your own place.

Six steps. Have a bed ready, bathroom essentials out, and somewhere to sit and eat, with move-in photos taken and basic checks recorded.

Before you open more boxes: find your phone and key, bedding, toilet paper, soap, towel, toothbrush and toothpaste. You need an assembled bed or mattress and a place to sit. If something is missing, getting it is part of tonight's work.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Ready to start? Give it about an hour and a half.

Longer if something's missing and you have to go get it. Not moved in yet? Pack tonight's essentials in one box you can reach.

  1. Photograph each room before unpacking more.

    Use your phone to photograph the walls, floors, oven and fridge interiors, and close-ups of existing damage. If people are bringing boxes in, keep walls visible while you make a video walk-through, then take close-ups.

    These records can help establish the condition when you arrived. They do not guarantee the outcome of a deposit dispute.

  2. Make the bed before you get tired.

    Find the sheets, put them on, and set out your blanket and pillows. Finish the place you will sleep tonight.

    Accessible bedding saves a late-night search. Bed assembly or acquiring a mattress needs additional time.

  3. Set out the bathroom essentials.

    Toilet paper on the holder. Soap by the sink. One towel hung up. Toothbrush and toothpaste within reach. Leave the rest of the bathroom box for tomorrow.

    The room is usable for tonight without unpacking every toiletry.

  4. Locate the power, water and heat controls.

    Find and photograph the breaker box, water shutoff and thermostat. Look for sink and toilet shutoffs too. If a control is locked, shared or unclear, write that down and ask the landlord or building manager where it is and who can access it. Do not force it or operate unfamiliar equipment.

    Knowing what is accessible helps later. A location request is still an open item until you have the answer.

  5. Check the fixtures and send one written fault report.

    Test hot water at each faucet, smoke alarms with their test buttons, your outside door key and lock, and the windows. Use safe access; do not climb on furniture. List faults and any checks you could not safely complete. Send the list and photos to the landlord or building manager: “On move-in today I found __. Please arrange repair or access and confirm the next step.”

    A dated report records what you found. Reporting a fault does not mean it is repaired; keep unresolved safety problems visible.

  6. Clear one surface and one place to sit.

    Clear a table or counter and one chair. Put out something to eat and sit down. Use food you already have or arrange a meal; cooking or delivery can add time.

    You have a place to rest tonight and a usable surface for tomorrow.

Unfinished safety checks need attention tonight

If an alarm does not respond, is missing, or cannot be tested safely, contact the landlord or building manager promptly for help. A reported alarm problem is not a working alarm.

You have tonight's essentials in place.

The bed and bathroom are ready, and you have somewhere to sit and eat. Move-in photos are saved and checks are recorded. Keep any unresolved fault or access request on your follow-up list; do not mark it repaired.

The rest of the unpacking can wait until tomorrow.

Before you unpack the rest.

Tomorrow, choose a home for each thing as you unpack it. Put everyday items where you use them. You can make those decisions a box at a time.

What just happened

You made the first night work.

You prepared for sleep, made the essentials accessible, and recorded the condition and faults while they were fresh.
