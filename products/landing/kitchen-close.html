I wake up to yesterday's kitchen mess.

Leave one clear working patch and the things you need for breakfast ready. Tomorrow can start with using the kitchen instead of clearing it.

Today's job. Tonight's kitchen close, not a deep clean. Focus on a breakfast place and the dishes you need in the morning. If there is a much larger backlog, do this small patch first; the rest is a different job.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Ready to do this? Start with the first step.

If you are not in the right place or ready to act, pick the exact time you will start. Set aside fifteen minutes to begin.

  1. Put food where it belongs.

    Put away food and ingredients that are still on the work surface, following their storage directions. Leave anything needed for breakfast in its normal suitable storage.

    Food and loose ingredients make a work surface harder to use.

  2. Choose tomorrow's breakfast dishes.

    Pick the bowl, plate, cup, and utensils you will need. Put clean ones aside, or identify the ones to wash now.

    You are preparing for a real morning, rather than trying to finish every possible kitchen job.

  3. Wash those dishes or put them away.

    Wash and leave the needed items ready to use. Put other dishes in the dishwasher if there is room; leave the rest outside the patch you are clearing.

    One usable breakfast set is a finish you can reach even when there is more washing left.

  4. Clear one working patch.

    Return the things with obvious homes. Put unresolved loose items in one container away from the preparation area and the walkway.

    You need enough space to make breakfast, not a perfect kitchen.

  5. Wipe the patch and deal with its trash.

    Use a surface-appropriate cleaning method, then take out a full trash bag if it would block the next use. Leave the work area usable.

    A clear patch that is still sticky or surrounded by a spill has not finished its job.

  6. Check the morning setup.

    Put the breakfast dishes within reach and check that you can use the counter and any appliance you need without moving a pile. Then stop.

    Tomorrow's first task can now be making breakfast. The rest of the house can wait.

Tomorrow has a place to start.

Your breakfast things are ready and there is somewhere to use them. Leave the larger cleanups for another session.

This job is finished.

Repeat the useful part.

The same small close-out can work on another night. Keep the order that helped you get the morning essentials ready.
