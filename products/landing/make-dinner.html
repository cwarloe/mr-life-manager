It's dinnertime. I don't know what to make.

Make bean wraps with a can of beans and tortillas. One ordinary meal, without choosing from a week of recipes.

Today's job. Have a can of ready-cooked beans and tortillas, plus any salsa, cheese, or seasoning you already like. You'll need a pan and a spoon. Missing the basics? Pick a time after you can get them; this task ends with food, not a shopping list.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Ready to do this? Start with the first step.

If you are not in the right place or ready to act, pick the exact time you will start. Set aside fifteen minutes to begin.

  1. Clear one working patch.

    Make enough room for the pan, spoon, tortillas, and ingredients. Put them in front of you before turning on the stove.

    Gathering the ingredients first keeps a simple meal from turning into a search.

  2. Open and drain the beans.

    Open the can of ready-cooked beans, drain them, and put them in the pan. Add a small splash of water.

    Canned beans keep this meal to warming and assembling, rather than cooking a separate protein from scratch.

  3. Warm the beans.

    Warm the beans over medium heat, stirring, until hot. Follow any heating instructions on the can. Add a little water if the pan becomes dry.

    You are making one familiar meal, not trying a collection of new ingredients.

  4. Get the tortillas ready.

    Set out the tortillas. If the package calls for warming them first, follow those directions. Put any topping you want within reach.

    Getting the filling and the tortillas ready is enough. You do not need a second cooked side dish.

  5. Fill the wraps.

    Spoon in the beans. Add salsa, cheese, or another topping you already have and want. Fold the tortillas around the filling.

    Beans and tortillas are enough to start. Optional toppings do not need to become an extra trip.

  6. Serve and turn off the heat.

    Put the wraps on plates and switch off the stove or other appliance you used. Leave the pan ready to wash after the meal.

    The finish is a meal you can eat. Shopping for six future dinners can wait.

Dinner exists.

Turn off the heat and eat. You do not need to plan the week's meals tonight.

This job is finished.

Keep one fallback meal.

If this worked for you, note the ingredients you used so you can replace them when they run out. You now have one meal you do not need to invent again.
