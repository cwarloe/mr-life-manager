It's dinnertime. I don't know what to make.

Make bean wraps for one person: a complete meal you can serve, with the cooking appliance switched off.

For one person: one 15-ounce can of ready-cooked beans and two large tortillas. Use the filling you want; store any unused beans as the package directs. Salsa or cheese is optional. For more people, decide the quantities before starting.

You need a compatible stove and pan, stirring spoon, can opener unless the can has a pull tab, strainer, sink, water and a plate. Check the food labels, heating directions and any dietary needs first. Gather missing basics before you start; shopping is a separate job.

Nothing to sign up for. Start at one.

Ready to do this? Start with the first step.

The reminder reserves 15 minutes to start, not a tested finish time. Allow time for the whole task. If a prerequisite is missing, arrange it before choosing your start.

  1. Clear a working patch and gather everything.

    Put the pan, spoon, plate, tortillas and beans within reach. Check that you can use the sink and stove. Read the bean and tortilla preparation directions before turning on heat.

    Gathering ingredients and tools avoids searches with an appliance already running.

  2. Open and drain the ready-cooked beans.

    Open the can with the pull tab or can opener. Put the strainer in the sink and pour in the beans to drain. Transfer the beans to the pan. Follow any label preparation directions.

    Use ready-cooked canned beans; dry beans would be a different cooking task.

  3. Warm the filling using the package directions.

    Heat the beans as the label directs, stirring with the spoon. If the label allows, add a little water to keep them from drying against the pan. Stay with the pan; switch off the heat when the filling is ready.

    The label and appliance instructions determine heating, rather than a guessed universal cooking time.

  4. Prepare the tortillas.

    Lay out the two tortillas. If their package calls for warming, follow those directions with equipment you have. Put any optional topping you already want beside them.

    A topping can stay optional; it should not trigger a shopping trip.

  5. Fill and fold the wraps.

    Spoon filling along the center of each tortilla, leaving room at the edges. Add optional toppings. Fold the sides inward, then roll or fold around the filling. Put the wraps on the plate.

    Keep enough room to fold; unused filling can be stored according to the package.

  6. Check the appliance is off and serve.

    Confirm every appliance you used is switched off. Store unused ingredients as their labels direct. Serve the wraps and eat. Leave pan washing for after the meal.

    The meal is finished when food is ready to eat and the heating appliance is off.

Dinner is ready to eat.

The wraps are served, unused ingredients are stored and the heat is off. Planning future meals can wait.

The meal is ready. I finished.

Keep one fallback meal.

If this worked for you, note the ingredients you used so you can replace them when they run out. You now have one meal you do not need to invent again.
