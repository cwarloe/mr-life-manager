Review Mr. Life Manager's path from a social post to one finished household task. Work with social-action-critic; offer an independent behavioral explanation of failure rather than echoing the marketing verdict. Do not publish, contact participants or alter live accounts.

Read the relevant video source, social launch kit, task page, workflow draft, current house rules, deferred-win research/architecture, route catalog and event implementation. Distinguish current implementation, proposed copy and observed user behavior.

## Questions

- Is there a wanted goal with a concrete consequence, rather than merely an easy chore?
- Can this person act where and when they see the post? What resources and time do they need?
- Is the next action specific enough to perform without another search or decision?
- Does the plan connect a cue/time/place to a response, rather than provide vague encouragement?
- If away, can they save an appointment and return to the correct task? Is scheduling displacing an available immediate start? Do not infer displacement from nonrandom path comparisons.
- Does the finish line describe the actual task outcome? Are prerequisites and work estimates honest?
- Does attention lead out of consumption into doing? A completed view is not the target behavior.
- Does any claim leap from one success to habit formation, reduced future scrolling, dopamine, addiction or neurological "reset"? Reject unsupported causal language while keeping the useful product goal.

## Evidence

Browse primary original studies, systematic reviews or official platform documentation for factual research/platform claims. Check the actual source, date, population, measured behavior and limitations. Label findings separately from inferences and design hypotheses. Correlation is not causation; evidence about vaccination, voting, exercise or clinical interventions does not establish the size of a household-task effect. Do not repeat unsupported statistics from repo research as facts.

Do not demand research for every creative choice. Where direct evidence is missing, state uncertainty and recommend a small practical test. No invented links, conversion forecasts or universal optimal video durations.

## Deliverable

Give a short verdict, a stage-by-stage friction map and the three most useful corrections. For each research claim cite a verified URL and explain what it supports and does not support. Explain the practical implication in plain words.

Propose measurements that fit the existing infrastructure and label their limits. The calendar-added event is a link click; came-back is a return-URL load; finished is self-acknowledgment. Do not equate events with distinct people. A separate, optional later follow-up could test recurring independent action, but first establish the immediate outcome.

Choose a test feasible for a small project. Avoid large experiments or assigning the owner a new research workload. Preserve reader dignity and consent.

## Acquisition standard clarified November 12, 2026

Read `docs/review/felt-pain-marketing/README.md` and the marketing-hooks section
of `content/README.md`. Challenge whether the hook recognizes a specific
problem and distress already present, rather than an aspiration, demographic,
calendar event without unreadiness, or advice someone can agree with and save.
An actual visit tonight plus an unready home is different from wanting to invite
people someday. Ongoing overwhelm can be immediate without a deadline.
Do not invent shame or universal emotional responses.

For this acquisition campaign, remove instructional checklist previews.
Recognition should lead to wanting help and opening the matching task, then
starting or choosing the exact time to do it. Polished production, correct routes
and readable text do not establish compelling marketing. Label creative judgment
and audience evidence separately. The original rendered-video pilot verdict was
limited and is superseded by this creative hold pending revised hooks.
