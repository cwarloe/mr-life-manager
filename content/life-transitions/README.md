# Life Transitions

The moments when everything changes at once.

## Why this matters commercially

**The transition is the buying trigger.** Nobody buys life-management content on
an ordinary Tuesday. They buy it the week they sign a lease, get married, move
across the country, or start over.

Transitions are also when systems break — which makes them the best possible
teaching window, because there's nothing to un-learn.

Marketing, timing, and partnerships should all be built around transitions rather
than around evergreen interest in tidiness.
([target-audiences.md](../../docs/vision/target-audiences.md))

## Planned content

**P1**
- Move-in checklist
- Move-out checklist (get the deposit back) — seasonal and high-search

**P2**
- The complete moving sequence: utilities, address changes, packing, unpacking
- First independent home — the full setup, which becomes *First Place*
- Marriage: merging possessions, accounts, calendars, standards, and families
- New job or relocation: rebuilding routines and relationships from zero
- Starting over after divorce, loss, deployment, or illness
- New baby: rebuilding routines without sleep
- Caring for aging parents
- Job loss

## Content shape

Transition content is **sequenced pathways through the other domains**, not new
subject matter. A transition guide is a curated route — what to do first, second,
third — assembled from content that already exists elsewhere.

That makes this directory unusually cheap to produce once the other domains have
content, and unusually valuable, because sequence is exactly what someone in
transition is missing.

## Tone requirement

Some transitions here — bereavement, divorce, deployment, illness, reentry —
involve people operating at genuinely reduced capacity, often with grief on top of
logistics. Content for them must be **gentler, shorter, and unfailingly free of
"you should have known this."**

Handle with care, or don't handle it at all.
