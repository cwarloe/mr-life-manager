# Life Domains — A Draft Taxonomy

## Why a taxonomy

Every business like this eventually drowns in unorganized tips. A domain
taxonomy is the spine: it tells us where a piece of content lives, what we
haven't covered, what a product should include, and — crucially — what is **out
of scope.**

**Design goals**
- Small enough to hold in your head (13 is near the limit; don't add a 14th
  casually)
- Each domain distinct enough that content routes obviously to one place
- Matches how people actually experience life, not how a librarian would file it
- Maps cleanly onto the [`content/`](../content/) directory structure

**Design tension:** exhaustive versus memorable. We choose memorable. A few
things will belong in two places; note the overlap and pick a primary rather than
inventing a new domain.

## The thirteen domains

### 1. Home

*The physical space you live in and how it's set up.*

Setup and layout · furniture placement by actual use · storage design · defining
where things live · cleaning routines and standards · supplies · the reset
rhythm · seasonal deep cleaning · renting (leases, deposits, landlord vs. tenant
responsibility) · renter's insurance · safety (detectors, extinguisher, shutoffs,
breaker panel) · small repairs

**Not here:** food and the kitchen as a working system (→ Food); scheduled
upkeep of systems and appliances (→ Maintenance); guests (→ Hospitality).

**Why it's first:** it's the visible, urgent, entry-point domain. It's how people
find us and where trust is earned.

### 2. Food

*Getting fed, reliably, without waste or daily crisis.*

Kitchen setup and essential tools · pantry stocking · meal planning · a core
repertoire of repeatable meals · grocery shopping and lists · storage and
leftovers · food safety and dates · reducing waste · cleaning as you cook ·
eating out vs. cooking · cooking for others

**Not here:** the grocery budget (→ Money, though it's taught together);
entertaining menus (→ Hospitality).

### 3. Money

*Managing what comes in, goes out, and gets set aside.*

Accounts and structure · autopay and bill systems · spending visibility ·
budgeting (simple enough to keep) · emergency buffer · debt and student loans ·
credit · saving and retirement basics · insurance · taxes and withholding ·
subscriptions · what things cost · money in couples · giving and generosity

**Not here:** documents and records (→ Paperwork & Admin).

### 4. Time

*Deciding what to do and when, and protecting attention.*

Calendar as single source of truth · capture and task lists · weekly planning and
review · routines (morning, evening, weekly reset) · estimating duration ·
protecting margin · saying no · scheduling maintenance and admin · rest and
sabbath · attention management

**Not here:** work-specific task systems (→ Work); digital tools and notifications
(→ Digital Life).

### 5. Health

*Maintaining the body you use for everything else.*

Preventative care and the appointment cadence · finding and keeping a doctor and
dentist · insurance and how to use it · prescriptions and refills · sleep · basic
nutrition · movement · mental health and when to get help · home medical supplies
· sick-day plan · medical records and history · emergency contacts

**Scope discipline:** we teach the *system* — cadences, records, appointments,
insurance mechanics. We do not give medical, nutritional, or psychological
advice. This boundary is not negotiable.

### 6. Work

*Managing the job, not doing the job.*

Task and commitment tracking · email as a system, not a to-do list · meeting prep
· boundaries between work and home · tracking accomplishments · benefits, PTO,
and actually taking it · professional documents · career admin (résumé, LinkedIn,
references) · workspace setup · commute or remote-work rhythms

**Not here:** the actual craft of any profession. Out of scope, permanently.

### 7. Digital Life

*The information and accounts that make up a modern life.*

Email systems · file organization and naming · cloud storage · backups ·
passwords and a manager · two-factor authentication · account inventory · photos
· notification hygiene · device maintenance and replacement · digital
subscriptions · privacy basics · digital legacy and account recovery

**Why it's separate from Paperwork:** the tools, failure modes, and skills are
entirely different. Losing a file and losing a birth certificate are not the same
problem.

### 8. Relationships

*Sustaining connection on purpose rather than by accident.*

Initiating and maintaining friendship · remembering dates and checking in ·
reciprocity · household agreements with roommates or a spouse · fair division of
labor and invisible labor · conflict and repair · family obligations · rebuilding
community after a move · neighbors · service and generosity toward others

**Not here:** hosting mechanics (→ Hospitality), though the two are deeply
linked — Hospitality is the *practice*, Relationships is the *fabric*.

### 9. Transportation

*Getting places, reliably and affordably.*

Car maintenance schedule · what dashboard lights mean · tires, fluids, brakes ·
registration and inspection · auto insurance · finding a mechanic · what a repair
should cost · buying and selling a vehicle · alternatives (transit, bike, walking)
· travel logistics · emergency kit

**Domain note:** could be folded into Maintenance, and it's the weakest standalone
domain in the list. Kept separate because a car is a major, high-cost,
high-anxiety asset that generates its own paperwork, insurance, scheduling, and
money questions — and because a large share of the audience's worst financial
surprises come from here.

### 10. Maintenance

*Keeping things working before they break.*

The maintenance calendar · HVAC filters · water heater · smoke and CO detectors ·
gutters, caulk, dryer vent, refrigerator coils, dishwasher filter · seasonal
tasks · appliance care and expected lifespans · basic tools · when to DIY vs.
call someone · finding and evaluating a contractor · warranties · tracking what
was done and when

**Why it's separate from Home:** Home is *setup and daily order*. Maintenance is
*scheduled upkeep on a longer clock*. The mental models genuinely differ — one is
about the state of a room today, the other about the state of a system over years.
This domain is where [preventative-vs-crisis.md](preventative-vs-crisis.md) is
most literally true.

### 11. Paperwork & Admin

*The documents and obligations that prove and enable your life.*

Mail processing · document taxonomy (vital, financial, legal, medical, property) ·
what to keep and how long · physical and digital filing · secure storage for
irreplaceable documents · renewals (license, registration, passport, insurance,
certifications) · handling official correspondence · voting and civic admin ·
wills, beneficiaries, and directives · transition paperwork

**Why "vital documents" is worth its own emphasis:** the small set of
irreplaceable items — birth certificate, Social Security card, passport, titles,
marriage/divorce records — deserves a dedicated, unmissable treatment. Everyone
has them; almost nobody can find them.

### 12. Hospitality

*Opening your home and life to other people.*

Guest-readiness as an ongoing state, not an event · the 48-hour hosting plan ·
repeatable menus · what guests actually need · guest supplies · overnight guests
· handling spontaneous visits · hosting on a budget · hosting in a small space ·
gatherings and traditions · welcoming neighbors and newcomers

**Why it's a domain and not a topic:** it's the **destination** of the whole
curriculum. [`principles.md`](../docs/vision/principles.md) §7 makes the claim
that order exists to make room for people; giving hospitality a full domain is
how the taxonomy backs that claim up structurally.

### 13. Life Transitions

*The moments when everything changes at once.*

Moving · first independent home · marriage and merging households · new job or
relocation · divorce or breakup · death of a spouse or family member · military
deployment and return · illness, injury, or reduced capacity · new baby · caring
for aging parents · job loss · empty nest

**Why it's a domain despite cutting across all others:** transitions are when
systems break, when the need is greatest, and when people are most willing to
learn and to buy. It's the primary sales trigger for the entire business.
Structurally it works as **sequenced pathways through the other twelve domains**
rather than as a body of unique content — a transition guide is a curated route,
not a new subject.

## How domains map to `content/`

The content directory is intentionally **flatter** than the taxonomy — it groups
by where someone would look, not by conceptual purity.

| Domain | Directory |
|---|---|
| Home (cleaning, organization) | `content/home/cleaning/`, `content/home/organization/` |
| Food | `content/home/kitchen/` |
| Hospitality | `content/home/hospitality/` |
| Time | `content/personal/routines/`, `content/personal/time-management/` |
| Digital Life | `content/personal/digital-life/` |
| Paperwork & Admin | `content/personal/personal-admin/` |
| Money | `content/money/` |
| Work | `content/work/` |
| Relationships | `content/relationships/` |
| Life Transitions | `content/life-transitions/` |
| Maintenance | `content/home/` (currently); split out if it grows |
| Transportation | `content/personal/personal-admin/` (currently); split out if it grows |
| Health | Not yet represented — see below |

**Known gaps, deliberately left open:**
- **Health** has no directory. It's the domain with the most scope risk and the
  least MVP relevance. Add one only when there's real content.
- **Maintenance** and **Transportation** are folded in for now. Both are strong
  candidates for promotion once they have more than a handful of files.

Don't create directories ahead of content. Empty structure is a promise the
project hasn't kept.

## Using the taxonomy

- **Tag every piece of content** with a primary domain and up to two secondary.
- **Products should name their domains.** *First Place* spans Home, Food, Money,
  Time, Maintenance, Paperwork, and Hospitality — that breadth is exactly the
  argument for it existing.
- **Coverage is a roadmap tool.** Domains with no content are either gaps or
  deliberate exclusions. Know which.
- **Scope defense.** If something fits no domain, it's probably not our business.

## Open questions

- Is 13 too many for customers? A public-facing version might compress to 5–6
  ("Home, Money, Time, People, Paperwork, Transitions") with the full taxonomy
  kept internal.
- Should Health be in scope at all, given liability and expertise limits?
- Does Transportation earn its place, or should it merge into Maintenance?
- Should Faith/Spiritual Life be a domain? It's central to the founder's
  motivation and to the stewardship framing, but adding it changes what the
  business is and who it's for. **Defer until the positioning question in
  [brand-positioning.md](../docs/business/brand-positioning.md) is settled.**
