# Dave's Principles — the engine

**Status:** Draft, 2026-10-26. Built only from Dave's recovered documents in
[`source-material/`](../source-material/INVENTORY.md). **Not yet reviewed by
Dave** — see [Principles, not a persona](#principles-not-a-persona).

**What it's for:** applying Dave's method to a domain he never wrote about, the
way the [office page](../products/landing/office.html) applied his garage
method to a desk. Walk the five stages, answer each test question for the new
domain, and the steps mostly write themselves.

**What it can't do:** supply facts about a new domain. It gives the *shape* of a
plan. Facts (what an engineer's records rule is, what goes in e-waste, how often
to change a filter) come from outside sources and have to be checked. See
[What the engine can't supply](#what-the-engine-cant-supply).

## The five stages

Dave's documents follow the same order every time: **decide, prepare, reset,
place, maintain.** The garage document runs through stages 1–4. The weekly
schedule is stage 5 and says outright that it only starts after stage 3 is
done. The house-goals document is stage 1 and stage 2 for a bigger project.

### Stage 1 — Decide

**1. Ask whether you actually want it.** — *Solid (G, K)*
> "Do I really want to have a clean and organized garage, and am I willing to work on it till goal reached?" (G)

**Test question for a new domain:** what is the "do I really want this, and will I finish it?" question here?

**2. Start from what the place is for, not from the mess.** — *Single (H)*
> "I want to have a comfortable place to live and show hospitality that is neat, clean and enjoyable to be in." · "What is the focal point of each room?" · "Reduce size of table when no company is around" (H)

Dave's goals are about people and how the space feels to them, not about tidiness.
**Test:** what is this space or system *for*, and who is it for?

**3. Order is planned. It never happens by itself.** — *Solid (W, K)*
> "Your house will not go from chaos to order unless there is some planning and effort to keep it cleaned and organized." (W)

In Dave's text this sits inside a statement of faith ("God is a planner, a God of
order"). The practical half travels to any domain. The theological half stays out
of general products ([ADR-009](../planning/decisions.md#adr-009--faith-framing-convictions-inform-the-work-belief-is-never-required-to-use-it))
and belongs to Charles's stewardship project.

### Stage 2 — Prepare

**4. Close every exit before you start.** — *Solid (G, K)*
> "Make sure that dump is open the day you decide to organize. We don't want any excuses that the dump isn't open." (G) · "Things to purchase before starting" (K)

Already its own framework: [remove-the-excuse.md](remove-the-excuse.md).
**Test:** what will be the first reasonable-sounding reason to stop halfway, and how do you remove it today?

**5. Plan it on paper and measure first.** — *Strong (G, K, H)*
> "You will need to sketch out the wall or walls..." (G) · "Draw a diagram of what your cupboards or kitchen will look like." (K) · "Take measurements of all the rooms" (H)

**Test:** what has to be drawn, measured, or counted before anything is bought or moved?

**6. Bring in competent help, and come prepared for them.** — *Single (H)*
> "I will find 1-3 proficient home decorators... I will have all measurements taken ahead of decorator's arrival." · "Get Dave or someone to sell pub table and chairs" (H)

Dave doesn't expect anyone to do it all alone. He expects them to do their homework
before using someone else's time.
**Test:** which part needs an expert, and what do you hand them so their time isn't wasted?

**7. Count the hidden cost of a trip.** — *Single (K)*
> "Remember that it costs money to drive to the store, as well as your time is worth so much money. So get as big a supply as you can to save trips." (K)

**Test:** what repeated small trip, errand, or interruption could one bigger decision remove?

### Stage 3 — Reset

**8. Reset once, before any routine.** — *Solid (W, G)*
> "Before implementing this schedule you must do an initial cleaning to the standard that you desire... The goal is to get you house, garage, and car the way you want to keep it all the time within a weeks time." (W)

The most important order in the whole method: maintenance is meaningless until
the reset has happened.
**Test:** what does "back to zero" look like here, and can it be reached in a day or a week?

**9. Work in one direction and finish in one session.** — *Single (G, stated twice)*
> "Start at one end of the garage and work toward the other end." · "Your plan is to have this done in one day or less, so start early in the morning." (G)

**Test:** what is the "one end to the other" here, and what is the size of job that fits one session?

**10. Three piles. Nothing goes in a fourth.** — *Solid (G, W)*
> "Pile one... thrown away. Pile two... give to friends, Good Will, Salvation Army. Pile three: These are items that need to be taken into the house and put away." (G) · "need to be thrown out, given away, or find a place to be stored." (W)

**Test:** what are the three piles in this domain? (For the office: throw away, return, stays here.)

**11. The use test.** — *Single (G, stated twice)*
> "Have I used this item in the last year, if not get rid of it. If functional and useful, keep." · "keep asking yourself 'when was the last time I used that'" (G)

**Test:** what is the "used it in the last year?" question here? Is there a different time window that fits?

**12. Be logical and honest about it.** — *Single (G)*
> "this can be emotional for some so you have to be brutal on yourself and be logical and honest with yourself." (G)

**13. Name the excuses before they show up.** — *Strong (G, W, E, K — 2009 to 2023)*
> "I will do it tomorrow" yeah right! Get your ass moving. (G) · "No bleeding heart, glazed eyes excuses that 'I am tired', 'I don't feel like it'..." (W, K) · "I have seen those glazed eyes on so many before." (E)

Dave's most persistent idea after the two rules. **Voice note:** he aims the
bluntness at the excuse, never at the person, and that's how pages should use it.
"You will hear yourself say them" keeps his point without his "get your ass
moving."
**Test:** what will this reader say to themselves to get out of it? List them on the page.

**14. Mark the finish, and leave it ready for next time.** — *Single (G)*
> "Sweep floor, empty trash can. Put unopened trash bag at bottom of trash can, then put trash bag you are going to use over it. Go get a beer." (G)

Two ideas: the job has a clear end you're allowed to enjoy, and the last act sets up
the next reset.
**Test:** what is the finishing ritual here, and what small thing makes next time easier?

### Stage 4 — Place

**15. Everything has its place.** — *Strong (G, W, K, E — the first golden rule)*
> "Memorize the model, Do it now, Everything has a place." (G) · "Do not leave anything laying around the house that you do not intend to use, everything has it's place." (W, repeated daily)

Framework: [everything-has-a-place.md](everything-has-a-place.md).
**Test:** does every item in this domain have one named home?

**16. You can see what you have.** — *Single (G, stated twice)*
> "Storage boxes are to be clear so that you can see all the items in the box from a distance." · "There are to be no items on the shelves that you can't tell their contents from a distance." (G)

**Test:** from across the room (or at a glance), can you tell what's there?

**17. Place by how often it's used and where it's used.** — *Single (G)*
> "least used at the top descending down to the most used items at easy to reach level." · "locations that are functional for that area" · "like things go with like things" (G)

**Test:** what's used daily, what's used yearly, and where does each get used?

**18. Surfaces are for using, not storing.** — *Strong (W, H, K)*
> "The dining room table, coffee tables are not for storage." · "There should never be anything in the sink unless you are washing the dishes" (W) · "I want to have free horizontal spaces" (H)

**Test:** what is the "table" of this domain, the open surface where things pile up? (The desk, an inbox, a desktop screen, a car seat.)

### Stage 5 — Maintain

**19. Do it now.** — *Strong (G, W, E, K — the second golden rule)*
> "Do it now." (W, K) · "Glad you haven't forgotten Do it Now." (E)

**Dave means it literally.** The chores happen "before going to bed," and
"I will do it tomorrow" gets "yeah right!" Our framework
[do-it-now.md](do-it-now.md) softens this to "decide now, do later." That is our
interpretation, not his. See [Gaps](#gaps-and-pushback).

**20. Putting it away now costs the same as later.** — *Single (W)*
> "It takes the same amount of energy to put it in the dishwasher as it does the sink. Same applies to counters." (W)

This is the reasoning behind rule 19: delay doesn't save effort, it just adds a second trip.
**Test:** what is the "sink" here, the in-between place where things wait to be dealt with twice?

**21. Prevent, don't rescue.** — *Solid (W, K)*
> "We are looking for preventative cleaning rather than crisis cleaning." · "By following these rules you will save 50% of your time" (W)

Framework: [preventative-vs-crisis.md](preventative-vs-crisis.md).

**22. A nightly close-out and a weekly list with a hard deadline.** — *Solid (W, K)*
> "All of the following chores are to be done before going to bed." · "Feel free to switch some of the chores to another day... BUT everything on the list has to be done by Sunday evening" (W) · "The car is to be cleaned out every night when coming home. Only items that belong there, or are going to be used the next day can be left." (W)

Flexible days, fixed deadline. Small daily, bigger weekly.
**Test:** what is the ten-minute close-out at the end of each day, and what rotates through the week?

**23. Set a date and have someone check you.** — *Solid (H, K; W's email subject: "will check your place to finish")*
> "Set date when you are going to start this due list and have it finished by. Have someone check you to see if you started and finished on time." (H)

**Test:** what's the finish date, and who checks?

## Using the engine on a new domain

1. **Run the five stages** and answer every test question for the new domain.
2. **Write steps only from answers to those questions.** A step that doesn't come
   from a principle is our idea. That's allowed, but label it as ours in the brief.
3. **List the outside facts the plan depends on** (rules, safety, cadences) and
   check each one against a real source.
4. **Keep the order:** decide, prepare, reset, place, maintain. A page that starts
   with a routine skips the reset, and Dave says that can't work (rule 8).
5. **Mark which rules are "Single"** and get Dave's confirmation before a paid
   product rests on them.

### Worked example: the office page

| Office step | Principle |
|---|---|
| Put two hours on your calendar | 1 (want it), 9 (one session), 4 (close exits) |
| Close the exits | 4, 13 |
| Three piles: throw away, return, stays | 10 |
| Paper: is there another copy? | 11 (use test, adapted), 12 (logical), 19 (do it now) |
| Take the piles away today | 10 (each pile goes where it belongs) |
| Put back in Dave's order | 16, 17 |
| One tray for new paper, then stop | 15, 18, 14 |
| Signup: ten minutes at the end of the day | 22 |
| **Records-retention and e-waste warning** | **None. Outside facts the engine can't supply.** |

## What the engine can't supply

- **Domain facts.** It gives the shape; facts need sources.
- **Content Dave never wrote about.** See the gaps table below.
- **New principles.** Only Dave can add those.

## Principles, not a persona

This is a set of ideas Dave has stated, pulled out so they can be applied to
areas he never wrote about. It is not a model of Dave, and nothing built with it
claims he said anything new. Pages built this way are ours, using his method.

Two working rules follow from that:

- **Task pages don't mention Dave at all** ([content rules](../content/README.md#referring-to-dave)).
  The method shows up as plain instructions. Dave is introduced later, on the
  completion page and in the emails.
- **Wherever we do quote him, use his exact words** from `source-material/`.
- **Keep learning from him directly.** The engine is for the meantime. Every
  conversation with Dave can confirm, correct, or add to the principles here.

## Gaps and pushback

**It's possible, and the office page shows it works,** but only for part of what
Mr. Life Manager wants to cover. In order of importance:

| Gap | Why it matters | What would close it |
|---|---|---|
| **Everything comes from physical spaces.** Garage, kitchen, condo, car. | The engine transfers well to anything with *objects and places*: office, car, storage unit, closet, probably digital files and photos. It transfers poorly to money, paperwork, food, time, and relationships, which are processes, not spaces. | Record Dave on those domains. |
| **Money: Dave's lesson is a warning, not a method.** In 2019 he wrote that he was "sinking financially through foolish choices" and is "sure I can help a lot of foolish young people not to be as stupid as I was." | The money principle he'd teach comes from his own mistakes, which is valuable, but it isn't written down, and it's personal. | Only Dave can say whether and how he wants to tell that story. |
| **Mail and paperwork: the source is lost.** The 2009 schedule says "refer back to earlier papers on mail." Those papers were never found. All we have is "Have mail sent to office" and "find a place in your office for mail." | Paperwork is a top problem for the audience. | Ask Dave; search for the earlier papers. |
| **Food, cooking, groceries:** nothing beyond kitchen cleaning. | Six Meals is ours, not Dave's. | Record Dave, or keep labeling food content as ours. |
| **Cadences beyond cleaning** (filters, car service, dentist): nothing. | "How Often Should I…?" is ours, fact-checked, not Dave's. | Fine as is; keep it labeled. |
| **10 of the 23 principles rest on a single document**, mostly the garage. | Some may be how Dave thinks; some may be what one garage needed. | Read each "Single" rule to Dave and ask "is that always true?" |
| **Our "Do it now" framework softens Dave.** He means literally now, before bed. We wrote "decide now, do later." | The engine and the framework currently disagree. | Decide which one we teach, or present Dave's version first and ours as the adaptation for people with no margin. |
| **Voice.** Dave is blunter than the house style ("Get your ass moving," "be brutal on yourself"). | Strip it out entirely and the pages stop sounding like him. | Keep his bluntness aimed at excuses, never at the person (rule 13). |
| **The reasoning is often missing.** Dave states rules, rarely why. Rule 20 is a rare exception. | The "why" is what makes a principle transfer to a new domain. | The Dave session: ask "why that way?" for each rule. |

**The best use of this list right now:** take it to the
[Dave session](../source-material/interviews/dave-session-guide.md). Read him
each principle and ask two questions: *"Is that right?"* and *"Where doesn't it
apply?"* That turns a vague interview into a concrete one, and it's exactly the
kind of question his
[capture guidance](../source-material/original-charles-cleanup-notes.md#capturing-daves-material-read-this-first)
says works: reacting to something specific instead of explaining what he thinks
is obvious.
