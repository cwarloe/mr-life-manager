# Bronco feedback docs

This note covers [competitor-scan.md](../../business/competitor-scan.md) and [willingness-to-pay-plan.md](../../business/willingness-to-pay-plan.md). The customer profile has its own note.

What already works well: the competitor scan is unusually careful. Prices are dated, sources are named, missing prices are marked "not found," and the conclusion is modest: the gap is real and also small, and the honest claim is the combination and the voice, not a new category. The willingness-to-pay plan labels every outside contact as needing Charles's OK, refuses fake scarcity, and sets success thresholds before any results come in.

## 1. "Prices seen are low: about $1 to $20" is broader than the table

[competitor-scan.md](../../business/competitor-scan.md), lines 65 to 67:

> People pay for this subject. Books (*Adulting*, *Decluttering at the Speed of Life*, *Unf*ck Your Habitat*) and Etsy printables show buyers exist. Prices seen are low: about $1 to $20.

The same table lists Clutterbug courses at $30, $94, and $99 or $149, and it earlier notes that household-systems brands "sell well." The low band is true of books, apps, and printables. The sentence reads as if the whole category is a $20 market.

Suggested rewrite:

> People pay for this subject. Books and Etsy printables show buyers exist, usually at about $1 to $20. Longer courses in the same neighborhood sell at higher prices, but the "adulting" courses aimed at 18 to 25 year olds show very small numbers.

## 2. This plan and the week-1 interview pack do not describe the same study

[willingness-to-pay-plan.md](../../business/willingness-to-pay-plan.md), lines 16 to 18, wants 3 to 5 people aged 18 to 25 who moved out in the last 12 months, and 3 to 5 parents, in about three weeks. [interview-pack-week1.md](../../research/interviews/interview-pack-week1.md), lines 7 and 14 to 15, wants 5 conversations this week toward 15 to 20, sampled across recent movers, newlyweds, recent grads, overwhelmed householders, and parents.

Both cannot be "the" interview plan. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 11. I am not rewriting either sample.

## 3. The reserve-page threshold may be hard to read without traffic numbers

Lines 84 to 85 count the reserve page as evidence if at least 5% of parents-page visitors who see the offer reserve one, with at least 20 reservations total. The rule of not moving the line afterward is good. The doc never says whether the parents page currently has enough visitors for 20 reservations to be a three-week test. If traffic is small, a miss would say more about reach than about willingness to pay.

This is a design question for the test, not a rewrite of the offer. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 12.

## 4. Two app prices rest on weaker sources than the rest of the scan

The Sweepy price, line 53, comes from a third-party comparison site. The Home Routines price, line 54, is "as summarized by search" and already asks the reader to confirm it on the page. The rest of the table is strong enough that these two stand out.

Suggested rewrite of the Home Routines price cell:

> Price not confirmed on the App Store page in this pass. A search summary on 2026-11-05 said free, then $1.99 a month or $19.99 a year. Treat as unverified until someone opens the listing.
