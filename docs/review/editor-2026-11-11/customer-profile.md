# Customer profile

What already works well: this is one of the most careful docs in the repo. Every line is labeled evidence or assumption, it says plainly that no customer interviews have been done, and it refuses to treat the parent as the decided buyer while ADR-010 is on hold. The gift-framing rule, that copy must never describe the student as a problem, is clear and reusable.

## 1. This file and the vision persona file use the same name for two different people

[customer-profile.md](../../business/customer-profile.md), lines 25 to 31, introduces "Tyler, the student or first-time renter," about 18 to 25, limited budget, "mostly nothing" as a buyer. [target-audiences.md](../../vision/target-audiences.md), lines 59 to 93, already has a Tyler who is 23, has a first real job, and is "the best MVP customer," likely to buy a comprehensive guide at $29 to $79.

A reader who starts with the vision set and then opens this file will think the business changed its mind about Tyler without saying so. This is a decision about who the customer is. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 2. I am not rewriting the profile.

## 2. "The three moment hooks" will not mean anything to an outsider

Line 53:

> Short videos on Reels, Shorts and TikTok, using the three moment hooks

Nothing in this file, or in the pages it points to, names those three hooks. A reviewer or a judge reading only this memo has to guess.

Suggested rewrite:

> Short videos on Reels, Shorts, and TikTok, each built around one moment the checklists already name, such as people coming over, a place that has gotten messy, or a covered table.

## 3. The "Tyler is a weak buyer" claim is stronger than the evidence under it

Lines 42 to 46 say Tyler "is the best user and probably a weak direct buyer," then mark that as evidence from Society of Grownups and small "adulting" course numbers, with an assumption for Tyler himself. The competitor scan supports a cautious claim about selling courses to 18 to 25 year olds. It does not show that this Tyler will not pay for a short, concrete guide, and the file already notes that books on the same subject do sell.

Suggested rewrite of the evidence clause, keeping the assumption:

> Tyler gets the free checklist. The value to Tyler is "I knew what to do first, and I did it." Whether Tyler will pay for a later guide is still an assumption. The competitor scan shows that adulting courses sold poorly to this age group, and that a well-funded storefront closed. That is evidence about courses and classes, not about this checklist or a short paid guide.
