# Audience pages

This note covers [products/landing/audiences/README.md](../../../products/landing/audiences/README.md) and the live pages [career.html](../../../products/landing/audiences/career.html), [students.html](../../../products/landing/audiences/students.html), and [military.html](../../../products/landing/audiences/military.html).

What already works well: the README is a clear builder's guide, the pages share one set of checklists, and the color schemes avoid school or service-branch names. ADR-023, that these pages are for a first stretch with nobody else running things, is stated plainly. The student underwater door is already a little plainer than the 31 October table, and that wording should be kept.

## 1. Career and student door 4 send "living out of boxes" to the first-night checklist

[career.html](../../../products/landing/audiences/career.html), lines 52 to 55:

> You moved for the job, and you're still sleeping between boxes.
> Get a made bed, shower things out, somewhere to sit and eat, and move-in photos before you unpack anything else.

[students.html](../../../products/landing/audiences/students.html), lines 52 to 55:

> You just got your first keys, and you're living out of boxes.
> Get a made bed, shower stuff out, somewhere to sit and eat, and move-in photos for your deposit.

Both links go to `/first-night.html`. That checklist is for the night someone moves in. It is not a job for unpacking after weeks of living out of boxes. A reader who recognizes their own messy room of boxes will start the wrong steps, including landlord photos and shutoff checks they may have already done, or skipped on purpose.

The 1 November draft already splits this into a still-unpacking workflow, with first night only as a move-in season swap. Which door should be live is a product decision. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 18. I am not rewriting the door here.

The student title also names two different moments in one line: just getting the keys, and already living out of boxes. Even if first night stays the door, those should not be joined.

## 2. The headlines still use the clipped speech the later copy was written to fix

[career.html](../../../products/landing/audiences/career.html), line 30:

> Too tired to deal with your place tonight? Pick the night you will.

"The night you will" has no object. The lede on line 31 says "pick a time and it goes on your calendar in three taps." "A time" is not tied to a job, and "it" has no clear referent.

The students and military ledes use the same pattern, with "if you're in class" or "if you're on duty." The 1 November workflow screens already say "pick the day and time you'll do the checklist."

Suggested rewrite of the career headline and lede, if this page keeps a start-later promise:

> Too tired to deal with your place tonight? Pick the night you will deal with it.

> Tap the one that is true and start now. If you are not home, pick the day and time you will do that checklist, and it will go on your calendar in three taps.

The same lede shape can be reused on the other two pages, with "in class" or "on duty" in place of "not home."

## 3. "Ready for people rather than a magazine" is unclear, and a little sharp

[career.html](../../../products/landing/audiences/career.html), line 49:

> You'll do the room you'll sit in, one bathroom, the sink and the trash, so it's ready for people rather than a magazine.

This is the same line as in the 31 October copy-final file. A first-time reader has to invent what a magazine has to do with a visit. It can also sound like a jab at a styled home.

Suggested rewrite:

> You'll do the room you'll sit in, one bathroom, the sink, and the trash, so you can have people over.

## 4. "The place has slid" is insider wording

[military.html](../../../products/landing/audiences/military.html), line 44:

> There's no inspection on the calendar, and the place has slid.

"Slid" may be clear to some readers and opaque to others, including a partner or a parent who opens the same link. The 1 November military box is plainer: "Nobody inspects your place anymore, and it's gotten messy." That line is already in the workflow file as a question for reviewers, because it may be too blunt. Either version is clearer than "has slid."

If the 1 November line is approved, use it here. If it feels too blunt, a middle version is:

> Nobody inspects your place anymore, and it has gotten messy.

## 5. The footer says "All checklists," which teaches a menu

All three pages, in the footer, link home with the words "All checklists." The principles and the offer filter say to offer one thing at a time, and that a menu asks someone to evaluate products. These pages already did the choosing work with four doors. A footer that promises all the checklists points the other way.

Suggested rewrite of that footer link:

> Back to the main page

## 6. The seasonal swap for first night was written down and not built

[copy-final-2026-10-31.md](../../research/copy-final-2026-10-31.md), lines 68 to 69, says that in August, January, and the first two weeks of any term, student door 4 should move to the top. The student page is static. Nothing in the HTML implements that swap. If first night remains a year-round door, this still needs a yes or no. If door 4 becomes still-unpacking, the seasonal swap is part of the same decision. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 18.

Military door 4, "The spare room is still full of boxes from the move," at least points at the one-room checklist, and the supporting line already says "Clear one zone." That pairing is closer to honest than the student and career door 4. Whether military should also get a still-unpacking door is a separate question. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 19.
