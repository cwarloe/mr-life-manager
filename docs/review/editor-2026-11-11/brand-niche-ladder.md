# Brand positioning, niche strategy, and product ladder

What already works well: [brand-positioning.md](../../business/brand-positioning.md) is sharp about what this is not, and the faith-framing section is clear enough to write from. [product-ladder.md](../../business/product-ladder.md) correctly records that Kitchen That Works is free and folded into Six Meals and a Stocked Kitchen, and that Laundry, Solved and the roommate kit are free only. The "nothing sold twice" and "win before wallet" rules are easy to apply.

## 1. The ladder's list of entry pages does not match the live site

[product-ladder.md](../../business/product-ladder.md), line 43:

> **Entry pages** — first night, guests, one room, underwater, office (added 2026-10-06; deliberate one-off per Charles 2026-10-08 — direct-link only, not in homepage/parents/partners pickers; MailerLite group not wired yet)

The five live checklists are first night, guests, dinner, underwater, and one room. Dinner is missing from this list. Office is a direct-link one-off, which the note already says, but listing it here in place of dinner makes a reader think office is a public door and dinner is not. The same omission is repeated at lines 186 to 189.

Suggested rewrite of the product cell on line 43:

> Entry pages: first night, guests, dinner, underwater, and one room. Office was added on 2026-10-06 as a direct-link one-off. It is not in the homepage, parents, or partners pickers, and its MailerLite group is not wired yet.

And in the build-order list, name dinner with the other four public doors, then mention office separately as the one-off.

## 2. A leftover sentence makes the flagship success line hard to read

Lines 120 to 123:

> **Success looks like:** the flagship is used — the house actually gets in order — and completion/outcome reports are strong enough to produce real testimonials, without discounting. Until then, nothing is sold that hasn't been earned.
> completion/outcome reports are strong enough to produce real testimonials.

The last line is a fragment left over from an earlier edit.

Suggested rewrite:

> Success looks like this: the flagship is used, the house actually gets in order, and the completion and outcome reports are strong enough to produce real testimonials, without discounting. Until then, nothing is sold that has not been earned.

## 3. The historical First Place price can still be read as current

Lines 206 to 209 label the 8 September 2026 pricing table as a historical record, and say the flagship priced there no longer exists. Lines 221 and 233 still discuss "$39 for *First Place*" in the present tense inside that table and in the commentary under it. A reader skimming the end of the ladder can miss the historical label and think First Place at $39 is still the plan. The earlier flagship section, lines 97 to 106, correctly says price, format, and name are not set.

I am not suggesting a price or a name. A one-line reminder at the top of that table would prevent the misreading:

> This table is evidence only. Do not take the $39 First Place row, or the mid-tier prices below it, as the current offer.

Whether those mid-tier prices still stand is a decision. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), questions 8 and 9.

## 5. "Thirteen domains" will puzzle a reader who only knows the live site

[brand-positioning.md](../../business/brand-positioning.md), line 247:

> A life that works; cleaning is one domain of thirteen

The live offer is five household checklists. The thirteen domains live in the frameworks, which a new reader of this doc may not have opened. As a positioning claim, "thirteen domains" sounds like a product that already exists.

Suggested rewrite of that cell:

> A life that works. Cleaning is the entry point, not the whole curriculum. The wider set of life domains is in the frameworks, and most of those are not products yet.

## 6. The niche strategy names products that are not on the ladder

[niche-strategy.md](../../business/niche-strategy.md), lines 78 to 82, offers *The Floor*, *The Reset*, and *Travel Mode* as containers for low-capacity readers, and the house-in-order flagship for high-capacity readers, and says this is still to be decided. None of those three names appear on the current ladder or the live site.

This is a product-scope decision. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 13.

## 7. The recommended taglines do not match the short mission

[brand-positioning.md](../../business/brand-positioning.md), lines 231 to 237, recommends "The life skills nobody thought to teach you" as the primary tagline, and "Practical systems for everyday adulthood" as the descriptor. [mission.md](../../vision/mission.md), line 76, gives the public short form as "Practical systems for running an adult life." Those are close, and close is enough to confuse anyone writing a homepage.

If the mission short form is adopted, the descriptor should use the same sentence. If the brand doc's wording is preferred, the mission short form should change to match. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 1, which already asks which mission to adopt. A follow-up is only needed if that short form stays in play.
