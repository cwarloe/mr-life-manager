# Vision

What already works well: [principles.md](../../vision/principles.md) is the strongest document in this set. The convictions are specific, the tests are usable, and the no-shame voice is already in the writing. [customer-outcomes.md](../../vision/customer-outcomes.md) is honest that every quote is invented, and [vivid-vision-2029.md](../../vision/vivid-vision-2029.md) keeps Charles's words and marks the filled-in lines clearly.

## 1. Two mission statements are still on the page, and neither is marked as adopted

In [mission.md](../../vision/mission.md), lines 68 to 76 recommend candidate 6, which names "first-time independent adults." Lines 88 to 99 then offer a shorter version that drops that phrase, and lines 108 to 116 still ask whether the shorter version should replace the recommendation and say the adopted mission should be recorded as a decision.

A new reader cannot tell which sentence is the mission. This is a decision, not a wording tweak. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 1.

## 2. The audience documents describe different businesses

[target-audiences.md](../../vision/target-audiences.md) is built around five personas: Maya, Tyler, Josh and Anna, Dana, and Ray. The recommendation at lines 242 to 245 is to build the MVP for Tyler, with copy Maya would share and Josh and Anna would buy.

The live site, the 1 November flier copy, and ADR-023 point at three other groups: students, early-career workers, and people in their first place off base. Newlyweds do not have a page. Tyler in this file is 23, has a first real job, and is "the best MVP customer" who would buy at $29 to $79 (lines 88 to 93). That is not the Tyler in the November customer profile. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 2.

Until that is decided, this file should open with a dated note that the personas have not been reconciled with the live audience pages, so nobody treats the MVP recommendation as current.

## 3. Several product names and prices in the personas are out of date

[target-audiences.md](../../vision/target-audiences.md), lines 39 to 43, still send Maya to a "First Apartment Checklist" as the "highest-fit free guide" and then to a low-cost $9 to $19 guide after a free win. Lines 82 to 86 send Tyler to "the house-in-order system (flagship, ADR-020)" plus a paperwork toolkit and a money module. Lines 122 to 128 give Josh and Anna a Household Operating Agreement, a hospitality toolkit, and a 30-day Home Reset.

Kitchen That Works is correctly absent. The trouble is that a reader of the vision set still meets a catalog of products and price bands that the later ladder marks as unset, free-only, or not re-decided. The willingness-to-pay figures are labeled as estimates, which is good. They still read like a plan.

Suggested opening line under "Likely products," used once and reused for each persona:

> These product names are illustrative. What is actually offered today is listed in the product ladder, and several paid items there are not yet decided.

Do not treat the dollar amounts as current. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), questions 7 and 8.

## 4. The outcome ladder and the mission do not point at the same prize

[customer-outcomes.md](../../vision/customer-outcomes.md), lines 167 to 174, puts "Attention for what matters" at the top and says "Marketing sells rung 1. The product must deliver rung 5." [principles.md](../../vision/principles.md), lines 126 to 141, and the recommended mission both say hospitality is the destination: "Order is not the point. Order makes room for people."

[vivid-vision-2029.md](../../vision/vivid-vision-2029.md) already asks Charles, at lines 163 to 166, whether having people over is part of the 2029 picture, because his answers did not mention it. The tension is real. The vision doc already has the right question, so I am not rewriting the ladder here. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 16.

## 5. The mission still leans on an ADR that is on hold

[mission.md](../../vision/mission.md), lines 88 to 93, explains the shorter mission by citing ADR-010, which separated who the content serves from who is asked to pay. That ADR is under review. The shorter mission may still be the right sentence. The citation makes it sound settled.

Suggested rewrite of the adjustment header and first sentence, if the shorter mission is kept as a candidate rather than as a decision:

> Adjustment, 2026-09-08, still open. A later note separated who the content serves from who is asked to pay. That question is on hold. The shorter mission below drops the audience phrase so it does not get read as naming the buyer.

## 6. A few lines in the vivid vision will confuse someone new

[vivid-vision-2029.md](../../vision/vivid-vision-2029.md), line 26, says "This was never about making money." The business-model and product-ladder docs are a careful plan for how the work does make money, including coaching and licensing. Those can both be true if the vision means "profit is not the purpose." As written, it sounds like a refusal of the rest of the docs.

Suggested rewrite of that sentence:

> Making money is not why this exists. It exists because a friend named Dave once helped me make progress when I could not make it on my own, and I still have progress to make. This is me passing on what he gave me.

Lines 86, "The checklists are free, and they always will be," match the current free-forever rule. Keep that. The open questions at the end of the vision are the right ones. I have not repeated them as new wording tasks.
