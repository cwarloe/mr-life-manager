## 2. Door 4 on the career and student tables promises unpacking, and sends people to the first-night checklist

Lines 37 and 66:

> You moved for the job, and you're still sleeping between boxes.

> You just got your first keys, and you're living out of boxes.

Both doors are the first-night checklist, timed at about 90 minutes. That checklist is for the night someone moves in: photos, a made bed, bathroom things, shutoffs, a note to the landlord, and one place to sit and eat. It is not an unpacking job. Someone who has been "sleeping between boxes" for weeks will get the wrong steps.

The 1 November draft already splits this into still-unpacking for the later moment, with first night only as a move-in season swap. Which door should ship is a product decision. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 18. I am not rewriting the door here.

## 4. "Ready for people rather than a magazine" will not land cleanly

Line 36, career guests description:

> You'll do the room you'll sit in, one bathroom, the sink and the trash, so it's ready for people rather than a magazine.

A smart outsider has to guess what "a magazine" is doing in that sentence. It is trying to say the place should be good enough for company, not styled for a photo. It also brushes against the no-shame rule, because it can be read as a jab at glossy homes.

Suggested rewrite, if this description is kept until the workflow copy replaces it:

> You'll do the room you'll sit in, one bathroom, the sink, and the trash, so you can have people over.

The career H1 on line 28, "Too tired to deal with your place tonight? Pick the night you will," has the same missing object. A complete version is "Pick the night you will deal with it." The 1 November career flier already leads with "Working long hours and living on your own for the first time?" and does not need this headline.
