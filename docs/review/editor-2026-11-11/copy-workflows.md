# Copy workflows

This note covers [workflow-wording.md](../../copy/workflow-wording.md) and the six workflow files in [docs/copy/workflows/](../../copy/workflows/).

What already works well: this is the best copy in the set for the house rule. Every verb has its object in most of the screens, each workflow can be read start to finish, and the reviewer questions are specific. Leaving the defensive time disclaimers out, as Charles asked, is the right call. The 1 November draft also correctly splits first night from "still living out of boxes," which the live audience pages still mix together.

## 1. Dinner step 2 tells the reader to clean up after a meal that has not happened yet

In [03-dinner.md](../../copy/workflows/03-dinner.md), line 129, during a checklist whose job is to clear the table so someone can eat:

> Put the dishes in the dishwasher or next to the sink. Clean up the kitchen after dinner. Then come back to the table.

The reader is in the middle of making a place to eat. "After dinner" and "come back to the table" sound like the meal is over, or like they should leave the table job and clean the kitchen first. Line 131 already says tonight's job is making a place to eat, and that washing every dish would turn this into a different job.

Suggested rewrite of line 129:

> Take the dishes off the table and put them in the dishwasher or next to the sink. Leave washing them for later. Then come back to the table.

## 2. First-night scheduling still talks about "tonight" after the reader picks Saturday morning

In [04-first-night.md](../../copy/workflows/04-first-night.md), lines 70 to 80, the time choices include "Saturday morning, 10:00 a.m." and "Monday evening, 7:00 p.m." After the reader picks, the page says:

> That's when you'll get your new place set up for tonight.

The calendar title on line 88 is "Get my new place set up for tonight." If they picked Saturday morning, "tonight" is the wrong word, and this workflow is meant for the actual first night. The later-visit problem that the guests file already flags is even sharper here.

Suggested rewrite of the confirmation line and the calendar title, used whenever the picked time is not later today:

> That's when you'll get your new place set up for your first night.

Calendar title: "Get my new place set up for the first night"

If the whole scheduling path is a poor fit for a "first night" task, that is a product decision. See [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 12.

## 3. The military first-night box assumes household goods have not arrived, and the checklist assumes a bed and sheets

[04-first-night.md](../../copy/workflows/04-first-night.md), line 17:

> You just moved into your first place off base, and your household goods haven't arrived yet.

Step 2, lines 132 to 136, starts with "Find your sheets, put them on," and the "before you start" list on line 124 includes bedding and a bed or mattress that is already put together. The landing-page note on line 62 does say it takes longer if something is missing, and that packing essentials in one box helps if they have not moved in yet. The flier line itself never says that getting missing things is part of the job.

Suggested rewrite of the flier box, if the checklist stays as it is:

> You just moved into your first place off base, and your household goods may not have arrived yet.

And add this under "Before you start," which the live military door already promises:

> If your bed, sheets, or bathroom things are not here yet, getting what you need for tonight is part of this job.

## 4. The career dishes-and-mess box does not mention clothes, and the checklist does

[01-underwater.md](../../copy/workflows/01-underwater.md), line 18, career box:

> You keep getting home late, and the dishes and mail keep piling up.

Step 4 is still "Put your dirty clothes in the hamper." The student box names dishes, clothes, and mail. The career box names dishes and mail only, so someone who scanned it can feel mid-checklist that the job grew.

Suggested rewrite of the career box:

> You keep getting home late, and the dishes, the clothes, and the mail keep piling up.

## 5. The spare-room flier promises a room full of boxes, and the checklist only clears one part

[05-one-room.md](../../copy/workflows/05-one-room.md), line 17:

> Your spare room is still full of boxes from the move.

The time on the same page is "about three hours." The landing heading for people who arrive without a flier, line 34, is already more honest: "There's a room in your place that you can't use anymore." The checklist, and the social launch kit, both warn not to promise an entire cleared room. The military flier still does.

Suggested rewrite of the military box:

> Part of your spare room is still full of boxes from the move, and you cannot use that space.

Keep "about three hours" only if a timed pass supports it. The file already asks reviewers to time it.

## 6. Still-unpacking says the whole room is unpacked, and also allows leftover boxes

In [06-still-unpacking.md](../../copy/workflows/06-still-unpacking.md), step 5, lines 149 to 153, tells the reader to label boxes they are not opening today and stack them against a wall. The why line even says "You don't have to unpack everything in one day." The finish text, line 187, then says "This room is unpacked and ready to use."

Those cannot both be true if boxes remain.

Suggested rewrite of the first sentence of the finish text:

> This room is ready to use. Everything you unpacked has a place, the empty boxes are out of the house, and the boxes you did not open are labeled and out of the way.

## 7. A reviewer question in the dinner file points at wording that is not in this draft

[03-dinner.md](../../copy/workflows/03-dinner.md), line 201:

> The page tells the reader to schedule a time "before your next meal at home."

That sentence is not in this workflow file. It is in the 31 October copy-final file, and it may still be on the live dinner page. A reviewer who only reads this document will go looking for a line that is not here.

Suggested rewrite of that reviewer question:

> The live page, and the 31 October copy, tell the reader to pick a time "before your next meal at home." This draft's scheduling screen does not use that line. Does a 10-minute job even need scheduling, or is "start now" the real action?

Whether dinner should have a scheduling path at all is already asked in the file. If that is also a product decision, see [needs-charles.md](needs-charles.md), question 13.

## 8. "Scan a QR code below and take action" is stiffer than the rest of the flier

The shared flier body, in [workflow-wording.md](../../copy/workflow-wording.md), line 34, and in each workflow file, ends the middle block with "Scan a QR code below and take action." "Take action" is the kind of line the plain-speech rule was written to catch. The rest of that block is already plain: a free checklist, no app, no email.

Suggested rewrite:

> Scan one of the codes below and start the checklist, or pick the time you will do it.
