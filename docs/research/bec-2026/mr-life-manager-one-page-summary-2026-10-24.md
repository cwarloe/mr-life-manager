# Mr. Life Manager

Practical systems for running a home. The life skills nobody thought to teach you.

Charles Warloe, Boise State University | mrlifemanager.com | Bronco Entrepreneur Challenge 2026, Early Stage Track

**For** capable adults running a home they were never taught how to run, starting with first-time independent adults (students moving off campus, new grads in a first apartment) at the moment of that move,

**who** re-decide where everything lives every day, clean only in a crisis, and scroll tips or save checklists they never finish,

**the** Mr. Life Manager **is a** free, task-first web guide (one page, one job, done today), with deeper guides sold only afterward,

**that** gets a specific job finished today: "Someone's coming over" is six steps in order, about 35 minutes, and still works with 10.

**Unlike** cleaning creators, organizing courses and books, "adulting" listicles, and Etsy binders of blank forms,

**our** method is one taught system for the whole home, delivered one finished task at a time, from a real person's tested method, and we sell only after you've had a free win.

## Customers

First-time independent adults (students, new grads), newlywed couples merging two households, and overwhelmed professionals. The common trigger is a transition: nobody buys this on an ordinary Tuesday. Payers can be that adult, a parent buying a gift, or an institution such as campus housing or a church program.

## Problem

Most household disorder isn't a cleaning problem. It's an undefined-home problem: things with no assigned place get re-decided every time they're set down. The market is full of information, but people don't finish it. Someone holding six undone checklists has made no progress.

## Solution

Each entry page asks "what's going on right now?" and hands over one action. There's no sign-up and no download, and the steps come in an order where stopping early is still worthwhile. "There's a room you stopped using" is one zone, about two hours, actually finished. The method comes from Dave, who taught the founder to run a house and has taught it for decades. The founder is the apprentice who wrote it down.

## Business model

(1) Free single-issue wins, free forever. (2) Deeper $9 to $19 guides per domain, offered only after a free win. (3) A flagship house-in-order system covering cleaning, organizing, dishes, and laundry. Later: coaching and institutional licensing.

## Why now and proof of gap

Three independent research passes found nobody selling a taught, multi-domain home system directly to the adult who needs it; every commercial success is single-domain. Etsy home-management binder shops show demand for multi-domain help (38.7k, 8.6k, and 5.2k reviews at $3 to $17), but they sell blank forms with no instruction.

## Progress so far

mrlifemanager.com is live with five free, ungated task pages (first night, guests, one room, underwater, office), each with a phone-friendly "doing mode," plus nine printable PDF guides. Email follow-up starts only after the win, completion is tracked through dedicated finish pages, and a feedback survey is live. A real request for help with an office cleanup became a live page in 30 minutes.

## Next step

Customer discovery with first-time independent adults to measure completion and validate the first paid $9 to $19 guide.
