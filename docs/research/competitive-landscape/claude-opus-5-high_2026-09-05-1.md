## 2. Home organization and decluttering

### Clutterbug (Cassandra Aarssen)
Source: https://clutterbug.me/courses · https://clutterbug.thinkific.com/ · https://course.clutterbug.me/courses/Clutterbug-Method — checked Sept 5, 2026

- **OBSERVED — the full ladder.** Free Organizing Masterclass ("no strings, no annoying upsells") → **30-Day Organizing Bootcamp $30** (one-time; one year of access; "$1 a day"; money-back guarantee) → **The Clutterbug Method $94 one-time or 3×$33** → **Become an Organizing Expert**, listed at **$99**, **$149**, and **3×$49.99** across different pages, including a printable "Certified Organizational Specialist™" certificate and a private Facebook group.
- **OBSERVED — positioning, verbatim.** "No shame. No perfection. No more trying to use a system that wasn't designed for you." The method is built on four organizing "styles," diagnosed to the individual.
- **OBSERVED — credibility markers on her own pages.** HGTV show *Hot Mess House*; a self-described #1 Apple Podcast in Home & Garden; four books; ADHD-friendly strategies named explicitly.
- **OBSERVED — business model.** Actual products, tiered, with a free-masterclass funnel at the top and a paid certification at the bottom. Media (TV, podcast, YouTube, books) feeds the funnel.
- **Volume signal: not found.** No enrollment counts or aggregate review totals displayed.
- **OBSERVED — what buyers say.** A testimonial on the course page emphasizes reasonable price and re-watchability: the course could be revisited to refresh technique.
- **INFERRED.** This is the closest working template to the business described. The mechanism is that the framework hands the buyer an *identity* ("I'm a Bee / Cricket / Ladybug / Butterfly") rather than a chore list — which converts a shame problem into a fit problem.

### The Home Edit (Clea Shearer, Joanna Teplin)
Source: https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/much-does-cost-home-edit-185939102.html · https://rottentomatoes.com/tv/get_organized_with_the_home_edit/s01 · https://variety.com/2020/tv/reviews/the-home-edit-netflix-1234741722 · https://clearyourlife.ca/the-home-edit/ — checked Sept 5, 2026

- **OBSERVED — prices.** In-person organizing **$185–$250/hour** by city (NY, Detroit, San Francisco, Nashville, Salt Lake City). Clients supply all containers and hooks and cover team travel and lodging. Remote: **$250** one-hour video consult, **$350** email package + shopping list, **$525** for three hours of mentorship, **$650** for a start-to-finish space consult.
- **OBSERVED — model.** Services + The Container Store product line + bestselling books + Netflix series + own online store. Both media-supported and product-supported.
- **OBSERVED — volume signal.** Rotten Tomatoes Season 1: **88% Tomatometer (8 reviews) vs. 17% audience score, fewer than 50 ratings.** Season 2: 0% Tomatometer (1 review), fewer than 50 audience ratings.
- **OBSERVED — complaints, verbatim from a Rotten Tomatoes audience review:** "Instead if organize and tidy they sell you expensive items to organize."
- **OBSERVED — professional critique.** An organizer's takedown states the core objection directly: "no amount of containers will solve a 'too much stuff' problem," and objects to the volume of plastic brought to each site.
- **OBSERVED — press critique.** *Variety* characterized the show as fetishistic maximalism and the polar opposite of Marie Kondo's shedding ethos, noting the systems are "cumbersome and time-consuming."
- **OBSERVED — the structural complaint.** A practitioner reviewer notes homeowners were rarely involved in creating the systems they were then expected to maintain.
- **INFERRED.** The **88% critic / 17% audience split is the single most decision-relevant number in this report.** Aspiration-and-aesthetics organizing performs for media buyers and fails the people who have to live in the result. That gap is your opening.

## 4. Personal finance education for young adults — BUSINESS MODEL STUDY

### Her First $100K (Tori Dunlap)
Source: https://herfirst100k.com/stock-market-school · https://www.geekwire.com/2022/her-first-100k-founder-has-her-first-app-tori-dunlap-wants-to-teach-more-women-how-to-invest/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tori_Dunlap · https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/financial-feminist/id1566054936 — checked Sept 5, 2026

- **OBSERVED.** 2.2M Instagram, 2.4M TikTok. **Grossed more than $3.4M in 2021** (her own statement, via NYT). Twelve employees as of 2021. Sponsored videos earned **$15,000 each** in 2021.
- **OBSERVED — revenue sources named:** speaking engagements, social media, and financial coaching. Plus webinars and programs.
- **OBSERVED — product ladder.** **$99** introductory Investing 101 workshop (~1 hour: 30–45 min guidance, live first investment, Q&A) → limited access to the Treasury interface, community, and educational resources → **Stock Market School** subscription.
- **OBSERVED — critical structural disclosure.** Her First $100K and Treasury are **separate companies**. HF$100K is "an advisory client of Treasury Interactive Investment Advisers LLC," and **Her First $100K pays Treasury** to provide financial content and non-discretionary advisory services. If the subscription lapses, the user keeps the Treasury investing account but loses the educational content.
- **OBSERVED — complaint, verbatim from an Apple Podcasts review titled "Be Choosier, Tori":** "Please be more selective about what sponsors you accept and what guests you have on."
- **Volume signal:** follower counts and one self-reported revenue figure **observed**; course/workshop enrollment counts **not found**.
- **INFERRED.** The revenue is majority **sponsorship and speaking**, not product. The $99 workshop is a funnel into a partner's financial product. This is a media business with a course attached, not a course business. Anyone modeling on it should model the audience-building, not the pricing.

## 6. Institutional life-skills programs

### DoD Transition Assistance Program (TAP)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_Assistance_Program · https://home.army.mil/okinawa/about/Garrison/DHR/army-tap · https://discover.va.gov/transition-programs/transition-assistance-program/ · https://myairforcebenefits.us.af.mil/Benefit-Library/Federal-Benefits/DAF-Transition-Assistance-Program-(TAP) — checked Sept 5, 2026

- **OBSERVED.** Statutory program under 10 USC Ch. 58. **Mandatory** for all service members with 180+ continuous days of active duty, **including National Guard and Reserve.**
- **OBSERVED — timing.** Must begin no later than **365 days before separation**; two years recommended for retirees.
- **OBSERVED — structure.** (1) Individualized Initial Counseling — mandatory, no exemptions, produces an Individual Transition Plan. (2) Pre-Separation Counseling. (3) Three-day workshop: **Day 1 (DoD)** — Managing Your Transition, Military Occupational Code Crosswalk, and Financial Planning for Transition, which satisfies the mandated financial literacy requirement; **Day 2** — VA Benefits and Services; **Day 3** — DOL Employment Fundamentals of Career Transition. (4) Post-assessment and Capstone, verifying Career Readiness Standards.
- **OBSERVED — scale.** About **200,000 service members transition to civilian life each year.**
- **OBSERVED.** Available in person, online, and virtually. Spouses can access TAP resources. Centrally funded commander's program.
- **INFERRED — the gap.** TAP covers career, benefits, and money. It covers **nothing** about running a household: home, food, maintenance, paperwork, hospitality. That is a specific, named, uncovered domain inside a mandatory program with a captive audience of 200,000 per year.

# THE FOUR QUESTIONS

## Q1. Does anyone teach practical systems across MULTIPLE life domains?

**A few produce multi-domain content, but nobody teaches a multi-domain system to independent adults.** Multi-domain material exists in exactly four places, each failing the spec in a specific way:

1. **Books.** *The Financial Diet* covers money, kitchen, closet, and "how to take care of your house like a grown-up" in one volume (OBSERVED). It is a one-shot artifact, not a system, and the author has since moved toward small-business finance.
2. **Assessments.** Casey Life Skills covers nine domains rigorously (OBSERVED) — but it is an assessment, it is free, and it is aimed at child-welfare practitioners rather than buyers. It explicitly is "not a test"; it diagnoses without instructing.
3. **Homeschool curricula.** SkillTrek, Well-Trained Mind Academy, LIFEPAC (OBSERVED) — multi-domain, but sold to *parents* for *minors*, mediated by a teacher.
4. **Etsy binders.** Multi-domain in form (OBSERVED) — but blank forms, not instruction.

Every paid winner in this landscape is **single-domain**: Clutterbug (organizing), Clean My Space (cleaning), Mercury Stardust (renter repair), Her First $100K (money), How to ADHD (executive function).

**INFERRED: nobody is selling a taught, multi-domain system directly to the adult who needs it.**

## Q2. Does anyone target first-time independent adults specifically?

**Institutions do. Businesses mostly don't — and the ones that tried, failed.**

Targeting first-time independent adults is concentrated in non-commercial channels: Casey Life Skills (14–21), Project LIFE, DoD TAP (200,000/year), extension Rent Smart / RentWise / Informed Renter, and university "Adulting 101" courses. **All free or funded.**

The commercial attempts that targeted this exact audience are the weakest performers found:

- The Adulting School — 65 X followers, no posts displayed (OBSERVED)
- Udemy "Adulting" — 361 students (OBSERVED)
- Outschool "Adulting 101 FLEX" — completed by 5 learners (OBSERVED)
- "Adulting: Real Life Skills" iOS app — free (OBSERVED)

Meanwhile the commercially successful players target a **different buyer**: Clutterbug and Clean My Space serve overwhelmed adults already running a household; The Home Edit serves affluent homeowners; Etsy binder shops serve household managers.

**INFERRED: the 18–25 first-independence buyer is well-served by free institutional programming and poorly monetized by commercial ones. The person with the problem and the person with the credit card are frequently not the same person.**

## Q3. Does anyone position around "nobody taught you this" rather than aspiration or shame?

**Yes — and it correlates with the successes, not the failures.**

Verbatim instances found:

- **The Adulting School:** "We're teaching the shit they don't teach you in school." Marketing line: "Come here instead of calling your parents."
- **"Adulting: Real Life Skills" app:** "No shame. No boomer advice. No spreadsheets, no guilt."
- **Clutterbug:** "No shame. No perfection."
- **How to ADHD:** "Forget 'try harder.' When your brain works differently, you need to try different."
- **UW-Madison Extension:** frames Adulting 101 as skills "often not taught in the classroom."

The counter-example is instructive. **The Home Edit** positions purely on aspiration and scores **88% with critics and 17% with the audience** (OBSERVED). **Mercury Stardust** positions on capability-plus-reassurance and scores **4.77 from 849 ratings** with a #1 NYT slot (OBSERVED).

**INFERRED: the positioning is not open territory — it is proven territory. Differentiation cannot be the frame. It has to be the scope (multi-domain) and the delivery (an actual taught system). Everyone currently using this frame is single-domain.**

## Q4. Where nobody is doing something — opening or graveyard?

### GRAVEYARD — evidence of demand failure

**"Adulting" as a standalone paid course category for young adults.** The evidence is consistent and it is bad:

- **The Adulting School** had the exact positioning under consideration, a four-pillar multi-domain curriculum, national press (CNBC, NPR, Maine Public, WGME, CBS12), and a $30 price point. It renamed within a year to "Adulting Collective." Its X account shows **142 posts, 65 followers, and nothing displayed.** The site is live but shows no dated activity, no pricing, no enrollment. (OBSERVED)
- **Udemy "Adulting":** 361 students since roughly 2020. (OBSERVED)
- **Outschool "Adulting 101 FLEX":** completed by 5 learners. (OBSERVED)
- The most recent entrant — a ~2-month-old iOS app with almost exactly this positioning — is **free.** (OBSERVED)

**INFERRED.** These are not marketing failures; several had press most startups would kill for. The pattern indicates the 18–25 buyer with the acute need has the least money, the lowest willingness to pay to fix a problem they find embarrassing, and free substitutes everywhere.

### GRAVEYARD-ADJACENT — the free-crowding problem

**Personal finance education for young adults, sold direct, is a hard category.** NGPF gives a full free semester course to teachers in schools serving 75% of US high schoolers (OBSERVED). Casey Life Skills is free and state-mandated. Rent Smart, RentWise, Informed Renter, Project LIFE, Freedom Homeschooling's curated lists — all free (OBSERVED). Even the successful commercial player, Her First $100K, earns primarily from **sponsorship and speaking**, not courses.

**INFERRED: don't lead with money content.** It is the domain with the best-funded free competition in the entire landscape. Ramsey shows it *can* be sold — but only into a community that wants a specific frame.

### GENUINE OPENING — empty, but with adjacent proof of payment

Three specific holes where nobody was found *and* where evidence shows people pay for the neighboring thing:

**(a) A paid, taught course for renters.**
Mercury Stardust proved renters pay: #1 NYT, 849 Goodreads ratings, #17 in Home Repair (OBSERVED). But the only *courses* for renters found are free extension programs and a UK council tenant academy. **Nobody has converted that demonstrated book demand into a course.** Reader complaints even name the format gap — some wanted video walkthroughs rather than QR codes off a printed page.

**(b) Multi-domain instruction sold to people who already buy multi-domain forms.**
The Etsy binder market is proven — shops with 38.7k, 8.6k, and 5.2k reviews selling home + food + money + paperwork + medical bundles at $3–$15 (OBSERVED). Those buyers have already self-identified as wanting one system across domains. **Nobody sells them the instruction that makes the forms work.** The forms are the graveyard of good intentions; the teaching is the missing product. The heavy discounting also suggests these sellers compete only on price — which means they are not defending a method.

**(c) The home / food / maintenance side of institutional transitions.**
TAP is mandatory for ~200,000 people per year and covers career, benefits, and money — and nothing about running a household (OBSERVED). Casey Life Skills *assesses* daily living and housing but explicitly points to a curriculum gap it does not fill. These are captive, funded, recurring audiences with a named uncovered domain. Extension services already demonstrate the paid tier is **train-the-trainer and certification** ($325 at UW-Madison), not the consumer course.

### The honest read

**The entry-point choice — household cleaning and organization — is correct and the evidence is unambiguous.** That is where verified paid demand actually sits: Clutterbug's $30 → $94 → $99–$149 ladder, Clean My Space's $0.99–$19.99 shelf, Go Clean Co's $40 CAD guides, the Etsy binder market's tens of thousands of shop reviews.

**The audience choice is where the risk sits.** Every commercial attempt verified at "first-time independent adults, multi-domain" underperformed badly, and every successful player sells to someone older with a household already running.

**INFERRED — strategic read rather than a finding:** the opening is not "adulting for 18–25." It is the **multi-domain taught system, entered through cleaning, sold to the person who is overwhelmed rather than the person who is new** — with the 18–25 first-independence use case as a **gift-purchase channel** and an **institutional licensing channel** rather than the primary consumer market. The gift buyer is a parent. The institutional buyer is TAP, a residence life office, a foster-care aging-out program, or a church young-adult ministry. Both have money. The 22-year-old does not.

Two structural patterns worth carrying forward:

1. **The certification tier is where this category actually monetizes at the high end.** Clutterbug ($99–$149 + Certified Organizational Specialist™), Go Clean Co (intent-to-use certification filing), UW-Madison Extension ($325 Financial Coaching Foundations + Rent Smart train-the-trainer). Three independent players in three sub-categories converged on the same answer.
2. **The mechanism that makes this content work is emotional scaffolding, not information.** Mercury Stardust's "emotional resets," Clutterbug's "no shame, no perfection," How to ADHD's "try different, not harder." The information is free everywhere. The permission to not already know is what people are buying.
