# Customer Problem Inventory

A catalog of the actual problems Mr. Life Manager exists to solve, organized by
domain. This is the **raw material for every product** — each problem is a
potential checklist item, article, module, or chapter.

**Status:** These are hypotheses drawn from the concept, not validated research.
Phase 1 of the [roadmap](../../planning/roadmap.md) tests them. Mark items with
✅ once confirmed by a real customer conversation.

**How problems are scored**

- **Frequency** — how many people in the target audience have it
- **Pain** — how much it hurts when it bites
- **Teachability** — how well a written system actually fixes it

The best MVP content is high on all three. High-frequency + high-teachability is
where checklists win; high-pain + low-frequency is where you build trust.

## Home (general)

- No idea what a home actually needs to function — arrives with dishes but no
  trash bags, plunger, batteries, light bulbs, first aid, tools
- Furniture and storage placed by accident, not by how the space is used
- No defined home for common objects; everything is re-decided daily
- Surfaces become universal drop zones (counter, table, chair, floor by the door)
- Storage exists but is unusable — too full, too deep, too far from point of use
- Doesn't know which small tools matter (screwdriver, tape measure, drill, plunger)
- No baseline: no idea what "acceptable" looks like, so it's either spotless or
  spiraling
- Doesn't know what's the landlord's problem versus their own
- Never reads the lease; doesn't know rules, notice periods, or deposit terms
- Renter's insurance: doesn't have it, doesn't know what it covers
- Doesn't know how to fix trivial things — toilet flapper, drain clog, tripped
  breaker, smoke detector chirp
- Doesn't know where the water shutoff or breaker panel is *(low frequency, very
  high pain — classic trust-builder content)*

## Cleaning

- Doesn't know **how often** anything should be done — the single most-asked
  question in the entire category
- Doesn't know what supplies to buy; over-buys specialty products, under-buys
  basics
- Doesn't know what actually cleans what (and which combinations are dangerous)
- Cleans reactively, only when it's already bad or someone is coming over
- Cleaning takes hours because it's always deferred, which guarantees more deferral
- No sequence — cleans in a random order, redoes work (dusts after vacuuming)
- Bathroom neglect until it's genuinely unpleasant; soap scum, mildew, toilet ring
- Never cleaned the things that don't look dirty: fridge coils, dryer vent,
  washing machine, dishwasher filter, showerhead, range hood, garbage disposal
- Supplies stored far from where they're used — pure friction
- Roommate conflict: mismatched standards, no agreement, resentment
- Perfectionism paralysis — won't start a job they can't finish perfectly
- Doesn't know what's cosmetic versus what's actual hygiene or damage prevention

## Cooking and food

- Can't cook a repeatable set of meals from raw ingredients
- Doesn't know what a stocked pantry contains
- No meal planning, so every dinner is a fresh decision at the worst hour
- Buys perishables aspirationally; throws away real money weekly
- Doesn't understand dates: sell-by vs. use-by vs. best-by
- Doesn't know how long leftovers actually last, or how to store them
- Food safety gaps: thawing, cross-contamination, danger-zone timing, reheating
- Doesn't clean as they cook, so cooking creates a second chore they now dread
- No kitchen tool knowledge — what's essential, what's a gimmick
- Doesn't know how to read a recipe or scale one
- Overspends on delivery while food rots in the fridge
- Can't cook for guests; hospitality gets blocked at the menu

## Shopping and supplies

- No running list; shops from memory and forgets the actual need
- Doesn't know what to keep stocked versus buy as needed
- Runs out of essentials at the worst time — emergency trips at premium prices
- Doesn't know reasonable prices, so can't tell a deal from a markup
- Bulk-buys things that expire; under-buys things that don't
- No system for consumables (paper goods, filters, batteries, soap)
- Impulse spending in-store and online with no visibility into the total

## Laundry

- Doesn't know sorting rules, or over-sorts into paralysis
- Wrong settings; shrinks, fades, or damages clothes
- Doesn't know detergent amounts (almost always too much)
- Leaves wet laundry to sour; rewashes
- Clean laundry lives in the basket or on the chair for a week — the "laundry
  chair" is nearly universal
- No cadence, so it becomes an all-day event nobody wants to start
- Stain handling: doesn't know to treat immediately, or what treats what
- Doesn't read care labels; doesn't know what dry-clean-only really means
- Never cleans the washer or lint trap *(dryer lint is a genuine fire risk —
  high-pain, low-frequency, great content)*
- Doesn't own an iron, a steamer, or the knowledge of when either matters

## Home maintenance

- No maintenance calendar; nothing is scheduled, so nothing happens
- Doesn't know what needs periodic attention at all: HVAC filters, smoke detector
  batteries, water heater, gutters, caulk, dryer vent, refrigerator coils
- Doesn't know seasonal tasks (winterizing, AC prep, gutters)
- Small problems ignored until they're expensive — a slow drip, a running toilet,
  a soft spot, a small stain on a ceiling
- Doesn't know when to DIY versus call someone
- Doesn't know how to find or evaluate a contractor
- No basic tool kit and no idea what belongs in one
- Car maintenance is entirely unmanaged: oil, tires, registration, inspection,
  fluids, brakes, the meaning of a dashboard light

## Money

- No visibility — genuinely does not know where the money goes
- No budget, or an over-complicated one abandoned in week two
- No emergency buffer; any surprise becomes debt
- Bills paid manually and late; late fees and credit damage
- Subscription creep: pays for things forgotten months ago
- Doesn't understand credit — score, utilization, why it matters, how to build it
- Student loans: repayment options, deferment, forgiveness all opaque
- Taxes: withholding, filing, what's deductible, what documents matter
- Insurance is a black box: renter's, auto, health, deductibles, what's covered
- Retirement/401(k) match left on the table
- No pricing intuition — can't tell a fair price from a bad one
- Money conflict in couples: different histories, no shared framework, no
  visibility, avoidance
- The "stupid tax": late fees, overdrafts, replacing lost items, express
  shipping, convenience premiums

## Paperwork and admin

- No system for physical mail; important envelopes get stacked, then lost
- Doesn't know which documents to keep, for how long, or where
- Can't find critical documents when needed: birth certificate, SS card,
  passport, title, lease, insurance policy, tax returns
- No secure storage for irreplaceable documents
- Missed renewals: registration, license, insurance, passport, professional
  certifications
- Doesn't know how to handle official correspondence — jury duty, IRS notices,
  benefit paperwork
- Never set up: renter's insurance, a will, beneficiaries, medical directives
- Transition paperwork burden (marriage, divorce, death, move, deployment) is
  overwhelming and completely undocumented anywhere they can find

## Scheduling and time

- No single calendar; commitments live in memory, texts, and three apps
- Double-books; misses appointments
- No weekly planning, so the week happens *to* them
- Underestimates how long things take
- Never schedules maintenance or admin, so it only happens under pressure
- No margin — every day fully committed, so any surprise cascades
- Couples don't share a calendar; conflict and missed obligations follow
- Doesn't protect rest; rest happens only through collapse
- Reactive to notifications all day; no protected attention

## Digital life

- Email: thousands unread, important messages lost in the pile
- Files scattered across desktop, downloads, phone, and three cloud services
- No naming or folder conventions; can't find anything
- No backups — a lost phone or dead laptop is a genuine catastrophe
- Password chaos: reuse, sticky notes, no manager
- No 2FA on critical accounts
- Photos: tens of thousands, unorganized, unbacked, unshared
- Notification overload; phone drives the day
- Doesn't know what accounts exist or which have their card on file
- Digital clutter creates the same cognitive load as physical clutter, unnoticed
- No plan for digital legacy or account recovery

## Work

- No system for tracking tasks and commitments; relies on memory
- Inbox is the to-do list, which means other people set the priorities
- Doesn't know how to prepare for or run a meeting
- Poor boundaries between work and home, especially remote
- Doesn't track accomplishments, so reviews and résumés start from zero
- Doesn't understand benefits, PTO policy, or how to actually take time off
- No professional-document system: offer letters, reviews, certifications
- Career admin (résumé, LinkedIn, references) only touched in a crisis

## Relationships

- Doesn't initiate; friendship maintenance is entirely passive
- No system for remembering birthdays, anniversaries, or checking in
- Doesn't reciprocate invitations, so invitations stop coming
- Household labor imbalance and invisible labor breed resentment
- No shared standards or expectations with roommates or spouse
- Conflict avoidance until it's a blowup
- Family obligations (holidays, visits, caregiving) are unplanned and stressful
- Isolation after a move or transition, with no plan to rebuild
- Can't host, so relationships stay shallow — **the loop that ties the whole
  business together**

## Hospitality

- Never has people over; the home isn't guest-ready and the gap feels too large
- Thinks hosting requires the home be perfect first
- No repeatable hosting menu; every gathering is invented from scratch
- Doesn't know what guests actually need (a place to sit, a place for coats,
  clean bathroom, a drink, food, a clear exit cue)
- No guest supplies: extra towels, clean sheets, toilet paper, an air mattress
- Panic-cleans for hours before guests, which trains them to avoid hosting
- Doesn't know how to host overnight guests
- Can't handle a spontaneous "can I come by?"
- Anxiety about being judged for the home, the food, or the effort

## Life transitions

- Moving: no checklist, no address-change list, no utility sequencing, no packing
  or unpacking method
- First independent home: everything above, all at once, with no baseline
- Marriage: merging possessions, accounts, calendars, standards, and families
- New job or relocation: rebuilding routines and relationships from zero
- Divorce or breakup: splitting a household, a life administration nightmare
- Death of a spouse or parent: severe paperwork burden during severe grief
- Military deployment and return: households that start, stop, and restart
- Illness or injury: reduced capacity, systems collapse without slack
- New baby: total routine disruption with no sleep to rebuild with
- Aging parents: caregiving load lands on top of an existing life
- **Every transition is a moment of maximum need and maximum willingness to buy.**
  It is also the moment when existing systems break and can be rebuilt — the
  best possible teaching window.

## Cross-cutting patterns

Five patterns show up in every domain. They're the reason a *systems* brand works
better than thirteen separate how-to brands:

1. **Nobody knows the cadence.** "How often should I…" is the most common
   question in every single domain. A cadence reference may be the single most
   valuable asset the business can produce.
2. **Reactive instead of scheduled.** Everything happens under pressure because
   nothing was scheduled. → [preventative-vs-crisis.md](../../frameworks/preventative-vs-crisis.md)
3. **Nothing has a defined place** — objects, files, documents, tasks, or money.
   → [everything-has-a-place.md](../../frameworks/everything-has-a-place.md)
4. **Everything is stored in memory**, which fails exactly when it's needed most.
   → Systems beat memory, in [principles.md](../vision/principles.md)
5. **Shame prevents asking**, which prevents learning, which deepens the shame.
   Breaking this loop is the brand's core job.

## MVP content priorities

Highest frequency × highest pain × highest teachability:

1. **Cadence reference** — how often to do everything ("How Often Should I…?")
2. **First apartment supply and setup list**
3. **Cleaning routines by frequency**, not by room
4. **Ten meals + a stocked pantry**
5. **Laundry, fully explained**
6. **Annual maintenance calendar**
7. **Paperwork: what to keep, where, how long**
8. **Money basics: accounts, autopay, buffer**
9. **Roommate/household agreement**
10. **Hosting: the 48-hour plan**
