# Customer Interview Script — Phase 1

**Goal:** find out whether these problems are real, and whether anyone pays to
solve them. **Not** to describe the product or get people excited about it.

**Target: 15–20 conversations** across the personas in
[target-audiences.md](../../vision/target-audiences.md). 20–30 minutes each.

---

## The rule that makes this work

**Do not describe what you're building.** The moment you do, they start being
polite, and polite answers are worthless.

If they ask, say: *"I'm trying to understand how people learn to run a house —
I'll tell you after, if you want."* Then actually tell them after.

**Ask about the past, never the future.** "Would you buy…" predicts nothing.
"What did you buy" is the only reliable signal there is.

---

## Opening

> "I'm trying to understand how people figure out the practical side of running a
> home. There are no right answers and I'm not selling anything. Mind if I ask
> about how you've handled it?"

Then get the situation:

1. How long have you been living on your own?
2. Walk me through how you got set up. What was the first week like?

---

## The core question ★

**This is the highest-yield question in the whole script.** Ask it early, then
shut up and let the silence work.

> **"What's something about running a home that you had to figure out on your
> own, and felt like you should have already known?"**

Follow-ups, in order:
- How did you eventually figure it out?
- How long did you go not knowing?
- Did it cost you anything — money, or a deposit, or a fight?
- Did you ask anyone? Why not?

*That last one gets at the shame barrier, which is the thing the whole brand is
built on. Don't skip it.*

---

## Where it actually hurts

3. What's the part of keeping a house you like least?
4. What in your place is bothering you right now that you haven't dealt with?
5. When was the last time something became a crisis that you could have caught earlier?
6. Is there anything you avoid dealing with because you're not sure how?
7. Has not knowing something ever cost you real money?

---

## What they've already done about it ★

**Past behavior is the only real signal.** Everything here is more predictive
than anything they say about the future.

8. Have you ever looked this stuff up? Where did you go?
9. Did what you found actually help?
10. **Have you ever paid for anything to help with this?** A book, an app, a
    course, a cleaner, an organizer, a planner?
11. What made you decide to pay that time?
12. Is there anything you tried and gave up on? What made you stop?

---

## Hospitality — the destination

13. Do you have people over? How often?
14. What has to be true before you'll invite someone?
15. If someone asked to come by in two hours, what would you say?

*If "no, my place isn't ready" is common, that's the thesis confirming itself.*

---

## The name test ★

**Do this near the end, so it doesn't colour everything before it.** And watch
their face, not just their words.

> "Last thing. If you saw something called **Mr. Life Manager**, what would you
> expect it to be?"

Then:
- Who do you picture it being for?
- Does it sound like it's for you?
- How does it make you feel — if anything?

**Note verbatim reactions**, especially from women 20–35. Listen specifically for
*butler*, *app*, *productivity*, *not for me*, and *condescending*. This is the
live question in
[ADR-001](../../../planning/decisions.md#adr-001--brand-name-mr-life-manager) and
no amount of analysis substitutes for hearing it.

---

## Closing

16. If you could hand your past self one page of instructions, what would be on it?
17. Is there anyone else you think I should talk to?

Then tell them what you're building, and ask what they think. **That answer
doesn't count as data** — they like you by now — but it's a courtesy, and it
sometimes surfaces a good objection.

## What you're looking for across all 20

The exit criteria from [roadmap.md](../../../planning/roadmap.md) Phase 1:

- **The top 10 problems confirmed** by real people, not assumed
- **At least 3 unprompted "I'd pay for that"** — unprompted is the whole word
- **Which persona has the most acute, most reachable pain**
- **Whether the name survives** contact with the actual audience

## Reading the results honestly

**Good signal.** They interrupt you to tell you a story. They ask when they can
have it. They've already paid for something adjacent. They name a problem you
hadn't written down.

**Bad signal you'll want to ignore.** Polite enthusiasm with no specifics.
"That's such a good idea." Agreement with everything you say. Nobody has ever
paid for anything in this category.

**The honest failure mode is a real problem nobody prioritizes.** If people
recognize every problem, have never paid to fix one, and don't ask when it's
ready — that's a finding, not a setback. It would point at the institutional and
gift channels rather than direct sale, which
[ADR-010](../../../planning/decisions.md#adr-010--beachhead-keep-the-audience-change-the-payer)
already leans toward.
