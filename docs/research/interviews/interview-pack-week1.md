## 1. Why this week matters

Phase 1 lives or dies on real conversations, not more docs. This week’s BD track runs **in parallel with recording Dave**: capture is the content spine; interviews are the market spine. Five warm-network chats this week prove you can book and run the format, surface verbatim language for copy, and start checking paid signal + name reaction — without waiting for the full 15–20. Polite enthusiasm is noise; past pain, past spend, and unprompted “I’d pay” are signal. Stay in learner mode; save the product for after the name test.

## 2. Who to ask first — 5 outreach slots

Keep messages short, non-salesy, and about *their* experience. Offer 20–30 minutes. Prefer text for friends; email for parents / church / campus contacts.

### Slot A — Friend who moved recently (recent mover)

**Text:**
> Hey — random favor. I’m trying to understand how people figure out the practical side of running a home after a move. Would you do a 20–30 min chat this week? Not selling anything — just curious what the first weeks were actually like. Coffee / call / walk, whatever’s easy.

### Slot B — Newlywed couple (or one of them)

**Text:**
> Congrats again on the wedding / settling in. I’m talking to a few people about how couples figure out the house side of life together — chores, hosting, systems, the boring stuff nobody teaches. Could I ask you (and [partner] if they’re up for it) ~25 minutes of questions this week? Totally informal, no pitch.

### Slot C — Recent grad / first job + first place

**Text:**
> Quick ask: I’m trying to learn how people handle the “adult house stuff” after leaving school / starting work — the things you suddenly realize you were supposed to already know. Would you let me interview you for 20–30 min this week? Zero sales, just stories. Happy to work around your schedule.

### Slot D — Overwhelmed householder (friend / coworker who’s drowning in home admin)

**Text:**
> Hey — I’m doing a few short conversations about the practical side of keeping a household running when life is already full. Would you be open to 20–30 minutes this week? I’m not pitching a product; I just want to hear what’s actually hard and what you’ve already tried. No prep needed.

### Slot E — Parent of a student / young adult (parents-as-buyers; church or campus network)

**Email / text:**
> Hi [Name] — I’m doing informal research on how young adults learn to run a home, and how parents think about helping during that transition. Would you have 20–30 minutes this week for a short conversation? I’m not selling anything — I’d love your perspective as a parent. Happy to call or meet after [church / campus event].

**Optional warm intros to ask for at the end of any call:** “Is there anyone else you think I should talk to?” (script Q17)

## 3. Pocket card — 8 questions max

Use this live. Ask about the **past**, never “would you buy…”. If they ask what you’re building: *“I’m trying to understand how people learn to run a house — I’ll tell you after, if you want.”*

**Opening (10 sec):**  
“I’m trying to understand how people figure out the practical side of running a home. No right answers, not selling anything. Mind if I ask how you’ve handled it?”

| # | Ask | Why |
|---|-----|-----|
| 1 | How long have you been living on your own / in this setup? Walk me through how you got set up — what was the first week like? | Context |
| 2 | **★ Core:** What’s something about running a home that you had to figure out on your own, and felt like you should have already known? *(Then shut up.)* | Highest yield |
| 3 | How did you eventually figure it out? How long did you go not knowing? Did it cost you money, a deposit, or a fight? Did you ask anyone — why / why not? | Shame + cost |
| 4 | What’s the part of keeping a house you like least? What’s bothering you right now that you haven’t dealt with? | Current pain |
| 5 | **★ Paid:** Have you ever paid for anything to help with this — book, app, course, cleaner, organizer, planner? What made you pay that time? Anything you tried and gave up on? | Only reliable WTP signal |
| 6 | Do you have people over? How often? What has to be true before you’ll invite someone? If someone asked to come by in two hours, what would you say? | Hospitality thesis |
| 7 | If you could hand your past self one page of instructions, what would be on it? | Product clue |
| 8 | **★ Name test (near end):** If you saw something called **Mr. Life Manager**, what would you expect it to be? Who’s it for? Sound like it’s for you? How does it make you feel? | Watch their face; note verbatim |

**Close:** Ask who else to talk to → *then* you may briefly say what you’re exploring. That answer is courtesy, **not data**.

## 4. After-call checklist (within an hour)

- [ ] **Verbatim notes** — their exact words, especially complaints (copywriting gold)
- [ ] **Problems confirmed** — which known problems they hit; any problem **not** in the inventory
- [ ] **Paid signal** — what they’ve actually paid for (and what they abandoned)
- [ ] **Name reaction** — verbatim; watch for *butler / app / productivity / not for me / condescending* (esp. women 20–35)
- [ ] **Persona match** — recent mover / newlywed / recent grad / overwhelmed householder / parent-as-buyer / none
- [ ] **Unprompted “I’d pay”** — yes/no (only if they volunteered it)
- [ ] **Follow-up intro** — anyone they named
