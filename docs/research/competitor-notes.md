# Competitor Notes

> **⚠️ This file contains no researched competitor facts.**
>
> Nothing here describes any specific company's real pricing, audience size,
> revenue, or strategy. It defines **categories to research** and a **method for
> researching them**. Specific claims get added only after someone actually looks
> — with a source and a date attached.
>
> Fabricated competitive intelligence is worse than none: it produces confident
> decisions built on nothing.

---

## Why this matters more than usual here

Mr. Life Manager sits at the intersection of several large, crowded categories.
It has **no direct competitor** in the sense of a company doing exactly this —
which is either the opportunity or the warning sign, and research is how we find
out which.

Two questions the research must answer:

1. **Is the gap real?** Does anyone teach practical life systems across domains
   to first-time independent adults — or does everyone do one domain?
2. **Is the gap empty for a reason?** Categories are sometimes vacant because
   nobody will pay. That is the more dangerous possibility, and it deserves an
   honest hearing.

## Competitor categories

### 1. Cleaning and home-care content

Creators and brands teaching how to clean. Usually free, ad- or
sponsorship-supported, sometimes with a paid product attached.

- **Overlaps:** the entry-point domain; the audience discovers us here
- **Differs:** single domain, entertainment-forward, aesthetic, rarely systematic
- **Research:** how do they monetize? Do any sell a structured curriculum, or
  only content? What do paid products cost?

### 2. Home organization and decluttering

Method-driven organizing systems and the surrounding book/media ecosystem, plus
the professional organizer trade.

- **Overlaps:** "everything has a place" is shared ground
- **Differs:** they focus on possessions and space; we cover thirteen domains and
  include maintenance, money, and admin
- **Risk:** we could be mistaken for this category, which is a positioning
  problem more than a competitive one
- **Research:** which methods have durable brands and why? What's the actual
  price and business model of a professional organizer?

### 3. "Adulting" / life-skills content

Books, courses, YouTube channels, and TikTok accounts aimed at young adults
learning practical skills.

- **Overlaps:** **the closest category to our positioning**
- **Differs:** usually listicle-shaped, tonally jokey, one-off tips rather than
  systems
- **Research priority: HIGHEST.** Who's doing this well? Do any of them make real
  money? If they're all free content with no products, why? If someone has
  already built the paid version, what did they learn?

### 4. Personal productivity and task systems

Time-management and task-management methodologies, apps, and their surrounding
ecosystems.

- **Overlaps:** capture, decision rules, review cadences —
  [do-it-now.md](../../frameworks/do-it-now.md) is in direct conversation with
  this category
- **Differs:** they're knowledge-work-shaped; almost none of it addresses a
  household. We should be explicit about that intellectual debt where it exists.
- **Research:** how do these communities handle domestic life? Is there an unmet
  need there?

### 5. Personal finance education

Money-management content, courses, and coaching for people early in adulthood.

- **Overlaps:** the money domain
- **Differs:** money is one of our thirteen domains, not the whole business
- **Research priority: HIGH** — this category is a **proven business model**
  teaching a life skill to a similar audience. Study its pricing, funnel, and
  institutional/church channel strategy. Best available analogue for what
  "working" looks like.

### 6. Homemaking and traditional home-economics content

Content teaching cooking, cleaning, sewing, and household management, often with
a values or faith framing.

- **Overlaps:** substantial content overlap; sometimes the same convictions
- **Differs:** frequently gendered, aesthetic, or ideological; ours is
  gender-neutral and systems-first
- **Research:** how does this audience overlap with ours? Is the faith-adjacent
  channel reachable, and on what terms?

### 7. Institutional life-skills programs

University first-year and residence-life programming, military transition
assistance, financial-literacy nonprofits, foster-care aging-out curricula,
reentry programs, church young-adult ministries.

- **Overlaps:** the licensing market
- **Differs:** they're buyers as much as competitors
- **Research priority: HIGH** — the questions are what curricula they currently
  use, who chooses it, what budget line it comes from, and what the buying cycle
  looks like. This determines whether Phase 5 licensing is realistic or fantasy.

### 8. Home maintenance and DIY

Repair, maintenance, and homeownership content.

- **Overlaps:** the maintenance domain
- **Differs:** homeowner-oriented and project-based; ours is renter-first and
  cadence-based ("what to do when," not "how to fix this")
- **Research:** is there anything good for *renters*? This may be a genuinely
  underserved niche.

### 9. Coaching and professional services

Life coaches, ADHD coaches, organizing consultants, household managers, and
concierge services.

- **Overlaps:** the premium rung of the ladder
- **Differs:** we teach transferable systems rather than doing the work for them
- **Note:** **ADHD and executive-function coaching deserves separate, careful
  study.** A meaningful share of our audience may be neurodivergent, and that
  field has real expertise about external systems that our frameworks should
  learn from — and should not casually duplicate or contradict.

### 10. Templates and digital product marketplaces

Notion template creators, printable/planner sellers, and digital-product
marketplaces.

- **Overlaps:** format and price point of our low and mid tiers
- **Differs:** they sell containers; we sell the curriculum that goes in them
- **Research:** what do household and life-admin templates actually sell for, and
  how many units do the visible ones move?

## Research checklist

> **A ready-to-send prompt for Phases A–D exists:**
> [research-prompts.md](research-prompts.md) § Prompt 2, along with an acceptance
> test to apply before any result enters this file. Use it rather than composing
> a fresh request — it encodes the failure modes of the first research pass.


### Phase A — Landscape (goal: know what exists)

- [ ] Identify 3–5 real players in each of the ten categories above
- [ ] For each: what do they sell, at what price, to whom, and how are they found?
- [ ] Note which are content businesses (ads/sponsorship) vs. product businesses
- [ ] Find the closest thing that exists to *First Place* — does it exist at all?
- [ ] Record every finding with **source URL and date checked**

### Phase B — Gap analysis (goal: know if the gap is real)

- [ ] Does anyone teach practical systems across multiple life domains?
- [ ] Does anyone target first-time independent adults specifically?
- [ ] Does anyone position around "nobody taught you" rather than aspiration?
- [ ] Does anyone connect household order to hospitality?
- [ ] **Where the answer is no — is that an opportunity or a graveyard?** Look for
      abandoned attempts, dead blogs, and discontinued products. Those are the
      most informative artifacts available.

### Phase C — Demand and pricing (goal: know if people pay)

- [ ] What do comparable digital products actually sell for?
- [ ] Any visible sales-volume signals (review counts, "X students," bestseller
      ranks)?
- [ ] Search demand for "first apartment checklist," "how often should I clean,"
      "adulting checklist," and similar
- [ ] Which subreddits and forums have this audience, and what do they ask?
- [ ] Copy exact customer phrasing verbatim — it's the best copywriting source
      that exists

### Phase D — Institutional channel (goal: know if Phase 5 is real)

- [ ] What curriculum do universities use for life skills, if any?
- [ ] Who decides, and out of which budget?
- [ ] What does the military transition curriculum cover?
- [ ] Which church/ministry publishers sell young-adult practical curriculum?
- [ ] What are typical per-seat or site-license prices in this space?
- [ ] What's the sales cycle, and what compliance/accessibility requirements exist?

### Phase E — Positioning (goal: know how to be different)

- [ ] Collect competitor taglines and positioning statements
- [ ] Identify claims that are overused (and therefore useless to us)
- [ ] Identify what nobody is saying
- [ ] Find where competitors' reviews complain — unmet needs live there
- [ ] Verify the "Mr. Life Manager" name isn't already in use, and check for

## Recording format

When adding real findings, use this shape so a claim can always be traced:

```markdown
### [Name] — [Category]
- **What they do:**
- **Who it's for:**
- **Pricing:**            (as observed, with date)
- **Business model:**
- **Strengths:**
- **Weaknesses / gaps:**
- **What we can learn:**
- **How we differ:**
- **Source:**             [URL] — checked YYYY-MM-DD
- **Confidence:**         high / medium / low
```

Keep observed facts separate from interpretation. Anything not directly observed
gets labeled as inference.
