# Competitive Landscape: Practical Adult-Life Systems

**Date checked:** 5 September 2026  
**Standard:** Accuracy over coverage. Numbers not found on a source page are written “not found.” Claims are labeled **Observed** (seen on a page) or **Inferred**. No company, price, audience size, or revenue figure is invented.

**The business being mapped:** Teaches practical adult-life systems — home, food, money, time, maintenance, paperwork, hospitality — to people who were never explicitly taught them. Primary audience: first-time independent adults (first apartment, leaving home, newly married). Products: ebooks, checklists, worksheets, courses. Entry-point topic is household cleaning and organization, but that is not the whole scope.

## Four questions

### 1. Does anyone teach practical systems across MULTIPLE life domains, or is everyone single-domain?

**Mostly single-domain.** Cleaning brands clean. Organizing brands organize. YNAB does money. TAP does transition-to-civilian-career.

Multi-domain examples that are real:

- **MSU Adulting 101** — money, renting, cooking, cars, scams, sleep, mental health. Free, one-hour slices, not a packaged system.
- **aceadulting101.com** — eight domains in one 10-week paid course. Volume unverified.
- **Adulting Academy app** — six categories, parent-sold, price opaque.
- **Foster-care ILP / Casey / PAL / NYAP / ARISE** — the most complete multi-domain curricula in existence, sold to agencies, not to first-apartment consumers.
- **Illinois Adulting Certificate** — multi-domain but academic and $3,978.

**Observed:** No well-known consumer brand simultaneously sells a *system* for home + food + money + time + maintenance + paperwork + hospitality at checklist/course price points.

### 2. Does anyone target first-time independent adults specifically?

**Institutions yes, consumer brands almost no.**

MSU, Waterloo, UCR, ACC, Center on Halsted, TAP, and foster ILP name the transition.

Consumer cleaning/organizing brands target **overwhelmed parents and homeowners** (Clean Mama, FlyLady, Home Edit, KonMari). YNAB’s college year is the sharpest young-adult *finance* wedge. Adulting Academy markets to **parents of teens**, not to the 22-year-old renter. aceadulting101.com is the rare DTC site that says “young adults” on the homepage.

First apartment / newly married / leaving home as a *named primary customer* in a paid household-systems product: **scarcely found**.

### 3. Does anyone position around “nobody taught you this” rather than aspiration or shame?

**Yes — and it is the winning frame where it appears.**

Verbatim / near-verbatim positioning:

- Clean It Like You Mean It: “Chances are you were never taught…”
- MSU: “practical life skills often not taught in traditional classes.”
- Adulting Academy: “the essential skills school forgot.”
- Dana K. White site: “I'm not the horrible person I had been believing I was. I just didn't know how to manage my stuff!”
- KC Davis: care tasks are “morally neutral”; mess is not a moral failing.
- Vice headline (May 2025): “Gen Z Is Taking ‘Adulting 101’ Classes Because No One Taught Them Anything.”
- Waterloo course copy: “there are a lot of important life skills you might have wished you learned sooner.”

**Aspiration** (Home Edit rainbow pantries, tradwife from-scratch gnocchi) and **shame-adjacent hustle** still dominate social reach. **“Nobody taught you” + morally neutral + small systems** is the cluster that actually matches this brief — and it is concentrated in KC Davis, Dana K. White, Clutterbug, FlyLady, and extension Adulting 101, not in the pretty-container economy.

### 4. Where no one is doing something — opening or graveyard?

**Both. The evidence does not support “nobody would pay.” It supports “this is hard to productize as a lifestyle brand, easy to run as a workshop or institution.”**

**Graveyard (attempts that died or shrank)**

- **Society of Grownups** — well-funded, designed by IDEO, charming storefront, millennial “adulting” classes. Physical product killed in two years; digital remnant disappeared as a consumer brand. **Inferred lesson:** IRL clubhouse + insurance-company parent was the wrong vehicle, not proof that the *need* was fake. People came; the unit economics of a Brookline shop did not.
- **Portland Adulting School / Collective** — global press, $5 events, planned $19.99/mo school. No operating business found in 2026. **Inferred lesson:** virality ≠ operations; volunteer instructors and bar-back-room workshops do not become a catalog.
- **K-12 home economics** — demand did not die; supply was cut. MSU and foster ILP exist *because* that supply disappeared.

**Still paying / still running**

- Clean Mama membership $189–$228/year
- Clutterbug courses $30–$149 with hundreds of ratings on at least one cert course
- YNAB $109/year, free for a student year
- KC Davis 350,000+ books + $14.99 course
- Illinois certificate $3,978
- Residential boot camp listed at $995 (2025 date)
- Etsy household templates at $3–$17, some with reviews

**What looks like an opening (inferred, labeled as such)**

- Multi-domain *systems* (not vibes) for the first 24 months of independent housing
- Renter-specific maintenance and paperwork, not just removable wallpaper
- “Nobody taught you this” without tradwife aesthetics and without therapy-speak as the whole product
- Distribution into FYE offices, RA training, churches, and ILP programs that already teach this for free but lack modern consumer-grade materials

**What looks like a graveyard**

- Brick-and-mortar “adulting cafes”
- Generic “Adulting 101” as a *brand name* — crowded, mocked, and already used by universities for free
- Notion/Etsy checklist slop without a method
- Aspiration homemaking aimed at 22-year-old renters (wrong life stage, wrong housing type)

Empty *consumer* shelf for a cross-domain first-independence curriculum is therefore closer to **an opening with a distribution problem** than to “nobody will pay.” People already pay for adjacent single-domain systems and already show up to free multi-domain workshops. The failed attempts died of format (storefront, one-off summit, insurer side-project), not of absence of demand.
