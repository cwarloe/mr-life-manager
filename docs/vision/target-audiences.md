# Target Audiences

Personas, not demographics. "Ages 18–34" tells you nothing about what someone
will buy. "Just signed a lease, has no idea what a renter's insurance policy is
for, and is scared their parents will visit" tells you everything.

Each persona below includes situation, pain, desired outcomes, likely products,
and buying behavior. Willingness-to-pay figures are **estimates for testing, not
findings** — every one of them is a hypothesis to validate in Phase 1 of the
[roadmap](../../planning/roadmap.md).

---

## Persona 1 — Maya, 20: First Apartment After Dorm Life

**Situation**
Junior in college. Just moved from a dorm into a four-bedroom house with
roommates. For two years, someone else cleaned the bathrooms, someone else
handled the trash, and food came from a dining hall. Now there's a kitchen she
doesn't know how to stock, a shared bathroom nobody has claimed, a lease with
terms she skimmed, and a utility bill split four ways that nobody has actually
figured out.

**Pain points**
- Doesn't know what to buy — arrived with a shower caddy and no trash bags,
  no dish soap, no plunger, no light bulbs
- Has never cooked a meal from raw ingredients on a repeatable basis
- Roommate friction over chores with no agreed system; resentment builds fast
- Dishes and laundry accumulate to the point of feeling unfixable
- Money leaks: food waste, delivery apps, forgotten subscriptions, a late fee
- Quiet embarrassment; won't invite people over

**Desired outcomes**
- A place that doesn't feel gross, without spending her weekends on it
- A short list of meals she can actually make
- A chore agreement roommates will honor
- Not being the person who forgot to pay the internet bill

**Likely products**
- **First Apartment Checklist** (free lead magnet) — highest-fit entry point
- Cleaning-supply starter list + roommate chore agreement template
- Room-by-room setup guide
- Low-cost ebook: *First Place* (student edition)

**Buying behavior**
Low personal budget, high sensitivity to price, very high sensitivity to
*effort*. Will not buy a $99 course. Will absolutely download a free checklist
and might buy at **$9–$19** if it's concrete and immediately usable. Discovers
through TikTok/Instagram/Reddit, not search.

> **Strategic note:** Maya is the best *audience* and the worst *customer*. She
> validates the problem cheaply and shares aggressively. Monetize her through
> **institutional licensing** — the university housing office, RA program, or
> parent orientation is the one who pays. See
> [business-model.md](../business/business-model.md).

## Persona 2 — Tyler, 23: Young Adult Leaving the Parents' Home

**Situation**
Graduated, has a first real job, moving into his own place — possibly his first
time living without a parent in the building. Everything at home was handled by
somebody else and largely invisibly: groceries appeared, laundry got done, the
car got serviced, the dentist got scheduled. He is competent at his job and
genuinely lost on all of it.

**Pain points**
- Doesn't know the *cadence* of anything — how often sheets, filters, oil, dentist
- Paperwork panic: renter's insurance, W-4, health plan, car registration, taxes
- No system for mail; important envelopes get stacked and lost
- Cooks the same three things or eats out constantly; spends far more than he thinks
- First real income with no plan for it; vague dread about money
- Won't ask his parents, because asking feels like admitting he can't do it

**Desired outcomes**
- To feel like an actual adult rather than someone playing one
- To know what's supposed to happen and when, without having to ask
- To stop bleeding money on things he can't name
- A place he's not embarrassed to bring someone back to

**Likely products**
- *First Place: The Practical Guide to Running Your First Home* (flagship)
- Adulting paperwork toolkit — what documents exist, why, where they live
- Home maintenance calendar (annual, by season)
- Money basics module: accounts, autopay, buffer, what to do with a first salary

**Buying behavior**
**The best MVP customer.** Real income, acute and time-bound pain, high
motivation, and privacy-seeking — he'd rather pay $39 than ask his dad. Likely
to buy **$29–$79** for a comprehensive guide, and to buy *right at the moment of
the move*. Found through search ("how often should I", "first apartment
checklist"), YouTube, and Reddit.

## Persona 3 — Josh & Anna, 26 & 25: Newlywed Couple

**Situation**
Married eight months. Two people who each ran a household badly in different
ways now sharing one. Both work. They have combined possessions without ever
combining *systems*, and every unspoken assumption about how a home should run is
now a live negotiation — usually held at the worst possible moment.

**Pain points**
- Mismatched standards and invisible labor; one person tracks everything and
  resents it
- Recurring low-grade conflict about chores that's actually about fairness
- Money: two histories, two habits, no shared framework or shared visibility
- Nobody owns the calendar; double-booked weekends and missed obligations
- They want to host — his small group, her family — and it's a source of stress
  rather than joy
- Merged stuff, no place for any of it; two of everything, homes for nothing

**Desired outcomes**
- A shared, explicit system so household work stops being a negotiation
- Fair division without scorekeeping
- To be a couple who hosts well
- To start marriage with good rhythms instead of five years of accumulated
  friction

**Likely products**
- **Household Operating Agreement** worksheet — division of labor, standards,
  cadences, who owns what
- Shared calendar + money system setup guide
- *First Place: Couples Edition*
- Hospitality toolkit: hosting without panic
- A 30-day "Home Reset" program done together

**Buying behavior**
**The best willingness-to-pay in the set.** Two incomes, a genuine desire to
invest in the marriage, and a cultural norm of buying books and courses for it.
Will pay **$49–$149** for a structured program, more for something framed around
the marriage rather than the housework. Reached through churches, premarital
counseling programs, wedding registries, and marriage-content creators — which
also opens a strong **partnership and licensing channel**.

## Persona 4 — Dana, 34: The Overwhelmed Professional

**Situation**
Successful career, respected at work, manages a team and a budget. Home is
another story. Has lived independently for a decade and has simply never built
domestic systems — got by on income (takeout, replacing lost things, occasional
cleaner) and on tolerating a level of chaos she doesn't admit to anyone. Recently
something shifted: a burnout scare, a relationship, a health issue, a parent
visiting.

**Pain points**
- Cognitive load — endless open loops, none of them written down anywhere
- Competence gap between work self and home self, which is quietly humiliating
- Reactive on everything: crisis Target runs, expedited shipping, late fees
- No margin; no capacity for anything unplanned
- Won't invite people over, which is now costing her friendships
- Has tried apps, planners, and organizing content; nothing stuck because it was
  all too elaborate

**Desired outcomes**
- To stop carrying it all in her head
- Something simple enough to survive a bad week
- To reclaim weekends
- To be able to say "come over" without a two-day countdown

**Likely products**
- 30-Day Life Reset (structured, sequential, low-daily-cost)
- Digital life + personal admin toolkit
- Preventative maintenance calendar
- **Coaching or a household setup consultation** — she's the one who'll pay for it
- Membership for ongoing accountability

**Buying behavior**
**Highest revenue per customer.** Time-poor and cash-comfortable; will pay to
skip the learning curve. **$99–$499** for a course or program, **$500–$2,000+**
for a done-with-you setup. Buys on trust, so she needs proof — reviews, a strong
free resource, a real person behind the brand. Reached through newsletters,
podcasts, LinkedIn, and word of mouth.

> **Strategic note:** Dana is not the beachhead, but she is the reason the
> premium tier exists. Keep the door open for her from day one; don't build the
> brand so tightly around 20-year-olds that a 34-year-old feels condescended to.

## Persona 5 — Ray, 41: Rebuilding After a Major Transition

**Situation**
Something structural changed — a divorce, a spouse's death, a return from a
military deployment, release from a long institutional stay, recovery from a
serious illness, or the end of a long period of caregiving. Ray is setting up a
household essentially from zero, sometimes for the first time, sometimes after
years of someone else owning half of it.

**Pain points**
- Starting over on everything at once: dishes, accounts, insurance, routines
- Skill gaps that were previously covered by a partner or an institution
- Grief, disorientation, or trauma sitting on top of the logistics — capacity is
  genuinely reduced
- Paperwork burden is often severe (name changes, benefits, titles, accounts)
- Loneliness; the home feels empty and reinforces the loss
- Existing "life admin" content assumes a stability he doesn't currently have

**Desired outcomes**
- A short, sequenced list — what to do first, second, third — not everything
- A functioning home that doesn't require heroics to maintain
- Enough stability to think about the future
- Eventually, a home where people gather again

**Likely products**
- **Starting Over Toolkit** — sequenced, gentle, minimal-decision
- Essential-setup guide (the 30 things you actually need)
- Paperwork and accounts checklist for a life transition
- Coaching, where affordable
- **Licensed programs** through churches, VA and military transition programs,
  divorce support groups, reentry programs, and grief ministries

**Buying behavior**
Highly variable and often constrained; may be in financial upheaval. Individual
willingness to pay is unpredictable — **$0–$99**. But the *institutional* market
here is real and durable: organizations that serve people in transition buy
curriculum, and they buy it repeatedly. Emotional stakes and gratitude are high,
which produces unusually strong word of mouth and testimonials.

> **Strategic and ethical note:** This persona demands a different tone. Content
> for Ray must be gentler, shorter, and unfailingly free of "you should have
> known this." Handle with care, or don't handle it at all.

## Prioritization

| Persona | Pain urgency | Willingness to pay | Reach | MVP priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tyler — leaving home | High | Medium-High | High | **1 — primary MVP target** |
| Josh & Anna — newlyweds | Medium-High | **High** | Medium | **2 — best margin** |
| Maya — first apartment | High | Low (direct) | **Very high** | 3 — audience + licensing |
| Dana — overwhelmed pro | Medium | **Very high** | Medium | 4 — premium tier later |
| Ray — rebuilding | **Very high** | Low/variable | Low direct | 5 — mission-critical, institutional |

**Recommendation:** Build the MVP for **Tyler**, with copy that a **Maya** would
share and a **Josh & Anna** would buy. Those three overlap heavily in content —
one flagship product, three framings. Dana and Ray get served later, with
adapted tone and different products.

## Common threads across all five

Every persona shares the same four things, which is what makes this one business
and not five:

1. **A transition** created the need. Nobody buys this on an ordinary Tuesday.
2. **Nobody taught them**, and they feel some shame about it.
3. **They need a sequence**, not inspiration. What first, what next.
4. **The real prize is hospitality** — every one of them, in different words,
   wants a home they'd let someone else into.

**The transition is the trigger.** Marketing, timing, and partnerships should all
be built around moments of transition, not around evergreen interest in tidiness.
