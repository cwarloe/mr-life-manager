# Present-pain campaigns: company tonight and a home you cannot start tackling

**Status:** Copy draft for adversarial review, November 12, 2026. No live page, video, PDF, email or account changed. Proposed words are ours, not customer quotations. Recognition and response are untested. Based on the [felt-pain audit](../review/felt-pain-marketing/README.md), current [company task](../../products/landing/guests.html) and [reset task](../../products/landing/underwater.html).

The offer is help doing the work in a defined order, not a set of ideas to assess. Acquisition creative contains no checklist excerpt or instruction to perform an isolated step. It sends the person to the matching task, where they start or commit to an exact time to do it.

## 1. Company tonight

**Intended moment:** A visit is already happening tonight. The reader's place is not ready, and choosing where to start is getting in the way of preparing it.

### Two hooks

**A — Recommend first**

> Someone's coming over tonight.
> Your place isn't ready.
> You don't know where to start.

**B — Concrete unfinished spaces**

> They'll be here tonight.
> The sink is full.
> The bathroom isn't ready.

A leads with the real deadline, unreadiness and the specific obstacle the ordered task addresses. B tests whether naming the visible work creates stronger recognition than naming uncertainty. B will exclude people whose sink and bathroom are already handled; that is acceptable, but it may fit fewer readers. Neither is an effectiveness claim.

### Primary creative copy

> Someone's coming over tonight.
> Your place isn't ready.
> You don't know where to start.
>
> Get the rooms they'll use ready. Follow the work in order.
>
> Open the profile link. Start getting ready for tonight.

One CTA to the company task. No instructional preview, follow/save/comment request or second destination. Small signature: Mr. Life Manager.

### Short caption

> Get the sitting area, bathroom, sink and walking route ready, with the trash outside. The free company checklist takes you through the work in order. Allow about an hour, longer with a full sink. Open the profile link and start. If you're away, choose the exact time you'll start when you get home—with enough time before they arrive.

**Matching destination:** https://mrlifemanager.com/guests.html?from=ig-company-tonight

The actual Instagram profile link must open that task. For a placement with a direct link, replace “Open the profile link” with “Open the company checklist.” Do not print a fake tappable button into a video.

### Proposed landing opening

**Heading**

> Someone's coming over tonight. Your place isn't ready. You don't know where to start.

**Result and offer**

> Get the spaces they'll use ready: a clear sitting area, a clean bathroom, an empty sink, swept floors where they'll walk, and the trash outside. Follow the checklist in order. The other rooms can wait.

**Time and preparation**

> Allow about an hour before they arrive. A full sink or missing supplies can take longer. You'll need trash bags, a basket or box, dish supplies, suitable cleaner and a cloth, a toilet brush, a clean hand towel, and a broom or vacuum.

**Start button**

> I'm home. Start getting ready.

**Schedule button**

> I'm away. Choose when I'll get ready tonight.

**Schedule panel opening**

> What time will you get home and start getting ready? Leave about an hour before they arrive, more if the work needs it.

After selection: “Add your preparation time to your calendar. The event includes the link back to the checklist.”

**Scope and implementation boundary:** The full finish includes all six existing steps; clearing a seat or doing ten minutes is not the complete outcome. This is not a promise to prepare any home before any arrival time. Current calendar choices are generic and do not know the visitor's arrival time. Before using this landing copy, the tonight path needs appropriate time choices and a way to avoid suggesting tomorrow or next week as solutions to tonight's visit. Copy alone cannot enforce enough lead time. Do not claim that it does.

## 2. The place is a mess and you cannot find a starting point

**Intended moment:** The mess is already distressing. There is no need to invent a visitor or deadline. The reader wants to deal with it but cannot choose where to begin.

### Two hooks

**A — Recommend first**

> The dishes are piled up.
> There are clothes all over the floor.
> You don't know where to start.

**B — Embarrassment and stuckness**

> You don't want anyone to see your place like this.
> But you don't know where to start.

A makes the recognition concrete and grounds the help in the existing reset. B tests whether the private consequence is the stronger entrance. B describes an intended reader's feeling; it does not tell them their home is shameful or assume everyone with clutter feels embarrassed. Use A first because the visible situation needs less emotional inference. Do not combine both hooks into a longer catalogue of distress.

### Primary creative copy

> The dishes are piled up.
> There are clothes all over the floor.
> You don't know where to start.
>
> Get the kitchen and main room usable again. We'll tell you what to do first and what comes next.
>
> Open the profile link. Start getting your place back.

Small signature: Mr. Life Manager. Keep “underwater” as the existing route identifier, not an acquisition label the reader has to interpret.

### Short caption

> You don't have to work out the order. Follow the free reset checklist through the kitchen, one main room, your bed, dirty clothes and waiting mail. Then set tomorrow's first task. Allow about an hour and a half; a backlog can take longer. Open the profile link and start, or choose the exact day and time you'll do the reset at home.

**Matching destination:** https://mrlifemanager.com/underwater.html?from=ig-home-reset

Run with a profile link that opens the reset task. A company-only profile link cannot also deliver this promise. Use a separate campaign period or an explicitly named, verified task link; do not make the reader search a general menu after a specific promise.

### Proposed landing opening

**Heading**

> The dishes are piled up. There are clothes all over the floor. You don't know where to start.

**Result and offer**

> The order is worked out. Follow the checklist through the kitchen, loose clutter in one main room, your bed, dirty clothes and waiting mail. Finish with those jobs handled and tomorrow's first task scheduled. The rest of the house is outside this reset.

**Time and preparation**

> Allow about an hour and a half. A backlog of dishes or mail can take longer. You'll need dish supplies, suitable cleaner and a cloth, trash bags, baskets or boxes, a hamper, and your phone. Use the bedding already on the bed.

**Start button**

> I'm home. Start the reset.

**Schedule button**

> Choose the exact time I'll do the reset.

**Schedule panel opening**

> What day and time will you be home to do the reset? Set aside about an hour and a half, longer if the dishes or mail have piled up.

After selection: “Add your reset time to your calendar. The event includes the link back to the checklist.”

**Scope and execution boundary:** Gathering clutter does not sort it; containing clothes does not wash them; recording mail deadlines does not pay bills. Do not promise a spotless home, completed finances or relief from every feeling of overwhelm. The first existing step resets the whole kitchen and can be the longest job. That remains a genuine execution risk for a stuck reader; calling the order clear does not prove the work is easy or the task will be completed.

## What to cut and what to challenge next

Cut aspiration-led openings, demographic introductions, instruction previews, “six useful tips,” “save this,” and guaranteed short finishes. Keep the bounded result after recognition, the actual effort before starting, and the execution choice visible without requiring a long introduction.

Review these drafts first on one question: **Does the intended person recognize the trouble they are in now and want this help, or can they simply agree and keep scrolling?** Then check the promise against the actual work. A route match and legible design are necessary but insufficient. These are ranked copy hypotheses, not audience-validated winners.

## Adversarial review disposition

The social-action-critic reviewed this draft against the owner's clarified
standard. Both recommended hooks name a current unresolved problem and blocked
start. Applied two corrections: lead the reset offer with usable rooms rather
than the checklist container; distinguish the company result from the order of
the work. The tonight scheduler remains an implementation dependency, not a
copy-only fix. Ready for owner copy review, not published or audience-validated.
