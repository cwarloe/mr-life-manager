# Flier and workflow wording (working draft)

**Status:** Draft for Charles to mark up, 2026-10-31. Nothing here is on the
site yet. When this is final, the fliers and every screen from the scan to the
finish get built from it, word for word.

**Rules:** the act-now filter and the plain-speech rule in
[`content/README.md`](../../content/README.md). Every button and heading says
what the reader is doing. "It" only when the thing was just named.

**How it flows:** flier → scan one problem → that problem repeated on the page
→ the six steps in plain words → start now, or pick a day and time to do it
→ calendar reminder → back to the steps → finish.

## 2. The screens every track shares

Words in [brackets] come from the track (section 3). "Your task" and "my
task" are the same job written two ways: on screen we say "your," and in the
calendar and the message to a friend the reader says "my."

### Right after the scan

> *Small label:* You scanned this one:
> **[The flier line, word for word]**
>
> Here's what you'll do. It takes [time]:
> 1–6. [The six steps, in plain words]
>
> **When are you going to do these six things?**
> [ I'm home now. Let's start. ]
> [ I'm not home yet. Let me pick a day and time. ]
> Rather have it on paper? *Print the checklist.*

### Picking a day and time

> **When will you [your task]?**
> It takes [time], so pick a time you'll actually be home. "Later" isn't a
> time.
>
> [ Tonight, 7:00 p.m. ] [ Tomorrow evening, 7:00 p.m. ]
> [ Saturday morning, 10:00 a.m. ] [ Monday evening, 7:00 p.m. ]
> Or pick your own day and time: [date and time] [ Use this time ]

### After they pick

> **Saturday at 10:00 a.m.**
> That's when you'll [your task].
> Add this time to your calendar. When it comes, your phone will remind you,
> and the checklist will open at step one.
>
> [ Add to my calendar ] [ Add to Google Calendar ]
> [ Ask a friend to check on you ]
> *Small print:* We don't keep the time you picked. The reminder lives in your
> calendar.

**Calendar entry title:** [My task, as a command: "Wash the dishes and pick up
my place"]
**Calendar entry note:** Open this link to start the checklist at step one:
[link]

**Message to a friend:** "I've set aside Saturday at 10:00 a.m. to [my task].
Will you check on me after?"

### When the reminder brings them back

> **This is the time you set aside to [your task].**
> It takes [time]. Step one is ready.
> *(If they stopped partway: "You stopped at step 3, so you'll start there.")*
>
> [ Start step one ] [ I can't do it right now ]

If they've already moved the time twice:

> **You've moved this time twice already.**
> You can start now, or drop this checklist for now.
>
> [ Start now ] [ Drop it for now ]

If they drop it:

> **Okay. You've dropped this one for now.**
> You won't get any more reminders about it. If you want to [your task]
> another time, this page will still be here.

### While doing the steps

> Bottom link: Need to stop? Pick another time to finish.
>
> *After step 6:* **That's all six steps.**
> Look back over them and make sure each one is done.
> [ I'm done ]

### Finish page

> *Small label:* One thing finished
> **That counts.**
> [What they finished, from section 3.] You don't need to start a bigger
> project now. Enjoy it.

## 3. The five tracks

### Dishes and mess (`underwater`)

- **Your task:** wash the dishes and pick up your place
- **My task:** wash the dishes and pick up my place
- **Time:** about an hour and a half
- **The six steps:**
  1. Wash the dishes or load the dishwasher, and wipe the counters.
  2. Pick up the clutter in your main room and put it in a basket or box.
  3. Make your bed.
  4. Put your dirty clothes in the hamper.
  5. Open your mail and write down anything that's due.
  6. Decide what you'll do first tomorrow, and when.
- **Finish:** Your kitchen is clean, your main room is picked up, your bed is
  made, and you know what you're doing first tomorrow.

### Company (`guests`)

- **Your task:** get your place ready for company
- **My task:** get my place ready for company
- **Time:** about an hour
- **The six steps:**
  1. Tie up the kitchen trash and open a window.
  2. Empty the sink and wipe the counter.
  3. Put everything lying around the sitting area into a basket.
  4. Clean the bathroom your guests will use.
  5. Sweep or vacuum the floor where your guests will walk.
  6. Take the trash outside and turn on some lamps.
- **Finish:** The sitting area is clear, the bathroom is clean, the sink is
  empty, and the trash is outside.

### Table (`dinner`)

- **Your task:** clear off your kitchen table
- **My task:** clear off my kitchen table
- **Time:** about 10 minutes
- **The six steps:**
  1. Throw away the trash on the table.
  2. Take the dishes to the kitchen.
  3. Put away anything that has an obvious place.
  4. Put everything else in one basket.
  5. Wipe the table.
  6. Clear the chairs and set a place for everyone who's eating.
- **Finish:** Your table is clear, and you can sit down and eat. Enjoy your
  meal. The basket can wait until after dinner.

### First night (`first-night`)

- **Your task:** get your new place set up for tonight
- **My task:** get my new place set up for tonight
- **Time:** about an hour and a half
- **The six steps:**
  1. Take photos of every room before you unpack anything else.
  2. Make your bed before you get too tired.
  3. Set out your toothbrush, soap, towel and toilet paper.
  4. Find the breaker box, the water shutoff and the thermostat.
  5. Check the faucets, smoke alarms, locks and windows, and send your
     landlord one list of anything that's broken.
  6. Clear off one table or counter and one chair, and sit down to eat.
- **Finish:** You have photos of the place, your bed is made, your bathroom
  things are out, and you know where the shutoffs are.

### Spare room (`one-room`)

- **Your task:** clear out one part of your spare room
- **My task:** clear out one part of my spare room
- **Time:** about three hours
- **The six steps:**
  1. Decide what that part of the room is for, and figure out where the
     trash and donations will go today.
  2. Go through one shelf or box at a time.
  3. For anything you can't decide on, label it with who to ask or what to
     check, instead of setting it aside.
  4. Get the trash and donations out of the house today.
  5. Put back what you're keeping, with labels you can see.
  6. Clean the shelves and the floor, then start using the space.
- **Finish:** You cleared out one part of the room, and you can use it again.

## 4. Open questions

1. **"Living out of boxes" (students #4, career #4) vs. the first-night
   checklist.** The checklist is built for the first night: photos before you
   unpack, bed, shutoffs. Someone who's been there three weeks will find
   "before you unpack anything else" a little off. Either change the flier
   line to the first night ("You just got your keys, and tonight's your first
   night in the new place."), or we need a "still unpacking" checklist.
2. **Military #4, the spare room.** The checklist behind it was written for
   "a room you stopped using." It fits a room of moving boxes, but it takes
   three hours, which is a big ask from a flier. Keep it, or swap in the
   table?
3. **The checklist steps themselves** still have titles that break the
   plain-speech rule, worst on the spare-room checklist ("Give the difficult
   items a clear disposition," "Name the use and arrange the exit"). Section 3
   has plain versions; the full step text needs the same pass.
