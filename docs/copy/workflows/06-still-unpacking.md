## 3. The checklist

This is the full page they follow, step by step. They see one step at a time on their phone.

### Before you start

Pick one room, the one you use most, like the kitchen or the bedroom. You'll unpack that one room today and no other.

You'll need trash bags, scissors or a box cutter, a basket or a box for things you can't place yet, labels or paper and tape, a cloth, a broom or vacuum, and a way to get the empty boxes out of the house today. Keep the doorway clear. You don't have to sign up for anything.

### Step 1: Decide where things in this room will live, and where the empty boxes will go.

Look at the boxes in this room. Decide where the everyday things will live, like the plates in the cabinet next to the sink or the clothes in the dresser. Figure out where the empty boxes will go today: the recycling bin, a pickup, or your building's collection area, and when you'll take them there. If you need help lifting a heavy box or moving furniture, set that up first.

*Why:* You finish by getting the empty boxes out of the house, so work out how they leave before you start. If you don't have a plan for that, set one up before you open anything.

### Step 2: Unpack one box at a time.

Open one box. Put each thing where you decided it lives. Flatten the empty box and stack it by the door. Then open the next box. If you can't decide where something goes, put it in the basket for things you can't place. Don't leave it on the floor. Take a short break when you need one.

*Why:* Doing one box at a time keeps the mess from spreading. There's no speed you have to hit.

### Step 3: Decide what to do with the things you can't place.

Look at the basket. For each thing, ask whether you use it and where it would live. Don't throw away anything that belongs to someone else, or papers you're unsure about. Keep emergency supplies, seasonal things and things with sentimental value on purpose. If something needs someone else's answer, put it in a labeled box with that person's name and your question, give the box a spot, and set a time to ask.

*Why:* Choosing to keep something is a decision too. A labeled box in a known spot keeps the question in view without rushing you to throw something away.

### Step 4: Get the empty boxes and trash out of the house.

Take the flattened boxes to the recycling, or wherever you decided. Take the trash to where it goes. If you have things to give away, take them to the place you arranged, or hand them over when they're picked up. Loading the car doesn't finish this step: it's done when the boxes are gone.

*Why:* Finish the plan you made. Don't leave new stacks by the door or in your car. Driving or waiting for a pickup can make this take longer.

### Step 5: Label the boxes you're not opening today.

If there are boxes left in this room, write on the side that faces out what's inside and which room it goes in. Stack them against a wall, out of the walkway. Check that you can find your everyday things without opening several boxes.

*Why:* Labeled boxes can wait without being in your way. You don't have to unpack everything in one day.

### Step 6: Wipe the surfaces and the floor, then use the room.

Wipe the shelves, counters or other surfaces you unpacked onto. Sweep or vacuum the floor and throw out what you swept up. Put the broom and extra supplies away. Check that everything has a place, the path is clear, and the empty boxes are gone. Then use the room, and stop.

*Why:* The whole room is unpacked, and the floor is clear. The next room is a separate job.

### Keep it safe

Cut tape and cardboard with the blade pointed away from you. Get help with heavy boxes and furniture, and keep the exits clear.

## 5. Questions for reviewers

**Check every line against these:**

1. Does every verb have its object? No "pick a time" without what the time is for, and no "it" or "this" that points at nothing.
2. Would a person actually say this? Mark anything stiff, clipped or "clever."
3. Is it about right now? Cut anything about later, someday, or a point to agree with.
4. Is it true? The flier line has to match the checklist behind it, and the time has to be believable.
5. Is it kind? It names the situation, never the person.

**Questions about this workflow:**

- This checklist is new. It's built from the spare-room and first-night checklists, so the steps are borrowed. Does it fit someone who moved in weeks ago? What would they skip or add?
- About two hours for one room is a guess, not a measurement. Please time one room.
- The safety line about cutting boxes is new. It isn't in either source checklist. Keep it?
- Should the finish page offer the weekly routine email, like the other four checklists do?

**Times:** the time on the flier is an estimate, not a measurement. If you can, do this checklist once and time yourself, and tell us how far off it is.
