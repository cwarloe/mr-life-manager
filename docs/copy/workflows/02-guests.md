# Workflow 2: Company

**Status:** For review, 2026-11-01. Nothing here is on the live site yet. This document holds the whole path for this problem, from the line on the flier to the finish page, so you can read it start to finish.

**How to review:** read each line out loud, as if you were saying it to a friend. Mark anything that sounds stiff or confusing, or that you wouldn't say. "This sounds off" is enough; you don't need to know why. Please don't spend time on colors, fonts or layout. This round is wording only.

Rules the copy follows: every verb gets its object, write the way a person talks, and only say what helps someone act today. The full rules are in `content/README.md`.

**The path:** flier → scan → the problem repeated on the page → start now, or pick a day and time → calendar reminder → the six steps → finish.

## 3. The checklist

This is the full page they follow, step by step. They see one step at a time on their phone.

### Before you start

Get these first: trash bags, a basket or a box, what you need to wash dishes (soap and a sponge), a cleaner and a cloth, a toilet brush, a clean hand towel, and a broom or vacuum.

You're doing the sink, the room where you'll sit, one bathroom, and the floors in between. Nothing else. You don't have to sign up for anything.

### Step 1: Tie up the kitchen trash and open a window.

Tie the kitchen trash bag closed and put a new bag in the can. Set the full bag by the door, out of the doorway, where you'll pick it up on your last trip. Open a window if the weather lets you.

*Why:* At the end, you'll take the kitchen trash and the bathroom trash outside together.

### Step 2: Empty the sink and wipe the counter.

If you have a dishwasher with room, load it. If not, wash the dishes and leave them to dry on the rack. Empty the sink completely, then wipe the counter next to it. If a pan needs to soak, that adds waiting time.

*Why:* This step is done when the sink is empty. Washing a pile of dishes by hand takes more time. Hiding dirty dishes somewhere out of sight doesn't count.

### Step 3: Put everything lying around the sitting area into a basket.

In the room where you'll sit with your guests, pick up what's lying on the tables, the chairs and the floor, and put it in a basket or a box. Use another box if the first one fills up. Put the boxes where your guests won't walk past them. Decide where the things go tomorrow.

*Why:* You're making room for people to sit together. What's in the basket is a separate sorting job.

### Step 4: Clean the bathroom your guests will use.

Clean the toilet bowl and seat, the sink and the mirror with cleaners made for those surfaces. Wipe the floor around the toilet. Follow the directions on the label, including how long to wait. Put out a clean hand towel and tie up the bathroom trash.

*Why:* You only need to finish one bathroom that works. Don't use the cloth from the toilet on the sink or in the kitchen.

### Step 5: Sweep or vacuum where your guests will walk.

Do the entryway and the floor of the sitting room. Pick up the crumbs and dirt you can see, then put the broom or vacuum away.

*Why:* If you stick to the rooms your guests will use, this won't grow into cleaning the whole place.

### Step 6: Take the trash outside and turn on some lamps.

Take both tied-up trash bags out to the trash bin, chute or dumpster. Keep your path clear. When you come back in, turn on lamps or other soft lights. Use the lamps you already have.

*Why:* With the trash outside and the seats clear, you can welcome your guests and sit down.

### While you clean

Don't mix cleaning products. Read the label on each one for how to use it safely and how much fresh air you need. Don't leave the floor so wet that someone could slip.

### Link at the bottom of every step

> Need to stop? Pick another time to finish.

### After step 6

> **That's all six steps.**
>
> Look back over them and make sure each one is done.
>
> **[ I'm done ]**

### What just happened

You got the parts of your place your guests will use ready. You had clear edges: the sink, the sitting room, the bathroom, the walkway and the trash. The other rooms can wait.

### Finish

Your place is ready for company. The sink is empty, the surfaces are clear, the bathroom is clean, and the trash is outside. The floors where your guests will walk are swept. You can welcome them in and sit down together. Sorting the basket is a separate job for tomorrow.

### After

About that basket. The room is ready for your guests, but the basket still needs sorting. Pick a time tomorrow to sort it: put each thing where it belongs, and decide where to keep anything that doesn't have a place yet.

## 5. Questions for reviewers

**Check every line against these:**

1. Does every verb have its object? No "pick a time" without what the time is for, and no "it" or "this" that points at nothing.
2. Would a person actually say this? Mark anything stiff, clipped or "clever."
3. Is it about right now? Cut anything about later, someday, or a point to agree with.
4. Is it true? The flier line has to match the checklist behind it, and the time has to be believable.
5. Is it kind? It names the situation, never the person.

**Questions about this workflow:**

- The flier says "tonight." Someone scanning on a Tuesday for a party next Saturday may skip it. Should there be a version without "tonight"?
- Step 4 mentions the toilet cloth. Is that useful, or too much?

**Times:** the time on the flier is an estimate, not a measurement. If you can, do this checklist once and time yourself, and tell us how far off it is.
