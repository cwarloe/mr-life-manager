# Workflow 3: Table

**Status:** For review, 2026-11-01. Nothing here is on the live site yet. This document holds the whole path for this problem, from the line on the flier to the finish page, so you can read it start to finish.

**How to review:** read each line out loud, as if you were saying it to a friend. Mark anything that sounds stiff or confusing, or that you wouldn't say. "This sounds off" is enough; you don't need to know why. Please don't spend time on colors, fonts or layout. This round is wording only.

Rules the copy follows: every verb gets its object, write the way a person talks, and only say what helps someone act today. The full rules are in `content/README.md`.

## 3. The checklist

This is the full page they follow, step by step. They see one step at a time on their phone.

### Before you start

The table and the chairs you need: that's the whole job. Leave the rest of the house alone. You'll need a trash bag, a cloth, and enough baskets or boxes to hold the loose stuff. There's nothing to sign up for.

### Step 1: Throw away the trash on the table.

Put the wrappers, empty packages and other obvious trash from the table into a trash bag. If something makes you stop and think, leave it for step 4.

*Why:* Taking off the easy stuff first gives you room to deal with what's left.

### Step 2: Take the dishes to the kitchen.

Put the dishes in the dishwasher or next to the sink. Clean up the kitchen after dinner. Then come back to the table.

*Why:* Tonight's job is making a place to eat. Washing every dish would turn this into a different job.

### Step 3: Put away anything that obviously has a place.

Put away only the things you know where they go, and only if you can do it in one quick trip. If you'd have to decide where something goes, leave it for the basket. Come straight back.

*Why:* Putting away things that already have a place helps. Figuring out a new place for everything can wait until after dinner.

### Step 4: Put everything else in one basket.

Put the rest of the loose stuff in a basket or a box. If it fills up, use another one, and keep each one light enough to carry. Set them somewhere you can reach: not on the table, not on the chairs you'll use, and not in the walkway. Decide what to do with what's inside after dinner.

*Why:* The basket holds the work you haven't done yet. It gives you a place to eat now. It doesn't mean the sorting is done.

### Step 5: Wipe the table.

Wipe the part of the table you need for the meal. If something is staying on the table, pick it up, wipe underneath, and put it back.

*Why:* You're making a surface you can eat on. The rest of the room can wait.

### Step 6: Clear the chairs and set a place for everyone who's eating.

Take the stuff off the chairs you need and put it in the basket. Pull the chairs out and set a place for each person who's eating. Sit down. You're done.

*Why:* The goal is sitting down to eat together. A clear table with nowhere to sit would leave the job unfinished.

### Link at the bottom of every step

> Need to stop? Pick another time to finish.

### Finish

Eat first. The table is clear and everyone has a seat. That's enough for this meal. You're done.

### After

The basket is still waiting. You made room for dinner. Later, the things in the basket need places to live, so your table doesn't turn into storage again. For now, go eat. Sorting the basket is a separate job for later.

## 5. Questions for reviewers

**Check every line against these:**

1. Does every verb have its object? No "pick a time" without what the time is for, and no "it" or "this" that points at nothing.
2. Would a person actually say this? Mark anything stiff, clipped or "clever."
3. Is it about right now? Cut anything about later, someday, or a point to agree with.
4. Is it true? The flier line has to match the checklist behind it, and the time has to be believable.
5. Is it kind? It names the situation, never the person.

**Questions about this workflow:**

- The page tells the reader to schedule a time "before your next meal at home." Does a 10-minute job even need scheduling, or is "start now" the real action?
- Student box: is eating on your bed the most common version of this problem? Career box: is eating standing at the counter?

**Times:** the time on the flier is an estimate, not a measurement. If you can, do this checklist once and time yourself, and tell us how far off it is.
