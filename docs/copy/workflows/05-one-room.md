**Status:** For review, 2026-11-01. Nothing here is on the live site yet. This document holds the whole path for this problem, from the line on the flier to the finish page, so you can read it start to finish.

**How to review:** read each line out loud, as if you were saying it to a friend. Mark anything that sounds stiff or confusing, or that you wouldn't say. "This sounds off" is enough; you don't need to know why. Please don't spend time on colors, fonts or layout. This round is wording only.

Rules the copy follows: every verb gets its object, write the way a person talks, and only say what helps someone act today. The full rules are in `content/README.md`.

## 3. The checklist

This is the full page they follow, step by step. They see one step at a time on their phone.

### Before you start

Pick one part of the room and mark its edges before you move anything. It could be a workbench and the floor in front of it, a set of shelves and the floor in front of them, or a reading corner with a chair, a table and a clear path. Pick something you can handle by yourself. A wall of old papers or heavy equipment can wait for another day or for someone to help.

You'll need bags, boxes you already have, labels or paper and tape, a cloth, a broom or vacuum, and a way to get the stuff you're getting rid of out of the house today. Keep the doorway clear. You don't have to sign up for anything.

### Step 1: Decide what this part of the room is for, and where the trash and donations will go.

Write down what this part of the room will be used for, like "my tools." Mark its edges on the floor. Figure out where the trash and the donations will go today: where you'll take them, when that place is open, and how you'll get them there or have them picked up. Get your boxes, bags, labels, cloth and broom or vacuum ready. If you need help lifting something, set that up first.

*Why:* You finish by getting stuff out of the house, so work out how it leaves before you start. If you don't have a plan for that, schedule it before you empty anything.

### Step 2: Go through one shelf or box at a time.

Start at one end and work across. Make four piles: keep here, belongs somewhere else, donate, and trash. Put anything you're not sure belongs to you, important papers, and valuables in one place for the next step. Sort on clear floor space and keep a path open. Take a short break when you need one.

*Why:* Doing one shelf or box at a time keeps the mess small. There's no speed you have to hit.

### Step 3: Decide what to do with the hard ones.

Ask yourself whether each thing fits the use you picked, and where it belongs. Don't throw away someone else's things or papers you're unsure about. Keep emergency supplies, seasonal things and things with sentimental value on purpose. If something needs someone else's answer, put it in a labeled box with that person's name and your question, give the box a spot, and set a time to ask.

*Why:* Choosing to keep something is a decision too. A labeled box in a known spot keeps the question in view without rushing you to throw something away.

### Step 4: Get the trash and donations out of the house.

Put things that live somewhere else in the house where they belong. Take the trash to where it goes. Take the donations to the place you arranged, or hand them over when they're picked up. Loading the car doesn't finish this step: the donation isn't done until someone has it.

*Why:* Finish the plan you made. Don't leave new piles by the door or in your car. Driving or waiting for a pickup can make this take longer.

### Step 5: Put what you're keeping back, with labels you can read.

Group similar things together. Put what you use most within easy reach, and heavy things low. Use boxes you already have, and put the label on the side that faces out. Give your labeled box of questions its own spot. Check that you can find your everyday things without opening several boxes.

*Why:* Clear bins are nice, but readable labels on boxes you already have work fine. You don't need to go shopping to finish.

### Step 6: Clean the shelves and the floor, then start using the space.

Wipe the work surface or seat, then sweep or vacuum the floor in your part of the room. Throw out what you swept up. Put the broom and extra supplies away. Check that the shelf labels are on, the path is clear, and the donations are gone. Then use the space for what you picked, and stop.

*Why:* The whole part you picked is organized, and the floor is clear. The next part of the room is a separate job.

### Keep it safe

Paint, solvents, batteries and electronics need to be thrown away the right way. Set them aside for a place that accepts them. Get help with anything heavy or unsteady, and keep the exits clear.

### What just happened

You cleared one whole part of the room. You made your own decisions, got rid of what was leaving, and left the space ready to use. The labeled box of questions has a next step attached.

### Finish

That part of the room is ready to use again. The things you kept have labeled places, the floor is swept, and the donations have gone to the people who will take them. Anything waiting on someone else's answer has a spot and a next step written down. The next part of the room is a separate job. Enjoy using the one you finished.

### After

Give the space a job. A clear use and labeled spots make it easier to put things back. When something new comes in, decide where it goes before you put it on the shelf.

## 5. Questions for reviewers

**Check every line against these:**

1. Does every verb have its object? No "pick a time" without what the time is for, and no "it" or "this" that points at nothing.
2. Would a person actually say this? Mark anything stiff, clipped or "clever."
3. Is it about right now? Cut anything about later, someday, or a point to agree with.
4. Is it true? The flier line has to match the checklist behind it, and the time has to be believable.
5. Is it kind? It names the situation, never the person.

**Questions about this workflow:**

- About three hours is the longest flier ask. Is it too much, or is it the kind of job people book for a Saturday morning?
- Is "spare room" the right name for the military box, or is there a better word for a room that's full of moving boxes?

**Times:** the time on the flier is an estimate, not a measurement. If you can, do this checklist once and time yourself, and tell us how far off it is.
