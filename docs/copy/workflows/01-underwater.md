# Workflow 1: Dishes and mess

**Status:** For review, 2026-11-01. Nothing here is on the live site yet. This document holds the whole path for this problem, from the line on the flier to the finish page, so you can read it start to finish.

**How to review:** read each line out loud, as if you were saying it to a friend. Mark anything that sounds stiff or confusing, or that you wouldn't say. "This sounds off" is enough; you don't need to know why. Please don't spend time on colors, fonts or layout. This round is wording only.

Rules the copy follows: every verb gets its object, write the way a person talks, and only say what helps someone act today. The full rules are in `content/README.md`.

**The path:** flier → scan → the problem repeated on the page → start now, or pick a day and time → calendar reminder → the six steps → finish.

## 3. The checklist

This is the full page they follow, step by step. They see one step at a time on their phone.

### Before you start

Get these first: what you need to wash dishes (soap and a sponge), a cleaner and a cloth, trash bags, a basket or a box, a laundry hamper, and your phone. You'll make your bed with the sheets that are already on it. Washing the sheets is a job for another day.

You're doing your kitchen, one main room, your bed, your dirty clothes and your mail. That's all. Don't clean the cabinets, the windows or any other rooms. You don't have to sign up for anything.

### Step 1: Wash the dishes and wipe down the kitchen.

Wash the dishes, or load them into the dishwasher. Wipe the counters and any sticky spots from cooking. Wait until the stove is cool before you wipe it. Take the trash out and put in a fresh bag. Read the label on your cleaner, and give the dishes time to soak if they need it.

*Why:* You're done with this step when the dishes are clean or in the dishwasher and you can use the counters. If the dishes have piled up, this step can take longer than the other five put together.

### Step 2: Pick up the clutter in your main room.

Put the things covering your chairs, tables and walkways into a basket or a box. Use a second one if the first fills up. Set the baskets where you can reach them, out of the way of the chairs and walkways. If something's too heavy to move by yourself, leave it where it is and ask for help later.

*Why:* The room becomes usable without you deciding where every item goes. Sorting what's in the baskets is a separate job.

### Step 3: Make your bed.

Pull the sheet and the blanket or comforter up, and put the pillows back. When you finish, you'll have a bed you can get into tonight.

*Why:* Use the bedding that's already on the bed. If it needs replacing, finding clean sheets will add time.

### Step 4: Put your dirty clothes in the hamper.

Pick up the dirty clothes from the floor and put them in your hamper or a basket. Keep them apart from your clean clothes. Wait to wash and dry them until you can stay home and finish the whole load.

*Why:* Tonight you're only getting the dirty clothes into one place. You don't have to wait for a washing machine.

### Step 5: Open your mail and write down anything that's due.

Open every envelope. Put junk mail in the recycling or the trash. Put letters and bills in one pile. For each bill or letter with a due date, write the date in your calendar and keep the paper. If something is overdue, due now, or confusing, mark it so you deal with it soon. Don't guess what it means. Paying the bills is a separate job.

*Why:* You're finding out what you owe and when, not solving every problem tonight. A pile of old mail or a confusing notice may need more time.

### Step 6: Decide what you'll do first tomorrow, and when.

Write down one specific job and the time you'll do it, like "8:30 a.m.: empty the dishwasher." Set an alarm, or leave the note where you'll see it. Look at the mail you marked first, so you don't miss anything urgent.

*Why:* Knowing exactly where to start tomorrow means you don't have to figure it out then.

### Don't do this

Don't try to clean the whole house tonight. That's how this turns into three hours and a feeling that you failed again. The six steps are the whole job. When you finish them, you're done.

### What just happened

You made tomorrow easier to start. You finished six clear steps, and you wrote down what needs doing next. Doing this again each week can help. Life will still get busy.

### Finish

Tonight is done, and tomorrow has a starting point. The dishes are done, the clutter is in baskets, your bed is made, and your dirty clothes are in the hamper. Your mail is open, and you've written down what's due and what's urgent. You've picked tomorrow's first job and the time you'll do it. Sorting the baskets and paying the bills are separate jobs. They haven't gone away, but now you know what to do next.

### After

Your place didn't get messy because something's wrong with you. "Clean the whole house" is too big a job when you're already tired. You needed small steps that have an end. A clean kitchen and one clear room is a routine you can keep. Do it on an ordinary Tuesday, when nobody's coming over, and it keeps working. You don't have to do everything now. Do the small job while it's still small.

## 5. Questions for reviewers

**Check every line against these:**

1. Does every verb have its object? No "pick a time" without what the time is for, and no "it" or "this" that points at nothing.
2. Would a person actually say this? Mark anything stiff, clipped or "clever."
3. Is it about right now? Cut anything about later, someday, or a point to agree with.
4. Is it true? The flier line has to match the checklist behind it, and the time has to be believable.
5. Is it kind? It names the situation, never the person.

**Questions about this workflow:**

- Step 5 (mail): is "write down anything that's due" clear? Is it too much for a tired person?
- The "After" paragraph says the mess isn't a character problem. Does that read as kind, or as talking down?

**Times:** the time on the flier is an estimate, not a measurement. If you can, do this checklist once and time yourself, and tell us how far off it is.
