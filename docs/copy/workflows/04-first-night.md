**Status:** For review, 2026-11-01. Nothing here is on the live site yet. This document holds the whole path for this problem, from the line on the flier to the finish page, so you can read it start to finish.

**How to review:** read each line out loud, as if you were saying it to a friend. Mark anything that sounds stiff or confusing, or that you wouldn't say. "This sounds off" is enough; you don't need to know why. Please don't spend time on colors, fonts or layout. This round is wording only.

Rules the copy follows: every verb gets its object, write the way a person talks, and only say what helps someone act today. The full rules are in `content/README.md`.

**The path:** flier → scan → the problem repeated on the page → start now, or pick a day and time → calendar reminder → the six steps → finish.

## 3. The checklist

This is the full page they follow, step by step. They see one step at a time on their phone.

### Before you start

Before you open more boxes, find your phone and key, your bedding, toilet paper, soap, a towel, your toothbrush and toothpaste. You need a bed or mattress that's put together, and a place to sit. If something is missing, getting it is part of tonight's work. You don't have to sign up for anything.

### Step 1: Take photos of every room before you unpack more.

Use your phone to take photos of the walls, the floors, the inside of the oven and the fridge, and close-ups of any damage that's already there. If people are carrying in boxes, take a video as you walk through the place and keep the walls clear so they show up. Then take the close-ups.

*Why:* These photos can help show what condition the place was in when you moved in. They don't guarantee you'll win a dispute about your deposit.

### Step 2: Make your bed before you get too tired.

Find your sheets, put them on, and set out your blanket and pillows. You're only making the bed you'll sleep in tonight.

*Why:* Having your bedding ready saves you a late-night search. If you still have to put the bed together or get a mattress, that takes extra time.

### Step 3: Set out your bathroom essentials.

Put the toilet paper on the holder. Put the soap by the sink. Hang up one towel. Keep your toothbrush and toothpaste within reach. Leave the rest of the bathroom box for tomorrow.

*Why:* That makes the bathroom usable tonight without unpacking every toiletry.

### Step 4: Find the breaker box, the water shutoff and the thermostat.

Find each one and take a photo of it. Look for the shutoff valves under the sinks and behind the toilet too. If something is locked, shared, or you can't tell where it is, write that down and ask your landlord or building manager where it is and who can get to it. Don't force anything open, and don't touch equipment you don't know how to use.

*Why:* Knowing what you can get to will help later. Asking your landlord where something is stays an open item until you get an answer.

### Step 5: Check the faucets, smoke alarms, locks and windows, and send your landlord one list of anything that's broken.

Run the hot water at each faucet. Press the test button on each smoke alarm. Try your key in the outside door and the lock. Open and close the windows. Don't climb on furniture to reach anything; if you can't reach something safely, write that down instead. List anything that's broken and any check you couldn't do safely. Send the list and your photos to your landlord or building manager with this message: "I moved in today and found ___. Please arrange a repair or access, and let me know what happens next."

*Why:* A dated message records what you found when you moved in. Reporting a problem doesn't fix it. Keep any safety problem on your list until it's fixed.

### Step 6: Clear off one table or counter and one chair, and sit down to eat.

Clear one table or counter and one chair. Put out something to eat and sit down. Use food you already have, or order a meal. Cooking or delivery can add time.

*Why:* You have a place to rest tonight and a clear surface for tomorrow.

### Don't skip this

Don't skip a safety check tonight. If a smoke alarm doesn't respond, is missing, or you can't test it safely, call your landlord or building manager right away and ask for help. A smoke alarm you've reported broken is still not a working smoke alarm.

### What just happened

You made your first night work. You got ready to sleep, you put your essentials where you can reach them, and you recorded the condition of the place and anything wrong while you still remember it.

### Finish

You have what you need for tonight. Your bed and bathroom are ready, and you have somewhere to sit and eat. Your photos are saved, and you've written down what you checked. Keep any problem that's still unfixed, and any question you're waiting on, on your follow-up list. Don't mark it fixed until it is. You can unpack the rest tomorrow.

### After

Before you unpack the rest: tomorrow, decide where each thing goes as you unpack it. Put everyday items where you use them. You can decide one box at a time.

## 5. Questions for reviewers

**Check every line against these:**

1. Does every verb have its object? No "pick a time" without what the time is for, and no "it" or "this" that points at nothing.
2. Would a person actually say this? Mark anything stiff, clipped or "clever."
3. Is it about right now? Cut anything about later, someday, or a point to agree with.
4. Is it true? The flier line has to match the checklist behind it, and the time has to be believable.
5. Is it kind? It names the situation, never the person.

**Questions about this workflow:**

- Is "household goods haven't arrived yet" (military box) how service members actually say it?
- Step 5 is the longest step in all six workflows. Should the landlord message be its own step?
- The student and career boxes for this workflow only appear in move-in season. Which weeks should that be?

**Times:** the time on the flier is an estimate, not a measurement. If you can, do this checklist once and time yourself, and tell us how far off it is.
