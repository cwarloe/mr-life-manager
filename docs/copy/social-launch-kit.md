# Social launch kit: from a post to a finished task

**Status:** Draft for review alongside the [workflow copy](README.md). This adds social entrances to existing tasks. It does not approve the pending workflow rewrites or publish posts.
**Owner direction:** clarity takes priority over complete sentences. Every element should move someone toward taking action and experiencing the result. Static images and lists are the default; recording is optional.

## The conversion

Recognize a problem → open its task → start now, or choose a date and time → save the calendar event → return and start → finish the task → experience the result.

The completed household result is the conversion. Scheduling is a useful intermediate commitment. A click, a time choice, or a calendar-link click is not proof that the task happened.

A social post sells the value of completing one task. Give enough detail to make the next step clear and credible, then send the reader into the existing workflow. Teaching every step in the feed is optional and should serve completion, rather than create another thing to consume.

This is an operating choice, not a claim about optimal posting times. Begin after copy review and account checks; no fixed launch date is assumed.

## Graphic production brief

One reusable portrait template, with a 1080 × 1350 working canvas. Check cropping and readability in the actual account before publishing. Match the task page's current branding; use ordinary household images only when they clarify the situation or result. Typographic cards are sufficient.

Hierarchy: recognizable problem → concrete result → one next step → small Mr. Life Manager signature. Use short lists or fragments when they are clearer. Sentence completeness is not a performance goal.

Keep the instruction to open the checklist readable on the image. Put explanatory details in the caption. Repeat essential instructions in text or alt text. A separate video series, carousel calendar, hashtag research project, or recurring content subscription is not needed for this launch.

## Attribution and evaluation

Use lowercase source labels with hyphens, at most 40 characters, matching the existing source validation.

| Placement | Source label |
|---|---|
| Facebook C1–C4 | fb-company-c1 through fb-company-c4 |
| Instagram company profile link | ig-company-profile |
| A partner's company link | partner-company-01, using a nonpersonal placement identifier |
| A physical company flier | qr-place-guests, replacing place with an agreed location label |

Social posts use tappable links where available, rather than asking a phone reader to scan an on-screen QR code. Printed material continues to use its existing QR scheme.

Use the existing audience tracker and monthly scorecard. Record publication date, placement, creative ID, source tag, production minutes, available visits and downstream events. Instagram profile-link visits are campaign-level evidence: they do not identify which feed post caused the visit.

Read the existing events as stages:
- start-now: immediate start;
- time-picked: chosen date/time;
- calendar-added: calendar-link click, not a verified saved event;
- came-back: a return URL was opened;
- started-from-reminder: the reader starts after returning;
- finished: the reader acknowledges the full finish line, not independently verified physical completion.

Count event totals as event totals unless unique sessions are available. Repeated clicks are not distinct people. Neither calendar clicks nor higher appointment-path completion establish a causal effect of scheduling, because readers choose their path.

After the initial four posts, examine where people stop. Reach without task visits calls for checking the message and link. Calendar clicks without returns call for checking calendar save and return behavior. Starts without finishes call for checking the task experience. Small counts are directional and do not establish a winning topic.

The decision criterion is whether the entrance helps people finish a household task, relative to the work it takes to maintain the channel. Likes and saves can help diagnose reach; they are not the conversion.
