# Checklist text in plain speech (proposed)

**Status:** Proposed rewrite of the five live checklists, 2026-11-01, for
review. Nothing here is on the site yet. The live text is in
`products/landing/{underwater,guests,dinner,first-night,one-room}.html`.

**What changed:** wording only. Every step does what it did before, in the
same order, with the same safety lines. The changes are the ones Charles asked
for: the way people actually talk, every verb gets its object, no stiff phrases
("give the difficult items a clear disposition"), and no "it" or "this" that
points at nothing. Where I made a small change beyond wording, it's marked
**[changed]** so a reviewer can check it.

**Rules:** [`content/README.md`](../../content/README.md) (act-now filter and
plain-speech rule). Companion draft for the fliers and the screens around the
checklist: [`workflow-wording.md`](workflow-wording.md).

**How to review one:** read each step out loud. If a sentence makes you stop
and ask "what's *it*?" or "do what with what?", mark it. Check that the safety
lines still say what they should.

**Default heading** is what the page says when someone arrives without a
flier. When they scan a flier, the heading is the flier's line, word for word.

## 1. Dishes and mess (`underwater`)

**Default heading:** Your dishes are piling up and your place is a mess.
**Time:** about an hour and a half. It takes longer if the dishes or the mail
have piled up.

**Before you start:**
Get these first: what you need to wash dishes (soap and a sponge), a cleaner
and a cloth, trash bags, a basket or a box, a laundry hamper, and your phone.
You'll make your bed with the sheets that are already on it. Washing the sheets
is a job for another day.

You're doing your kitchen, one main room, your bed, your dirty clothes and your
mail. That's all. Don't clean the cabinets, the windows or any other rooms.
There's nothing to sign up for.

**1. Wash the dishes and wipe down the kitchen.**
Wash the dishes, or load them into the dishwasher. Wipe the counters and any
sticky spots from cooking. Wait until the stove is cool before you wipe it.
Take the trash out and put in a fresh bag. Read the label on your cleaner, and
give the dishes time to soak if they need it.
*Why:* You're done with this step when the dishes are clean or in the dishwasher
and you can use the counters. If the dishes have piled up, this step can take
longer than the other five put together.

**2. Pick up the clutter in your main room.**
Put the things covering your chairs, tables and walkways into a basket or a
box. Use a second one if the first fills up. Set the baskets where you can
reach them, out of the way of the chairs and walkways. If something's too heavy
to move by yourself, leave it where it is and ask for help later.
*Why:* The room becomes usable without you deciding where every item goes.
Sorting what's in the baskets is a separate job.

**3. Make your bed.**
Pull the sheet and the blanket or comforter up, and put the pillows back. When
you finish, you'll have a bed you can get into tonight.
*Why:* Use the bedding that's already on the bed. If it needs replacing, finding
clean sheets will add time.

**4. Put your dirty clothes in the hamper.**
Pick up the dirty clothes from the floor and put them in your hamper or a
basket. Keep them apart from your clean clothes. Wait to wash and dry them
until you can stay home and finish the whole load.
*Why:* Tonight you're only getting the dirty clothes into one place. You don't
have to wait for a washing machine.

**5. Open your mail and write down anything that's due.**
Open every envelope. Put junk mail in the recycling or the trash. Put letters
and bills in one pile. For each bill or letter with a due date, write the date
in your calendar and keep the paper. If something is overdue, due now, or
confusing, mark it so you deal with it soon. Don't guess what it means. Paying
the bills is a separate job.
*Why:* You're finding out what you owe and when, not solving every problem
tonight. A pile of old mail or a confusing notice may need more time.

**6. Decide what you'll do first tomorrow, and when.**
Write down one specific job and the time you'll do it, like "8:30 a.m.: empty
the dishwasher." Set an alarm, or leave the note where you'll see it. Look at
the mail you marked first, so you don't miss anything urgent.
*Why:* Knowing exactly where to start tomorrow means you don't have to figure
it out then.

**Don't do this:** Don't try to clean the whole house tonight. That's how this
turns into three hours and a feeling that you failed again. The six steps are
the whole job. When you finish them, you're done.

**What just happened:** You made tomorrow easier to start. You finished six
clear steps, and you wrote down what needs doing next. Doing this again each
week can help. Life will still get busy.

**Finish:** Tonight is done, and tomorrow has a starting point. The dishes are
done, the clutter is in baskets, your bed is made, and your dirty clothes are
in the hamper. Your mail is open, and you've written down what's due and what's
urgent. You've picked tomorrow's first job and the time you'll do it. Sorting
the baskets and paying the bills are separate jobs. They haven't gone away, but
now you know what to do next.

**After:** Your place didn't get messy because something's wrong with you.
"Clean the whole house" is too big a job when you're already tired. You needed
small steps that have an end. A clean kitchen and one clear room is a routine
you can keep. Do it on an ordinary Tuesday, when nobody's coming over, and it
keeps working. You don't have to do everything now. Do the small job while it's
still small.

## 2. Company (`guests`)

**Default heading:** People are coming over and your place isn't ready.
**Time:** about an hour. It goes faster if the dishwasher is empty, and takes
longer if the sink is full.

**Before you start:**
Get these first: trash bags, a basket or a box, what you need to wash dishes
(soap and a sponge), a cleaner and a cloth, a toilet brush, a clean hand towel,
and a broom or vacuum.

You're doing the sink, the room where you'll sit, one bathroom, and the floors
in between. Nothing else. There's nothing to sign up for.

**1. Tie up the kitchen trash and open a window.**
Tie the kitchen trash bag closed and put a new bag in the can. Set the full bag
by the door, out of the doorway, where you'll pick it up on your last trip.
Open a window if the weather lets you.
*Why:* At the end, you'll take the kitchen trash and the bathroom trash outside
together.

**2. Empty the sink and wipe the counter.**
If you have a dishwasher with room, load it. If not, wash the dishes and leave
them to dry on the rack. Empty the sink completely, then wipe the counter next
to it. If a pan needs to soak, that adds waiting time.
*Why:* This step is done when the sink is empty. Washing a pile of dishes by
hand takes more time. Hiding dirty dishes somewhere out of sight doesn't count.

**3. Put everything lying around the sitting area into a basket.**
In the room where you'll sit with your guests, pick up what's lying on the
tables, the chairs and the floor, and put it in a basket or a box. Use another
box if the first one fills up. Put the boxes where your guests won't walk past
them. Decide where the things go tomorrow.
*Why:* You're making room for people to sit together. What's in the basket is a
separate sorting job.

**4. Clean the bathroom your guests will use.**
Clean the toilet bowl and seat, the sink and the mirror with cleaners made for
those surfaces. Wipe the floor around the toilet. Follow the directions on the
label, including how long to wait. Put out a clean hand towel and tie up the
bathroom trash.
*Why:* You only need to finish one bathroom that works. Don't use the cloth
from the toilet on the sink or in the kitchen.

**5. Sweep or vacuum where your guests will walk.**
Do the entryway and the floor of the sitting room. Pick up the crumbs and dirt
you can see, then put the broom or vacuum away.
*Why:* If you stick to the rooms your guests will use, this won't grow into
cleaning the whole place.

**6. Take the trash outside and turn on some lamps.**
Take both tied-up trash bags out to the trash bin, chute or dumpster. Keep your
path clear. When you come back in, turn on lamps or other soft lights. Use the
lamps you already have.
*Why:* With the trash outside and the seats clear, you can welcome your guests
and sit down.

**While you clean:** Don't mix cleaning products. Read the label on each one
for how to use it safely and how much fresh air you need. Don't leave the floor
so wet that someone could slip.

**What just happened:** You got the parts of your place your guests will use
ready. You had clear edges: the sink, the sitting room, the bathroom, the
walkway and the trash. The other rooms can wait.

**Finish:** Your place is ready for company. The sink is empty, the surfaces
are clear, the bathroom is clean, and the trash is outside. The floors where
your guests will walk are swept. You can welcome them in and sit down together.
Sorting the basket is a separate job for tomorrow.

**After:** About that basket. The room is ready for your guests, but the basket
still needs sorting. Pick a time tomorrow to sort it: put each thing where it
belongs, and decide where to keep anything that doesn't have a place yet.

## 3. Table (`dinner`)

**Default heading:** Your kitchen table is covered with stuff, and you need a
place to eat.
**Time:** about ten minutes.

**Before you start:**
The table and the chairs you need: that's the whole job. Leave the rest of the
house alone. You'll need a trash bag, a cloth, and enough baskets or boxes to
hold the loose stuff. There's nothing to sign up for.

**1. Throw away the trash on the table.**
Put the wrappers, empty packages and other obvious trash from the table into a
trash bag. If something makes you stop and think, leave it for step 4.
*Why:* Taking off the easy stuff first gives you room to deal with what's left.

**2. Take the dishes to the kitchen.**
Put the dishes in the dishwasher or next to the sink. Clean up the kitchen
after dinner. Then come back to the table.
*Why:* Tonight's job is making a place to eat. Washing every dish would turn
this into a different job.

**3. Put away anything that obviously has a place.**
Put away only the things you know where they go, and only if you can do it in
one quick trip. If you'd have to decide where something goes, leave it for the
basket. Come straight back.
*Why:* Putting away things that already have a place helps. Figuring out a new
place for everything can wait until after dinner.

**4. Put everything else in one basket.**
Put the rest of the loose stuff in a basket or a box. If it fills up, use
another one, and keep each one light enough to carry. Set them somewhere you
can reach: not on the table, not on the chairs you'll use, and not in the
walkway. Decide what to do with what's inside after dinner.
*Why:* The basket holds the work you haven't done yet. It gives you a place to
eat now. It doesn't mean the sorting is done.

**5. Wipe the table.**
Wipe the part of the table you need for the meal. If something is staying on
the table, pick it up, wipe underneath, and put it back.
*Why:* You're making a surface you can eat on. The rest of the room can wait.

**6. Clear the chairs and set a place for everyone who's eating.**
Take the stuff off the chairs you need and put it in the basket. Pull the
chairs out and set a place for each person who's eating. Sit down. You're done.
*Why:* The goal is sitting down to eat together. A clear table with nowhere to
sit would leave the job unfinished.

**Finish:** Eat first. The table is clear and everyone has a seat. That's
enough for this meal. You're done.

**After:** The basket is still waiting. You made room for dinner. Later, the
things in the basket need places to live, so your table doesn't turn into
storage again. For now, go eat. Sorting the basket is a separate job for later.

## 4. First night (`first-night`)

**Default heading:** Tonight is your first night in your new place.
**Time:** about an hour and a half. It takes longer if something's missing and
you have to go get it. Haven't moved in yet? Pack tonight's essentials in one
box you can reach.

**Before you start:**
Before you open more boxes, find your phone and key, your bedding, toilet
paper, soap, a towel, your toothbrush and toothpaste. You need a bed or mattress
that's put together, and a place to sit. If something is missing, getting it is
part of tonight's work. There's nothing to sign up for.

**1. Take photos of every room before you unpack more.**
Use your phone to take photos of the walls, the floors, the inside of the oven
and the fridge, and close-ups of any damage that's already there. If people are
carrying in boxes, take a video as you walk through the place and keep the
walls clear so they show up. Then take the close-ups.
*Why:* These photos can help show what condition the place was in when you
moved in. They don't guarantee you'll win a dispute about your deposit.

**2. Make your bed before you get too tired.**
Find your sheets, put them on, and set out your blanket and pillows. You're
only making the bed you'll sleep in tonight.
*Why:* Having your bedding ready saves you a late-night search. If you still
have to put the bed together or get a mattress, that takes extra time.

**3. Set out your bathroom essentials.**
Put the toilet paper on the holder. Put the soap by the sink. Hang up one towel.
Keep your toothbrush and toothpaste within reach. Leave the rest of the
bathroom box for tomorrow.
*Why:* That makes the bathroom usable tonight without unpacking every toiletry.

**4. Find the breaker box, the water shutoff and the thermostat.**
Find each one and take a photo of it. Look for the shutoff valves under the
sinks and behind the toilet too. If something is locked, shared, or you can't
tell where it is, write that down and ask your landlord or building manager
where it is and who can get to it. Don't force anything open, and don't touch
equipment you don't know how to use.
*Why:* Knowing what you can get to will help later. Asking your landlord where
something is stays an open item until you get an answer.

**5. Check the faucets, smoke alarms, locks and windows, and send your landlord
one list of anything that's broken.**
Run the hot water at each faucet. Press the test button on each smoke alarm.
Try your key in the outside door and the lock. Open and close the windows. Don't
climb on furniture to reach anything; if you can't reach something safely,
write that down instead. List anything that's broken and any check you couldn't
do safely. Send the list and your photos to your landlord or building manager
with this message: "I moved in today and found ___. Please arrange a repair or
access, and let me know what happens next." **[changed: "Stay on the floor"
replaced by the same safety rule in plain words.]**
*Why:* A dated message records what you found when you moved in. Reporting a
problem doesn't fix it. Keep any safety problem on your list until it's fixed.

**6. Clear off one table or counter and one chair, and sit down to eat.**
Clear one table or counter and one chair. Put out something to eat and sit
down. Use food you already have, or order a meal. Cooking or delivery can add
time.
*Why:* You have a place to rest tonight and a clear surface for tomorrow.

**Don't skip this:** Don't skip a safety check tonight. If a smoke alarm
doesn't respond, is missing, or you can't test it safely, call your landlord or
building manager right away and ask for help. A smoke alarm you've reported
broken is still not a working smoke alarm.

**What just happened:** You made your first night work. You got ready to sleep,
you put your essentials where you can reach them, and you recorded the
condition of the place and anything wrong while you still remember it.

**Finish:** You have what you need for tonight. Your bed and bathroom are
ready, and you have somewhere to sit and eat. Your photos are saved, and
you've written down what you checked. Keep any problem that's still unfixed, and
any question you're waiting on, on your follow-up list. Don't mark it fixed
until it is. You can unpack the rest tomorrow.

**After:** Before you unpack the rest: tomorrow, decide where each thing goes as
you unpack it. Put everyday items where you use them. You can decide one box at
a time.

## 5. Spare room (`one-room`)

**Default heading:** There's a room in your place that you can't use anymore.
**Time:** about three hours. That includes getting the giveaway pile out the
door. No car? Arrange a pickup first.

**Before you start:**
Pick one part of the room and mark its edges before you move anything. It could
be a workbench and the floor in front of it, a set of shelves and the floor in
front of them, or a reading corner with a chair, a table and a clear path. Pick
something you can handle by yourself. A wall of old papers or heavy equipment
can wait for another day or for someone to help.

You'll need bags, boxes you already have, labels or paper and tape, a cloth, a
broom or vacuum, and a way to get the stuff you're getting rid of out of the
house today. Keep the doorway clear. There's nothing to sign up for.

**1. Decide what this part of the room is for, and where the trash and
donations will go.**
Write down what this part of the room will be used for, like "my tools." Mark
its edges on the floor. Figure out where the trash and the donations will go
today: where you'll take them, when that place is open, and how you'll get them
there or have them picked up. Get your boxes, bags, labels, cloth and broom or
vacuum ready. If you need help lifting something, set that up first.
*Why:* You finish by getting stuff out of the house, so work out how it leaves
before you start. If you don't have a plan for that, schedule it before you
empty anything.

**2. Go through one shelf or box at a time.**
Start at one end and work across. Make four piles: keep here, belongs somewhere
else, donate, and trash. Put anything you're not sure belongs to you, important
papers, and valuables in one place for the next step. Sort on clear floor space
and keep a path open. Take a short break when you need one.
*Why:* Doing one shelf or box at a time keeps the mess small. There's no speed
you have to hit.

**3. Decide what to do with the hard ones.**
Ask yourself whether each thing fits the use you picked, and where it belongs.
Don't throw away someone else's things or papers you're unsure about. Keep
emergency supplies, seasonal things and things with sentimental value on
purpose. If something needs someone else's answer, put it in a labeled box with
that person's name and your question, give the box a spot, and set a time to
ask.
*Why:* Choosing to keep something is a decision too. A labeled box in a known
spot keeps the question in view without rushing you to throw something away.

**4. Get the trash and donations out of the house.**
Put things that live somewhere else in the house where they belong. Take the
trash to where it goes. Take the donations to the place you arranged, or hand
them over when they're picked up. Loading the car doesn't finish this step: the
donation isn't done until someone has it.
*Why:* Finish the plan you made. Don't leave new piles by the door or in your
car. Driving or waiting for a pickup can make this take longer.

**5. Put what you're keeping back, with labels you can read.**
Group similar things together. Put what you use most within easy reach, and
heavy things low. Use boxes you already have, and put the label on the side
that faces out. Give your labeled box of questions its own spot. Check that you
can find your everyday things without opening several boxes.
*Why:* Clear bins are nice, but readable labels on boxes you already have work
fine. You don't need to go shopping to finish.

**6. Clean the shelves and the floor, then start using the space.**
Wipe the work surface or seat, then sweep or vacuum the floor in your part of
the room. Throw out what you swept up. Put the broom and extra supplies away.
Check that the shelf labels are on, the path is clear, and the donations are
gone. Then use the space for what you picked, and stop.
*Why:* The whole part you picked is organized, and the floor is clear. The next
part of the room is a separate job.

**Keep it safe:** Paint, solvents, batteries and electronics need to be thrown
away the right way. Set them aside for a place that accepts them. Get help with
anything heavy or unsteady, and keep the exits clear.

**If you have to stop partway** (the note the page shows when you pick another
time): If something interrupts you, bag the trash, label the boxes you've
sorted, and clear the path. Write down the next thing left to do before you
reschedule. The job isn't finished until the donations are gone and the floor
is clean.

**What just happened:** You cleared one whole part of the room. You made your
own decisions, got rid of what was leaving, and left the space ready to use.
The labeled box of questions has a next step attached.

**Finish:** That part of the room is ready to use again. The things you kept
have labeled places, the floor is swept, and the donations have gone to the
people who will take them. Anything waiting on someone else's answer has a spot
and a next step written down. The next part of the room is a separate job.
Enjoy using the one you finished.

**After:** Give the space a job. A clear use and labeled spots make it easier
to put things back. When something new comes in, decide where it goes before you
put it on the shelf.
