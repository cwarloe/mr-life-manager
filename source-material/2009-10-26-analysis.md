## The two rules are unchanged across fourteen years

2009, closing the preparation section:

> Memorize the model, **Do it now, Everything has a place.**

2023, opening the cleanup notes:

> Memorize and apply the golden housekeeping rules...
> **1. Everything has it's place. 2. Do it now.**

Same two rules, same verb ("memorize"), same position — stated up front as the
thing you carry into the work. Fourteen years apart, written for two different
projects, for the same man.

This matters more than it looks. Until now the risk was that "Dave's system" was
really Charles's reconstruction of a few remembered phrases. It isn't. Dave has
been teaching the same irreducible pair, in the same words, for at least
seventeen years. [frameworks/do-it-now.md](../frameworks/do-it-now.md) and
[frameworks/everything-has-a-place.md](../frameworks/everything-has-a-place.md)
are documented, not reconstructed.

## "Glazed eyes" is a named failure mode, and it is ours

The phrase was the reason this search started. It is in the 2009 document, under
a heading Dave wrote himself:

> **d. Common excuses at this stage**
> 1. This will take too long
> 2. **There is too much to do, glazed eyes start appearing**
> 3. I need to take a nap

He is describing the overwhelm response *and filing it under excuses* — not
sympathy, not diagnosis. He anticipates it, names it, and moves past it. He
repeats the phrase to Charles ten years later, unprompted: *"I have seen those
glazed eyes on so many before."*

[Principle 11](../docs/vision/principles.md) and
[ADR-014](../planning/decisions.md) — completion is what converts, and handing
someone more than one thing to act on is a failure — were reasoned out last week
from first principles. Dave named the same failure in 2009 and built his document
to prevent it. **The overriding business principle is inherited, not invented.**

## The genuinely new pattern: remove the excuse before you start

This is the most useful thing in the document and it is not in the repo anywhere.

Section I is not preparation. It is **excuse removal**, and Dave says so:

> Make sure that dump is open the day you decide to organize. **We don't want any
> excuses that the dump isn't open.**

Every item in the preliminary section closes off a specific future escape hatch —
the donation site is shut, there's no truck, there's nowhere to put the trash,
the shelves aren't bought. Then the excuse catalog in section II.d lists the ones
he couldn't close off, so you recognize them when your own mouth produces them.

That is a design pattern for our products, not just his: **a prep list's job is
to eliminate the reasons a person will stop, not to gather supplies.** Written up
as [frameworks/remove-the-excuse.md](../frameworks/remove-the-excuse.md).

## Reset once, then maintain — the sequence the repo was missing

Surface tension: 2023 says *"preventative cleaning rather than crisis cleaning."*
2009 is a one-day crisis blitz of a garage.

It resolves cleanly, and the resolution is an architecture:

- The garage document is the **one-time reset**. You cannot maintain a space that
  has never been brought to zero.
- The condo notes are the **maintenance regime** that keeps it there.

Dave bounds the reset deliberately — *"Your plan is to have this done in one day
or less, so start early in the morning"* — because an unbounded reset becomes the
glazed-eyes problem. And he ends it with a terminus you can feel: *"3. Go get a
beer."*

Our guides currently only teach maintenance. A person whose place has never been
reset can't start there. That's a product gap.

## "Everything has a place," made testable

2009 gives the rule a pass/fail test, which the abstraction had lost:

> There are to be no items on the shelves that **you can't tell their contents
> from a distance.**

Plus the ordering rule: least-used at the top, descending to most-used at easy
reach; like with like; located where that area of the space is used. And the
keep/discard rule: *"Have I used this item in the last year, if not get rid of it."*

These are checkable from across the room. Worth pulling into the framework —
right now it explains a concept where Dave gave a standard.

## Flags — handle honestly, don't launder

**The closing quote is not Dave's.** He labels it "Quote" and does not claim it:

> We always overestimate what we can do in a year and under estimate what we can
> do in 10.

This aphorism circulates widely and is commonly attributed to Bill Gates, with
the attribution generally considered unreliable; related phrasings trace to
Roy Amara. **Do not print it with an attribution we cannot verify, and never
attribute it to Dave.** If it's used, it's used unattributed or with the
uncertainty stated. (Repo research standards, applied to our own founder.)

**The voice is blunter than the brand.** *"Get your ass moving."* *"Go get a
beer."* *"you have to be brutal on yourself."* This is the second source in a row
that reads harder than our published guides. That is now a decision that needs
making rather than a one-off — see the register question in the
[2023 analysis](2023-07-17-analysis.md).

**"Do I really want this, and am I willing to work on it till goal reached?"**
appears as question (a) in *both* 2009 and 2023. Dave opens every document with a
commitment question before any instruction. None of our guides do this. That's a
consistent, deliberate pattern, not a quirk.

## What to do with this

1. **Write the reset guide.** The repo teaches maintenance to people who have
   never reset. Dave's garage document is a template for the missing product.
2. **Add the commitment question** to the guides. It is in both sources, first,
   every time.
3. **Give "everything has a place" its test back** — the from-a-distance
   standard, the least-used-at-top ordering, the one-year rule.
4. **Settle the register**, now that three documents agree the source is blunter
   than the derived work.
5. **Retrieve the four unpulled `.docx` guides.** Dave wrote them for Charles
   directly, and two of them are about cleaning products — the exact subject of
   an unresolved contradiction. See [INVENTORY.md](INVENTORY.md) for the list,
   the thread IDs, and how to pull them.
6. **Recording Dave is still the critical path.** Three documents now cover
   cleaning and organizing. Nothing covers money, food, paperwork, maintenance,
   or transitions — and the 2019 thread shows he has hard-won things to say about
   money in particular.
