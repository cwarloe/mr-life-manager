## What this refines in the written method

**Sorting is a decision structure, not a fixed pile count.** The garage's three
piles describe destinations. This visit's four groups also distinguish how
often retained possessions are used. Both help assign a next destination.

**Preparation and reset can overlap.** The garage plan aims for one day. Here,
Dave explicitly plans a return with supplies (10:42–11:49), and the recording
ends with temporary placement pending containers (30:01–30:25). A bounded win
and a concrete next step fit this evidence better than requiring the whole
reset in one session.

**The recording supplies reasons.** Clear storage saves searching; stable
homes save thinking; visible quantities make existing stock knowable. Those
explanations make the principles easier to apply elsewhere. This strengthens
the evidence for visibility and placement without establishing methods for
unrecorded domains such as money or relationships.

**Specific scheduling is visible in practice.** The TV conversation is a useful
analogy for [ADR-021](../planning/decisions.md#adr-021--when-the-reader-cant-act-yet-ask-for-a-when).
It is not a test of the website's reminder flow or proof that a particular
scheduling feature improves completion.

## What to reuse

The [intervention session template](../content/intervention-template.md) turns
the observed moves into a reusable facilitator guide: agree on a goal,
understand use and constraints, open working space, sort, assign visible homes,
and write specific next actions. It includes blank session fields and a partial

## Limits and next evidence

One historical session supports a method worth trying. It does not establish
commercial demand, a price, a typical session duration, a repeatable service
outcome, or long-term maintenance. The roughly 30-minute recording length is
not a promise of how long an intervention takes.
