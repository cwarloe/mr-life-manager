# Analysis: Dave Webster — "Vu Intervention" (January 15, 2014)

**Status:** Analysis of primary video source [2014-01-15-dave-webster-vu-intervention.md](2014-01-15-dave-webster-vu-intervention.md).
**Date:** 2026-10-26.

This transcript captures Dave Webster live in action during a real 30-minute intervention in Vu's apartment/room, filmed by Charles Warloe. It is the only known recording of Dave teaching and executing his method in real time with a third party.

---

## 1. Core Principles Demonstrated Live

### A. The Principle of Visibility ("Nothing can be hidden")
* **Clear containers over cardboard:** *"One of the reasons that Charles brought these clear boxes is that you can see what's in it. You don't have to open it up, you can look on the outside and see what's inside there."*
* **The "Black Hole":** *"The black holes where things collect and you find and never find in a million years."* If items are in opaque bags or deep piles, they become functionally invisible.
* **Silverware tray rule:** Rummaging through a jumbled drawer burns willpower. When items are separated and visible, *"all you have to do is reach down and pick it up. It's right there visually. One of the rules is nothing can be hidden if we can help it."*

### B. The 4 Sorting Categories
Dave establishes four explicit categories before sorting:
1. **Throw away:** Junk nobody can use.
2. **Give away:** Things someone else can use (e.g., Goodwill, family).
3. **Use all the time:** High-frequency everyday items.
4. **Use some of the time:** Occasional or seasonal items.

### C. Frequency-of-Use Hierarchy & "In Your Sleep"
* Daily items stay closest at hand (lower/middle shelves, front of cupboard).
* Infrequent items go far away (top shelves, storage closet).
* **Cognitive conservation:** *"Each thing has a place. So every time you come in here, you put it back in the same way... You could do it in your sleep and go 'oh, flour is on the right hand side'—you don't even have to look... that saves mental thinking and it saves time."*

### D. The Immediate Specific Commitment ("The Due List")
When Vu mentions giving his old TV to his dad, Dave refuses to let it remain a vague intention:
> **Dave:** "When are you going to give it to your dad?"
> **Vu:** "I'm gonna call him tonight."
> **Dave:** "What time tonight? You gonna call him?"
> **Vu:** "Before I go to bed."
> **Dave:** "Write that down on your list. You're gonna call dad before you go to bed tonight. This is your due list. This is due list number two."

*(Note: This is the exact behavioral science principle of implementation intentions that underpins [ADR-021](../planning/decisions.md#adr-021--when-the-reader-cant-act-yet-ask-for-a-when) and the "Not right now" calendar scheduling flow.)*

### E. Start Easy to Build Momentum
* *"Start with the easy things first. That kind of encourages you guys. Get the floor cleaned off, the shelves cleaned off. If you like you're making progress, then little by little around, get the things that are going to be a little rougher."*

### F. Clear the Working Space First
* Clear the floor and pathways first so nobody is tripping or stepping over clutter.
* Stage trash bags right by the door so they are ready to leave the room.
* Clutter and disorder actively drain energy: *"When things are all just shoveled all over the place, it just drives me down... so I kind of clean things up so that I have work room to begin the big and nasty stuff."*

### G. Dignity, Non-Judgment, and Excitement
* Dave never shames Vu for the mess. He reframes the entire situation with confidence: *"Well, this is my specialty, as you know. Oh, yeah. This is gonna be no problem, dude. You're gonna be all set up."*
* Dave gets genuinely excited by progress: *"What are you excited about, Dave? We have lots more empty space so we can put things in!"*
