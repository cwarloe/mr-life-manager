# Recording Dave — Session Guide

**Take this to the session. Don't send it to him beforehand.**
Reading the questions in advance gives him time to decide what's worth saying,
and what he decides isn't worth saying is exactly what we're after.

---

## Before you start

- **Record audio.** Phone is fine. Video if you're doing a walkthrough.
- **Tell him what it's for**, honestly: *"People genuinely weren't taught this.
  I want to write down what you taught me so other people can have it."*
- **Warn him you'll ask obvious things**, and that obvious is the point.
- **30–45 minutes.** Short and repeated beats one long session — the good
  material surfaces after the obvious material runs out.
- **Don't correct him** toward anything in `frameworks/`. If he contradicts a
  framework, the framework is what's wrong.

---

## Opening — get him talking, low stakes

1. How did you learn to run a house? Did somebody teach you, or did you work it out?
2. What did your parents make you do that you didn't understand until later?
3. When you moved into your first place, what went wrong?

*These are warm-up. Don't push for insight yet — just get him comfortable talking.*

---

## The highest-yield question set — other people's failures

**This is the route around his embarrassment.** He won't state what he knows.
He'll happily describe what he's watched go wrong.

4. What do people get wrong most often?
5. What have you had to tell someone more than once?
6. What makes you wince when you see it in someone's house?
7. What's the thing people don't realize is going to cost them money?
8. When you walk into someone's kitchen, what do you notice?
9. What do people do that makes their own life harder without knowing it?
10. Who's someone you watched struggle with this, and what were they missing?

*Follow every answer with:* **"Why does that happen?"** and **"What should they
do instead?"**

---

## Cadence — he'll have real answers and won't think they're interesting

11. How often do you do the sheets? Towels? Floors?
12. What do you do every single day without thinking about it?
13. What's on your list once a year that most people never do at all?
14. What's something people do way more often than they need to?
15. What's something people should do far more often than they do?

---

## Method — the why underneath the rule

16. Why that way and not the other way?
17. What's the order you do things in, and does the order matter?
18. What's the shortcut that actually works, versus the one that seems like it should?
19. What do you do when you've let it all go and it's overwhelming? Where do you start?
20. What's not worth doing at all, that people think they're supposed to do?

---

## If you can walk around together

**Do this if at all possible.** Narrating while doing surfaces detail that
sitting and recalling never will.

21. Walk me through a room. What are you looking at?
22. Do a real task and narrate it. *What are you doing right now, and why that way?*
23. Where do you keep things, and why there?
24. Show me something you fixed or set up. Why like that?

---

## The one that teaches him to see it

25. **"You just said something like it was nothing. I had no idea. Say more about that."**

Use this every time it happens. Over the course of several sessions this teaches
him to recognize what's non-obvious — which is the most valuable thing that can
come out of this whole project. He can't judge what's worth saying; you can.

---

## Closing

26. What would you want someone to know in their first month on their own?
27. If you could only tell them three things, what would they be?
28. Is there anything you assumed I already knew that I clearly didn't?

---

## Right after the session — while it's fresh

- [ ] **Note what surprised you.** That list is the content. It's also the thing
      only you can produce.
- [ ] **Note his exact phrasing** where he said something well. Original voice
      beats rewritten voice nearly every time.
- [ ] Transcribe verbatim into
      [`source-material/`](../original-charles-cleanup-notes.md) — typos,
      tangents, unfinished thoughts and all. Derive separately, later.
- [ ] Note what he said that contradicts something already in
      [`frameworks/`](../../frameworks/). Those are the most interesting finds.

## What to run next time

Pick a domain and go deep rather than broad: a session on the kitchen, a session
on maintenance, a session on money, a session on having people over. Same
question shapes, one subject.
