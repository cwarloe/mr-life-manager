# What the Weekly Schedule Settles

**Derived from** [2009-11-04-dave-webster-weekly-schedule.md](2009-11-04-dave-webster-weekly-schedule.md),
retrieved 2026-09-19. Kept separate from the source per the rules in this folder.

This is the most operationally useful document recovered so far, and it settles
a question the repo had been guessing at.

---

## The reset-before-maintenance sequence is Dave's, stated outright

The [2009 garage analysis](2009-10-26-analysis.md) proposed — as a hypothesis —
that the garage blitz and the maintenance regime weren't contradictory but
sequential. Dave says it himself, in section I.B, before any schedule:

> **Before implementing this schedule you must do an initial cleaning to the
> standard that you desire.** This includes having everything in your house
> having a place, your garage organized and things placed where you want, things
> thrown out that you don't intend to keep, floors, shelves cleaned, car cleaned
> out. [...] The goal is to get you house, garage, and car the way you want to
> keep it all the time **within a weeks time.**

So: **reset first, bounded to one week, then the schedule holds it.** Not a
hypothesis anymore. It also bounds the reset — a week, not "whenever you get to
it" — which is the same anti-overwhelm move as the garage doc's "one day or less."

**This is the repo's biggest product gap.** All six guides teach maintenance.
Dave says maintenance is meaningless until the reset is done, and he puts the
reset first every time. We have no reset guide.

## The preface is boilerplate — and that changes how we read the 2023 notes

Section I.A of this 2009 document and the preface of the
[2023 kitchen notes](2023-07-17-dave-webster-cleanup-notes.md) are **word for
word identical** — the golden housekeeping rules, the 50% claim, the bleeding
heart and glazed eyes, preventative vs. crisis, God as a planner, chaos to order.

Two things follow, and the second is a correction:

1. **It's a fixed opening he attaches to every guide.** Not a one-off framing.
   Dave opens each document with the same non-negotiable block before any
   instruction. That's a deliberate instructional choice and a usable pattern.
2. **The 2023 email was titled "All that I could find."** Dave was digging up his
   own old files. So the 2023 document is partly a *re-issue*, not independent
   fresh writing — which means "the rules are unchanged across fourteen years"
   needs to be stated more carefully than the
   [garage analysis](2009-10-26-analysis.md) first put it. What's genuinely
   demonstrated is that the October 2009 garage doc, the November 2009 schedule,
   and whatever Dave still had in 2023 all carry the same two rules. That's
   still a stable teaching. It is not three independent drafts.

## The car is a whole domain we've missed

**"Check car for anything left in it"** appears on **all seven days.** Plus
Saturday: clean out car and trunk, wash, vacuum, dust, windows. And the reset in
I.B includes "car cleaned out." And section C.5:

> The car is to be cleaned out every night when coming home. Only items that
> belong there, or are going to be used the next day can be left.

Dave treats the car as a room of the house. Nothing in
[`frameworks/life-domains.md`](../frameworks/life-domains.md) or any guide does.
For the first-independent-adult audience, a car is often the second-most-lived-in
space they own. This is a real omission, and it's cheap to fix.

## "Free horizontal spaces," fourteen years early

The [2023 analysis](2023-07-17-analysis.md) flagged "free horizontal spaces" as
new material worth adopting. Here it is in 2009, more concrete:

> **The dining room table, coffee tables are not for storage.**

And the sink rule, with its reasoning attached — which is the part that makes it
stick:

> There should never be anything in the sink unless you are washing the dishes in
> the sink after using. **It takes the same amount of energy to put it in the
> dishwasher as it does the sink.** Same applies to counters.

That second sentence is the whole argument against a dish pile, in fourteen
words. We should use it.

## Flexible order, fixed deadline — ADR-014 in his words

> NOTE: Feel free to switch some of the chores to another day if that works out
> better for your schedule, **BUT everything on the list has to be done by Sunday
> evening**, or Monday evening if you have weekend warrior.

He gives away the sequence and keeps the completion. That's exactly
[ADR-014](../planning/decisions.md) — completion is the thing that counts — and
it's a better-designed version than ours, because the flexibility he grants is
the flexibility people actually need, while the part that would let them off the
hook stays nailed down.

Also note the "weekend warrior" allowance. He anticipates a specific life
pattern and adjusts the deadline for it rather than pretending everyone's week
looks the same.

## The refrain, used deliberately

Every single day ends with the same line:

> Note: Do not leave anything laying around the house that you do not intend to
> use, everything has it's place.

Seven times. Identical. That is not padding — it's spaced repetition of the one
rule that carries the whole system, placed where the reader will hit it daily.
Our guides state a principle once, at the top, and never repeat it. Worth
stealing.

## The punch list: he inspected the actual place

Section C isn't general advice. It's a survey of Charles's specific condo:

> 2. Light bulb needs replaced in bathroom.
> 8. Get screen for your bathroom window.
> 3. Need to find a place in your office for mail, refer back to earlier papers on mail.

The email subject said it: *"will check your place to finish."* Dave walked the
place and wrote the findings into the guide.

**This matters for the business model.** The thing Dave actually did was a
*service* — a personalized site survey feeding a customized plan. Our products
are the generic layer under that. Worth being clear-eyed: the personalization is
where the value was, and a downloadable checklist doesn't carry it. That's either
a limitation to accept or a higher tier to design, but it shouldn't be invisible.
(Note "refer back to earlier papers on mail" — **there are more documents**, and
one of them is about handling mail.)

## The cadence itself is gentler than it looks

Stripped of the daily constants (kitchen surfaces, pick-up, car), each day has
exactly one real job:

| Day | The one task |
|---|---|
| Mon | Floors, dust, blinds, mirrors + shelves |
| Tue | Laundry, start to finish, put away + bar |
| Wed | Bedroom + main bathroom; check windows/deck/stairs |
| Thu | Kitchen deep: appliances, cupboards, fridge + closets |
| Fri | — nothing extra |
| Sat | Second bathroom, garage sweep, car wash |
| Sun | — nothing extra |

Two rest days built in. Worth checking our
["How Often Should I...?"](../products/checklists/) cadence against this: Dave's
real schedule may be more forgiving than what we published, and if so, his wins.
