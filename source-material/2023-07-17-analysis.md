## Contradictions — and these matter

### 1. "Buy as big a supply as you can" vs. "buy the smallest size"

**Dave:** *"Remember that it costs money to drive to the store, as well as your
time is worth so much money. So get as big a supply as you can to save trips."*

**The live guide:** *"Buy the smallest size of anything new to you. A bulk jug of
something you end up hating is worse than no cleaner."*

These are in direct opposition, and **both are right about different people.**
Dave is optimizing for someone who knows what they use — trips cost more than
storage. The guide is optimizing for a first-timer who doesn't yet know what
they'll use. **The audience decides**, which is exactly the kind of thing the
frameworks should say and currently don't. This needs resolving in the guide.

### 2. Dave lists ~25 products. The guide says nine.

Pine Sol, Mr. Clean, 409, Windex, Comet, Pledge, Soft Scrub, Swiffer, glass-top
cleaner, granite cleaner, shower spray, blue toilet tablets, Febreze.

The guide's whole thesis is *"you need about nine, not forty"* and *"most
specialty cleaners are the same few ingredients with a different label."* **Dave
is on the other side of that argument** — he lists a specific product per surface.

### 3. Dave's list is heavily scented. The commerce rules say avoid scented.

Pine Sol **lemon scent** is specified. **Febreze air freshener** is its own
category. The new [commerce rules](../docs/business/commerce-rules.md) say prefer
unscented and avoid products whose main feature is fragrance.

**Per this folder's own rule — if the framework and the source disagree, the
framework is what needs examining.** These three are not typos. They are a real
difference between what Dave taught and what the derived product now says, and
they should be resolved deliberately rather than quietly kept as they are.

**A possible resolution worth testing with him:** Dave was writing a bespoke
list for one man's specific condo — granite counters, glass-top stove, wood
furniture. The guide is written for someone with none of those yet. That would
make both correct in context, and it would make the guide's job *naming the
context*, which it currently doesn't do.

But that is a hypothesis. **Ask him.**

## New material not in the repo at all

- **"Free horizontal spaces"** as a goal. Concrete, memorable, and a better
  statement of the surfaces problem than anything currently written.
- **"I want a hotel atmosphere."** A specific standard someone can picture.
- **"Draw a diagram of what your cupboards will look like"** — design before you
  start, which nothing in the repo currently says.
- **"Do I really want this, and am I willing to work on it till the goal is
  reached?"** — a commitment question asked *before* any instruction.
- **"By following these rules you will save 50% of your time."** A specific
  claim. Unverified, and worth asking where it came from before it's ever
  printed.
- **Shelf paper, spice racks** — buy the organizers *before* starting, which
  directly contradicts the guide's "container trap" warning in
  [everything-has-a-place.md](../frameworks/everything-has-a-place.md). Another
  one to raise with him.

## What to do with this

1. **Resolve the three contradictions** — put them to Dave directly. They are
   perfect session questions because he will have strong opinions.
2. **Add "free horizontal spaces" and "hotel atmosphere"** to the frameworks.
   They are his, they are vivid, and they are better than the current phrasing.
3. **Decide on the register.** The source is blunter than the brand. That is a
   voice decision, not an accident to be smoothed over.
4. **Retrieve `Do it now.jpg`** — an attachment on the original email, not yet
   pulled.
5. **Recording Dave is still the critical path.** This is one document about one
   condo. It proves the frameworks are real; it does not cover food, money,
   laundry, maintenance, paperwork, or transitions.
