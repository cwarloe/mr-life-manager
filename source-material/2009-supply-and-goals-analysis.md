# The Supply List and the House Goals

**Derived from** [2009-11-10-dave-webster-materials-list.md](2009-11-10-dave-webster-materials-list.md)
and [2010-01-02-dave-webster-house-goals.md](2010-01-02-dave-webster-house-goals.md),
retrieved 2026-09-19. Kept separate from the sources per the rules in this folder.

With these two, **all four of Dave's 2009–2010 guides are recovered.** One
resolves the repo's longest-standing contradiction. The other turns out not to
be about cleaning at all.

---

## The "25 products" contradiction mostly dissolves — it was a counting error

The [2023 analysis](2023-07-17-analysis.md) recorded this as a live contradiction:
Dave listed *"about twenty-five products, one per surface,"* against the guide's
nine. The 2009 list makes clear what happened.

Dave's list is organized **by room**, and the same product is re-listed in every
room it's used in. Windex appears four times. Mop three. Pine Sol, Mr. Clean,
Swiffer, vacuum, broom, sponges and Pledge appear twice each.

Counted as line items: **28.** Counted as distinct things to buy: **16** — and
six of those are tools and consumables (vacuum and bags, broom, mop, Swiffer and
refills, sponges, toilet brush), not cleaning agents.

**Distinct cleaning agents: ten.** Glass-top cleaner, granite cleaner, Pine Sol,
Mr. Clean spray (or 409), Windex, Soft Scrub, Pledge, Comet, toilet bowl tablet,
Febreze.

Our guide says nine. **Dave and the guide were never really in conflict on
volume.** The contradiction was an artifact of reading a per-room shopping list
as a product count. That should be corrected rather than carried into a
conversation with him as though he'd been caught out.

### What does remain a genuine disagreement

Two things, and they're narrower and more interesting than the original framing:

1. **He buys per-surface.** Glass-top cleaner, granite cleaner, Pledge, Comet,
   Soft Scrub — five products whose job our guide argues is mostly done by one
   all-purpose spray plus a non-scratch pad. That's a real difference of opinion
   about five items, not sixteen. Worth noting that his five are the ones where
   the surface can actually be *damaged* by the wrong product, which is a more
   defensible position than "one cleaner per surface."
2. **He uses scent deliberately.** Pine Sol in lemon and Febreze, in both the
   2009 and 2023 lists. Fourteen years apart, unchanged. That's a considered
   preference, not an oversight, and it connects straight to the "hotel
   atmosphere" goal below — scent is part of the effect he's going for.
   [Commerce rule 3](../docs/business/commerce-rules.md) already settled that we
   won't be dogmatic here; this is the evidence for why.

**Also worth noting:** no vinegar and no baking soda anywhere in either list. Our
guide leans on both. That's ours, not his.

### The list barely moved in fourteen years

2023 adds exactly three things to 2009: cleaning gloves, shower spray, and paper
towels — plus the annotation "Pledge for wood work **(not for dining table)**."
That's the entire delta. The supply list is settled doctrine for him.

## "House goals" is a fourth domain, and we have nothing in it

This document is not about cleaning. It's about **making a place work for
people** — light, artwork, focal points, paint, furniture, what a room is *for*.

> I want to have a comfortable place to live and show hospitality that is neat, clean and enjoyable to be in.
> I want to have free horizontal spaces
> I want to show movies, entertain 8-10 people and if more use the cabana.
> **I want a hotel atmosphere**

Three things follow.

**Hospitality is goal number one, and it's quantified.** Not "have people over"
— *eight to ten people, and movies.* [Principle 7](../docs/vision/principles.md)
("order exists to make room for people") is confirmed with a capacity number
attached. The cleaning system exists to serve this. It is not the point.

**"Free horizontal spaces" is a 2010 goal, not a 2023 one.** The
[2023 analysis](2023-07-17-analysis.md) flagged it as promising new material.
It's eleven years older than that, and it's stated as a *goal* rather than a
rule — which is the better framing. You don't clear surfaces because clutter is
bad; you clear them because you want free horizontal space.

**He tells Charles to hire professionals.** *"I will find 1-3 proficient home
decorators."* Dave does not claim expertise he doesn't have, and he builds the
handoff into the plan — with measurements taken in advance so the decorator's
time isn't wasted. That's the
[remove-the-excuse](../frameworks/remove-the-excuse.md) pattern applied to
someone else's job.

## Correction: the completion rule is from 2010, not 2023

The [2023 analysis](2023-07-17-analysis.md) states that
[ADR-014](../planning/decisions.md) "is in the source, from 2023," citing:

> Set date when you are going to start this due list and have it finished by.
> **Have someone check you to see if you started and finished on time.**

That line closes *this* document, on 1 January 2010. The 2023 notes carry it
forward. Sixteen years, not three. The 2023 analysis has been corrected.

This is the third instance of the same pattern: the 2023 email was titled "All
that I could find," and its goals, its preface, and its completion rule all trace
to 2009–2010 documents. **2023 is an archive, not a draft.**

## The part we should be honest about

This document is written for a man with a condo, a cabana, a fireplace, a bay
window, a pub table worth $150, and the budget to hire decorators. The
[beachhead](../planning/decisions.md) is first-time independent adults with
almost none of that.

That doesn't invalidate anything — the two rules, the reset-then-maintain
sequence, the excuse removal and the completion check are all
condition-independent. But it does mean **the application has always been
translated, never lifted.** Dave's documented practice is a homeowner's practice.
Every guide we publish is an adaptation, and we should say so internally rather
than implying we're transcribing him.

It also sharpens what the [2023 analysis](2023-07-17-analysis.md) guessed at: the
granite-and-glass-top supply list isn't Dave being maximalist, it's Dave writing
for a specific kitchen. Both he and the guide are right for their own reader. The
guide's real job is **naming its reader**, which it still doesn't do.

## Small things worth keeping

- **"What is the focal point of each room?"** — one question that does more
  organizing work than a page of instruction.
- **He priced the furniture.** *"Sell pub table $150, chairs $87 each."* The
  level of concreteness is the whole method.
- **"Reduce size of table when no company is around."** Furniture as a variable,
  not a fixture.
- **"Have mail sent to office"** — partially answers the weekly schedule's
  reference to "earlier papers on mail," though a dedicated mail document may
  still exist.
- **"Decide where you are going to put current clutter and move it."** Decide,
  then move. [Do It Now](../frameworks/do-it-now.md) in one line.

## What to do with this

1. **Correct the record on the supply list** before raising it with Dave. Ask
   about the five surface-specific products and about scent — not about a
   product count that was our miscount.
2. **Write the reset guide** (still the top gap, from the
   [weekly schedule](2009-11-04-analysis.md)).
3. **Open the hospitality domain.** "What is this room for?", focal points, free
   horizontal spaces, and a capacity target are a product, and they're closer to
   the actual promise than another cleaning checklist.
4. **Name the reader in every guide.** The contradiction between Dave's list and
   ours was a context mismatch, and it will recur in every domain until the
   guides say who they're for.
5. **Record that 2023 is an archive**, so no future analysis treats it as
   independent evidence again.
